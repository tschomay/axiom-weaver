# Grounding and Detail — Toll

**Score: 3/5** — Each scene's place is vivid and the scour plan is carefully explained. But the climax mechanics are muddled: whether the gate opens in Scene 5 or Scene 8, which valve she could have thrown instead, and how the storm crisis ended. The reader has to reconstruct the turning point instead of following it.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The scour gate seems to open twice
- **Where:** Scene 5 (and Scene 8)
- **Quote:** "the mechanical pawl engaged the inner ratchet, seating the lock dogs into the carriage of the low scour gate... no switch at Arthur Finch's board could command the gate closed against the bronze wedge now holding the conduit open." (Scene 5); "the mechanical dog clicked home, locking the sediment scour valve into its open aperture." (Scene 8)
- **Problem:** Scene 5 describes the gate being locked open, then calls the mechanism only "primed... waiting for the hour". Scene 8 performs the same action again as the climax. The reader cannot tell what state the valve was in between the two scenes, or what Scene 5 actually achieved.
- **Reader needed:** A clear distinction between staging the key (Scene 5) and turning it (Scene 8), for example "set but not turned".

### 2. [major] The climax offers a choice between valves the reader never met
- **Where:** Scene 8 (against Scene 3 and Scene 6)
- **Quote:** "If she wrenched the primary floodway bypass, the dam would disgorge its belly in a singular, ruinous fury" (Scene 8); "The immense mass-gravity barrier would remain stable, unbreached and unstrained" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** Scene 3 establishes one plan, the scour, which leaves the head and the dam intact. But Myra in Scene 6 ("If you dump eighty fathoms into the riverbeds") and Arthur in Scene 8 ("If you throw the main floodgates") argue against a full drain. Teresa then weighs a "primary floodway bypass" that has never been shown or placed in the gallery. The reader cannot tell what the alternative was, so the moral choice has nothing to rest on.
- **Reader needed:** The destructive option set out as a real, physical mechanism before the climax, or the opponents' fears tied explicitly to the scour.

### 3. [major] The storm crisis resolves offstage
- **Where:** Scene 7 to Scene 9
- **Quote:** "the pool is rising an inch every three minutes." (Scene 7); "We are four inches from overtopping the parapet." (Scene 8); "\"The town downstream is safe,\" he said quietly." (Scene 9)
- **Problem:** Scene 7 builds an overtopping emergency, and Arthur leaves the console at its peak. Scene 9 then opens at dawn with the danger simply over. Whether the scour relieved the pressure, or Arthur went back to the levers, is left for the reader to supply.
- **Reader needed:** One beat, in Scene 8 or at the start of Scene 9, showing the open scour pulling the pool down, or the gauges falling.

### 4. [minor] Kester's offense, and how Teresa learned of it, are implied
- **Where:** Scene 4 (and Scene 5)
- **Quote:** "Nobody asked for silver communion plate from Saint Jude's to be hauled up forty feet of silt either" (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** The reader has to work out from fragments ("tender boat", "dives", "rivermen", Saint Jude's) that Kester is salvaging from the drowned village and scrubbing the records. How Teresa found out is never given. The sensor-blinding he promises is never shown, and he vanishes after Scene 5.
- **Reader needed:** One sentence naming his scheme outright and how Teresa caught it.

### 5. [minor] Scene 1 cast and POV are unanchored
- **Where:** Scene 1
- **Quote:** "The paper was dry, stiffened with government sizing, and it rattled in Arthur Finch's hand" (opening); "Inside the hall, Myra gripped Teresa's shoulder"
- **Problem:** The scene opens in Arthur's POV, then moves to Teresa's without a hinge. Myra's relation to Teresa is withheld until Scene 6 ("her cousin"). Nothing gives Teresa's age: she speaks and acts like the farm's adult owner, and only in Scene 8 does the reader learn she was a child.
- **Reader needed:** Teresa's age and household role, and who Myra is, in the flood scene itself.

### 6. [minor] Scenes 5 through 7 have no time cues against the plan
- **Where:** Scene 5, Scene 6, Scene 7
- **Quote:** "\"Tomorrow night,\" Teresa said" (end of Scene 4); "By midnight, the storm had transformed every dry creek" (Scene 7)
- **Problem:** Scene 5 has no time marker, and Kester's blind spot is "every dawn", not night. Scene 6 is floating in time, and Myra already knows the plan without being told on the page. Scene 7's "midnight" is not tied to any of these. The reader cannot order the staging, the visit and the storm.
- **Reader needed:** One time marker per scene, and a clause saying Teresa told Myra.

### 7. [minor] Jump from the gallery to the drained basin
- **Where:** Scene 8 to Scene 9
- **Quote:** "turned toward the north gallery stair to watch the drowned hearth rise from the mud." (Scene 8); "The iron rungs gave way to daylight... unspooling across the raw rim of the Blackwood Valley Basin" (Scene 9)
- **Problem:** Hours pass and the setting shifts from inside the dam to the upstream shelf with no transition. Arthur's "long night at the windlass" points to activity the reader never saw.
- **Reader needed:** A cue that the night passed and how Teresa reached the valley floor.

### 8. [nit] The scour arithmetic is recapped at the climax
- **Where:** Scene 8
- **Quote:** "the bottom scour conduits had a discharge rate of three thousand cubic feet per second, an aperture sufficient to evacuate the siltered silt and peel the shallow waters off the old Blackwood homestead"
- **Problem:** This repeats Scene 3 in the middle of the confrontation. A short reminder would do.
- **Reader needed:** Nothing more.

## What works
- Scene 3 teaches the key mechanism clearly: "It would strip the reservoir's upper reach, laying the northern basin shelf bare under open air while leaving forty feet of water against the dam face."
- The time jump is anchored in Scene 2's first sentence: "Twenty years had passed since the river drowned the orchard".
- Scene 7 gives the control room physical, followable blocking: "His knuckles were white against the brass levers of the auxiliary spillway governor, his boots braced against the oiled walnut console".

## Out of lane
The spindle key is "hooked to a rusted peg" in Scene 3 but a five-foot casting in a cage in Scene 5, and the telemetry is dead "twenty minutes" in one scene and "two hours" in the next (consistency).
