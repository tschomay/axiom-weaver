# Setup and Payoff — Toll

**Score: 2/5** — the drained hearth is carefully planted and delivered, but the climax's moral turn (sparing the town instead of breaking the dam) is a choice the story never set up, rests on a property of the scours the reader was never shown, and repeats a valve-opening the story had already performed.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The vengeance-or-justice choice at the climax was never on the table
- **Where:** Scene 8 (against Scene 1 and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "If she wrenched the primary floodway bypass, the dam would disgorge its belly in a singular, ruinous fury... It was the total reckoning she had promised the hearth stones" (Scene 8)
- **Problem:** Her vow in Scene 1 was only "I will see the lake emptied until our hearth stones stand dry under open sky", and her plan in Scene 3 was always the scour: "The dam would stand, but the hearth would dry." No "primary floodway bypass" or dam-breaking plan was ever planted. So "Vengeance was an appetite... Justice was an engineering problem" presents as a turning point what is just the original plan carried out. Arthur's heroism in Scene 7 changes nothing she does.
- **Reader needed:** Earlier evidence that Teresa was working out, or leaning toward, a breach that would hurt Oakhaven, so that choosing the scour in Scene 8 is a real change of course.

### 2. [major] The town is saved by assertion, against what the story set up
- **Where:** Scene 8 and Scene 9 (against Scene 6 and Scene 7)
- **Quote:** "without dropping the reservoir's operating head or starving the generators that kept Oakhaven's levee pumps alive" (Scene 8); "The town downstream is safe," (Scene 9)
- **Problem:** In Scene 6 Teresa accepts that her plan will cut the power ("The pumps will find coal if they must"). In Scene 7 Arthur establishes that any release reaches "the levee bend at Oakhaven in twelve minutes", and Scene 3 says the scours empty "directly into the lower gorge". Yet 3,000 cfs goes into that gorge during a storm and the town is fine. The fact that the scour spares the generators appears only in Scene 8, and the overtopping crisis ("four inches from overtopping the parapet") is never resolved on the page.
- **Reader needed:** Scene 3's calculation should show that the scour keeps the turbine head and spares the downstream levees, and Scene 8 or 9 should show the storm crisis ending, not just have Arthur report it.

### 3. [major] The scour gate is opened twice
- **Where:** Scene 5 and Scene 8
- **Quote:** "the mechanical pawl engaged the inner ratchet, seating the lock dogs into the carriage of the low scour gate... the bronze wedge now holding the conduit open." (Scene 5); "the mechanical dog clicked home, locking the sediment scour valve into its open aperture." (Scene 8)
- **Problem:** Scene 5 already carries out the climactic act, with the lock dogs engaged and the conduit held open. Then Scene 8 performs it again as the climax, and only then does the water move. A reader has to reread Scene 5 to work out what was left to do.
- **Reader needed:** Scene 5 should clearly stop one step short (key seated and primed, gate still shut), so the only release happens in Scene 8.

### 4. [minor] The Kester blackmail thread dangles
- **Where:** Scene 4 (never recalled after Scene 5)
- **Quote:** "The motion grid on the third level has a twelve-minute diagnostic blind spot every dawn," / "Tomorrow night," Teresa said
- **Problem:** The bargain sets up a precise timing constraint (the dawn blind spot) and a hold over Kester (the silver from Saint Jude's). Neither is used. The act happens at midnight in a storm, Kester disappears after Scene 5, and Arthur's "Your instruments have been dead for two hours" is put down to the storm, not to the jumpered relays.
- **Reader needed:** Either the blinded sensors and blind spot should matter when Teresa goes down in Scene 8, or the scene should be cut back to what the plot actually uses.

### 5. [nit] The compensation voucher is dropped
- **Where:** Scene 1
- **Quote:** "He reached into his satchel for the voucher of compensation."
- **Problem:** The refused payment is a natural thing to recall in a story called "Toll", when Arthur confesses in Scene 8 or at the hearth in Scene 9, but it never comes up again.
- **Reader needed:** Nothing essential. It is an unused chance for a callback.

## What works
- The vow and its hollow fulfilment are planted and paid off exactly: "I will see the lake emptied until our hearth stones stand dry under open sky" (Scene 1) becomes "The stones were dry... The vow was kept, and it had yielded nothing." (Scene 9).
- The uneven drawdown is set up with real specifics: "The Blackwood farmstead lay on that shallow bench, submerged beneath only ten fathoms" (Scene 3), so the shelf draining while the dam holds feels earned by arithmetic.
- The recognition is fairly delayed. "He did not know her." (Scene 2) and the repeated grease-burn scars pay off when Arthur's torch finds her face: "Caleb Vane's girl." (Scene 8).

## Out of lane
Teresa confronts Arthur as an adult in Scene 1 but is "Eleven" at the eviction in Scene 8, and the signing moves from the porch to "the hood of a county car" (consistency).
