# Prose Craft — Toll

**Score: 2/5** — Individual sentences are often vivid, but the scenes do not share a world or a voice. The era, the heroine's age and the geography shift between scenes, and one identifying tag is repeated so often that it pulls the reader out again and again.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Scene 1 and Scene 8 describe incompatible pasts
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 8
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "'Keep your silver paper, Arthur. You brought your chainmen and your cofferdams, but you never bought the dirt under this sill, and you never will.'" / Scene 8: "'You were ten years old when the bailiffs cleared the orchards.' / 'Eleven,' she said."
- **Problem:** Scene 1 dramatises Teresa as an adult on her own porch, addressing Arthur by his first name and handing back the notice. Scene 8 remembers her as a child whose mother was "loading the cookstove into the dray" while Arthur signed "with a gold pen on the hood of a county car". Scene 8's key moment (he finally recognises her) contradicts the scene the reader actually watched. Myra also goes from an unexplained companion in Scene 1 to "Myra Vance—her cousin" in Scene 6.
- **Reader needed:** A single account of the flooding day, so the recognition in Scene 8 matches what Scene 1 showed.

### 2. [major] The setting's era and furnishings shift from scene to scene
- **Where:** Scene 1 vs Scenes 2, 4, 6, 7
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "the red roof tiles and church tower of Oakhaven lay huddled against the mist, safe behind the high diversion levies" / Scene 6: "Oakhaven Settlement huddled against the river's lower elbow, a warren of clapboard houses and sod embankments"
- **Problem:** Oakhaven changes from a tiled town with a church tower to a clapboard settlement. The authority changes from a "provincial water commission" (Scene 1) to the "Imperial Water Authority" (Scene 2). The technology jumps from vellum, weir locks and silver paper (Scene 1) to "vacuum tubes and humming relay banks" (Scene 4), a "windscreen" and "megawatt tallies" (Scene 7). Water depths drift too: the farm lies under "ten fathoms" (Scenes 3, 9), "sixty feet of slate water" (Scene 6), with "thirty fathoms" (Scenes 1, 3) against the dam and then "eighty fathoms" (Scenes 5, 6, 8). Every scene seems to have imagined its own dam.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent period and set of figures, so the engineering plot feels real.

### 3. [major] "Grease-burn scars along her jaw" is used as a tag in six scenes
- **Where:** Scene 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8
- **Quote:** Scene 7: "her fingers brushing the grease-burn scars along her jaw."
- **Problem:** Almost every scene re-identifies Teresa by the same scar in near-identical words: Scene 2 "scarred along the jawline by grease-burns", Scene 3 "The grease-burn scars across her jaw tightened", Scene 4 "down to the grease-burn scars along her jaw", Scene 6 "the grease-burn scars along Teresa's jawline", Scene 8 "the ridge of scars tracing her jawline". Other tags repeat the same way: Arthur's "iron-gray" hair (Scenes 2, 7, 8, 9), the "oiled walnut" desk (Scenes 2, 4, 7), "three notched square-toothed keys" (Scenes 3, 5), "three hundred feet below/beneath the crest" (Scenes 3, 5, 8), and "the bronze manual spindle override key" in full (Scenes 3, 8).
- **Reader needed:** An identifying detail set up once and then trusted. Repeating it by rote reads as each scene re-introducing her.

### 4. [minor] Characters and calculations re-introduced as if new
- **Where:** Scene 7, Scene 8
- **Quote:** Scene 7: "stood Arthur Finch, the man whose signature on the eviction parchment had buried her family’s valley two decades ago."
- **Problem:** By Scene 7 the reader has met Arthur four times. Scene 8 also repeats the Scene 3 discharge arithmetic ("the bottom scour conduits had a discharge rate of three thousand cubic feet per second, an aperture sufficient to…"), which the reader has already followed.
- **Reader needed:** Callbacks that assume the reader has been reading.

### 5. [minor] Scenes end on aphorisms and portentous one-liners
- **Where:** Scene 2, 3, 6, 8, 9
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "Vengeance was an appetite that demanded the whole world drown to prove a wound. Justice was an engineering problem."
- **Problem:** Most scenes close on a summarising line or thesis: "bite into her flesh like an old promise" (Scene 2), "The math was pitiless and exact. The dam would stand, but the hearth would dry." (Scene 3), "a fragile strand stretched across a deep and coming dark" (Scene 6), "The vow was kept, and it had yielded nothing." (Scene 9). The last one tells the reader what the fine hearth description just showed.
- **Reader needed:** Endings that trust the image. The dry hearth in Scene 9 already says the vow yielded nothing.

### 6. [minor] Villain and heroine talk in exposition
- **Where:** Scene 4, Scene 7
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "'The drum recorder was calibrated to register drift timber, not an unauthorized tender boat riding above the drowned churchyard,' Teresa said"
- **Problem:** Teresa's opening line is a technical briefing addressed to the reader. In Scene 7, Arthur recaps his own backstory mid-crisis ("I signed the warrants that drove sixty families off the meadows so this concrete could stand."). In Scene 8 he confesses in rounded speeches ("I know what was drowned. I know what was stolen.").
- **Reader needed:** Dialogue that has a purpose inside the scene rather than one aimed at the reader.

### 7. [nit] Doubled words and careless repetition within sentences
- **Where:** Scene 2, 5, 8
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "an aperture sufficient to evacuate the siltered silt" / "its seepage troughs trickling with black seepage"
- **Problem:** The same kind of echo happens elsewhere: "a massive casting… terminating in a massive cruciform head" (Scene 5), "across the official seal, setting her mark beneath the stamped seal" (Scene 2), and "the infant warmers humming… hummed against its porcelain insulators" (Scene 6). "Hollow"/"hollowed" appears in at least seven places across Scenes 2–9.
- **Reader needed:** A line edit, so the prose doesn't stumble at close range.

### 8. [nit] Similes stacked in the action beats
- **Where:** Scene 5 (also Scene 6)
- **Quote:** "her boot heels striking the perforated steel decking with the dull resonance of lead dropped onto wet flagstones. Kester Holt descended behind her, his breath rasping through his teeth like paper dragged over gravel"
- **Problem:** Two sound similes in consecutive sentences, followed by Scene 6's "with the dull, hollow knocking of an iron clock". The descent gets decorated rather than paced.
- **Reader needed:** Fewer figures, placed where the sensation matters.

## What works
- The anticlimax of the hearth is plain and exact: "There was no ghost waiting for her in the chimney flue… The hearth was just five slabs of gray river granite resting in the mud."
- The flooding in Scene 1 has a fresh, grounded image: "The river did not pause; it backed into the basin like dark ale rising in an earthen jug."
- A spare, effective beat: "He did not know her."

## Out of lane
Scene 8 describes her choice as "If she wrenched the primary floodway bypass…" when the key is already seated on the scour valve, which muddles what she is actually choosing between; that's for the plot-logic lens.
