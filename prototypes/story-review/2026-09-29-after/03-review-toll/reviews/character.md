# Character Coherence — Toll

**Score: 3/5** — Arthur is well motivated and consistent, but Teresa's climactic "choice" between vengeance and justice is one she never faced on the page. Her plan was always the harmless shelf drain, so the turn is announced, not earned, and her reaction to Arthur's heroism is skipped.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The climactic turn reverses a plan she never had
- **Where:** Scene 8, against Scene 3
- **Quote:** "The immense mass-gravity barrier would remain stable, unbreached and unstrained, even as the valley floor above it was drained to stone." (Scene 3) / "If she wrenched the primary floodway bypass, the dam would disgorge its belly in a singular, ruinous fury... It was the total reckoning she had promised the hearth stones" (Scene 8)
- **Problem:** Everything Teresa does from Scene 3 on (the calculation, the key, the scour gate) aims at a drain that leaves the dam and the town intact. In Scene 8 the story suddenly offers her a total-destruction option she never planned, so she can nobly turn it down: "Vengeance was an appetite... Justice was an engineering problem." The moral turn is a line of narration, not a decision we watched her weigh.
- **Reader needed:** Earlier evidence that Teresa actually wanted, or was tempted by, the ruinous option, so that choosing the scour is a real change.

### 2. [major] Teresa accepts harm to the town her own maths says it won't face
- **Where:** Scene 6, against Scene 3 and Scene 8
- **Quote:** "The pumps will find coal if they must... I will not leave them under Arthur Finch's pond to save his town from an unseasonable freshet." (Scene 6) / "without dropping the reservoir's operating head or starving the generators that kept Oakhaven's levee pumps alive" (Scene 8)
- **Problem:** Teresa knows the scour leaves forty feet of head against the dam. Yet when Myra warns her, she doesn't reassure her. She coldly accepts the risk to four hundred people. Then in Scene 8 the same plan is presented as the thing that spares the town. Her stance toward Oakhaven swings to suit each scene's drama.
- **Reader needed:** Either Teresa tells Myra the town is safe (and Myra's fear becomes something else), or Teresa truly doesn't know yet, and the Scene 8 discovery is her own.

### 3. [major] Arthur's heroism never lands on Teresa
- **Where:** Scene 7 to Scene 8
- **Quote:** "Yet here he stood in the dead of night, broken-backed and alone, straining his frail heart against the deluge to shield the very town she was willing to place at risk." (Scene 7) / "She fled the console while his chin was still sunk against his collar" (Scene 8)
- **Problem:** Teresa sees her enemy as a man in the scene built to change her view of him, and her response is to run straight to the valve. We don't know whether she opens the scour to help him in the flood, in spite of him, or in a panic. The key moment of her arc has no visible motive.
- **Reader needed:** One beat, either in the control room or on the stairs, where we see what Arthur's struggle does to her intent.

### 4. [minor] Kester is a plot puppet
- **Where:** Scene 4 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** "Kester moved past her toward the pedestal... He dropped to his knees in the film of stagnant wash, unbuckling a leather tool kit." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Kester is blackmailed into blinding the sensors, then with no stated reason goes further and greases the gears for her in the gallery, taking on far more risk. After that he disappears. His fear shows in his body ("trembling fingers") but we never learn what he wants.
- **Reader needed:** A reason Kester follows her into the gallery, or a line showing what he gets or fears beyond the drum chart.

### 5. [minor] Arthur's recognition comes late for a name he'd know
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 8
- **Quote:** "He did not know her." / "The board requires the hydraulic seal certificate signed in triplicate, Mistress Vane" (Scene 2) / "Vane... Caleb Vane's girl." (Scene 8)
- **Problem:** Arthur says he has heard the evictions every night for twenty years, yet a Vane signs on at his desk and works under him for weeks without the name registering. His guilt, which is well drawn, makes that blindness harder to believe.
- **Reader needed:** A reason the name slips past him (she uses another surname, a common name, his distraction), or an earlier flicker of recognition.

### 6. [nit] Arthur knows Myra by name
- **Where:** Scene 8
- **Quote:** "Myra's ward is full of children who will suffocate if the grid drops before dawn."
- **Problem:** Nothing shows how the dam superintendent knows Teresa's cousin by name, or knows that the two are linked.
- **Reader needed:** A hint of Arthur's ties to the clinic or to Myra.

## What works
- Arthur's guilt is set up from his first line, "his voice flat, drained of any will to quarrel", and pays off in "I signed sixty-four of them in a single week."
- Arthur's reason for not opening the crest gates is specific and in character: "I will not have them drowned by my negligence to spare the crown of the wall."
- The anticlimax at the hearth is shown through objects, not stated feeling: "The hearth was just five slabs of gray river granite resting in the mud."

## Out of lane
Scene 1 shows Teresa as an adult refusing Arthur's voucher, but Scene 8 has her aged "Eleven" with her mother loading the dray (consistency).
