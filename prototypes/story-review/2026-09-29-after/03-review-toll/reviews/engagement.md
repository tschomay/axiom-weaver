# Engagement and Believability — Toll

**Score: 3/5** — The flooding and the storm-night control room are gripping, but the climactic moral choice is hollow (her plan was harmless from Scene 3), the middle scenes meet no resistance, and the dawn payoff breaks the physics the storm just set up.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The climactic choice was settled in Scene 3
- **Where:** Scene 8 (against Scene 3 and Scene 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "The immense mass-gravity barrier would remain stable, unbreached and unstrained, even as the valley floor above it was drained to stone." / Scene 8: "Vengeance was an appetite that demanded the whole world drown to prove a wound. Justice was an engineering problem."
- **Problem:** Scene 3 establishes that the scour plan drains only the shelf and leaves the dam and head intact. Scenes 6 and 8 still stage a crisis of conscience over drowning Oakhaven, and Scene 8 brings in a "primary floodway bypass" option that Teresa never prepared. So "Keep your town" isn't a sacrifice or a turn. She does exactly what she planned. The pressure from Myra and Arthur ends up aimed at a threat that never existed.
- **Reader needed:** Either a plan that really does endanger the town (so choosing the scour is a real retreat), or for Teresa to know in Scene 6 that it's harmless and stay silent for a reason that costs her something.

### 2. [major] The dawn payoff contradicts the storm
- **Where:** Scene 9 (against Scene 7 and Scene 8)
- **Quote:** Scene 7: "the pool is rising an inch every three minutes" / Scene 8: "We are four inches from overtopping the parapet." / Scene 9: "What remained below was not water, but an expanse of slate-colored sludge"
- **Problem:** A few hours after the reservoir is about to overtop from a flash flood, one scour conduit has left the northern shelf as dry mud with the hearth stones drying in the sun. The storm was built up as enormous, then it vanishes, and nobody says what the 3,000 cfs release did to the flooded gorge and town downstream. A reader stops believing the ending right when it needs to land.
- **Reader needed:** Time passing (days, the storm easing) or an explanation of why the shelf drains despite the inflow, plus a word on what happened downstream.

### 3. [major] Scenes 2–5 meet almost no resistance
- **Where:** Scenes 2, 3, 4, 5
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "He did not know her." / Scene 4: "\"The motion grid on the third level has a twelve-minute diagnostic blind spot every dawn,\" he muttered. \"I can wire the scour loop into the bypass shunt.\""
- **Problem:** Finch hands her the keys, the schematic hands her the answer, Kester gives in after one threat, and the override key is waiting on a peg. Four scenes of preparation all sit at the same low hum, with every obstacle dissolving on contact. My attention slipped here until the storm arrived.
- **Reader needed:** At least one real setback in the preparation (a near-recognition, Kester pushing back or trying to double-cross her, the valve refusing to move).

### 4. [minor] The storm turns up exactly on the night of the plan
- **Where:** Scene 7 (against Scene 4)
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "\"Tomorrow night,\" Teresa said" / Scene 7: "the rain having broken over the crest in a sudden, violent deluge"
- **Problem:** A record flood arrives on the very night she has set up. That puts her on stage with Arthur at his most sympathetic, but it's coincidence rather than consequence. Nobody reconsiders whether a scour release is wise in the middle of a flood.
- **Reader needed:** Either Teresa timing her act to the forecast storm on purpose, or her plan being forced forward because the storm threatens to expose it.

### 5. [minor] Kester drops out of the story
- **Where:** Scene 5 (then absent)
- **Quote:** "Kester Holt descended behind her, his breath rasping through his teeth like paper dragged over gravel"
- **Problem:** The blackmail deal and the sensor blinding are set up with care, and then Kester and the sensors play no part on the storm night. Finch simply walks down and finds her. The deal's payoff is never cashed.
- **Reader needed:** Either the blinding matters on the night (it's why Finch's instruments are dead, or its failure brings Finch down), or a shorter subplot.

### 6. [minor] Arthur's recognition and confession come as a monologue
- **Where:** Scene 8
- **Quote:** "\"I signed it,\" he said, his breath hitching on the confession. \"God help me, Teresa, I signed sixty-four of them in a single week.\""
- **Problem:** Scene 7 already won sympathy for Arthur through what he did. Scene 8 then has him recognise her, confess, and offer his hands to be crushed, all in one speech. The antagonist collapses before she has to press him. His mention of "Myra's ward" also assumes a connection the story never set up.
- **Reader needed:** Let Scene 7's actions carry Arthur's decency, and keep some resistance in him.

### 7. [nit] The emptiness is stated outright at the end
- **Where:** Scene 9
- **Quote:** "The vow was kept, and it had yielded nothing."
- **Problem:** The paragraph before already shows the hollowness well ("The hearth was just five slabs of gray river granite resting in the mud"). This line spells it out.
- **Reader needed:** Trust in the image to carry it.

## What works
- The flooding of the farm in Scene 1 is vivid and gives the vow real weight: "It swallowed the rain barrel, poured through the lower sashes with a shatter of glass, and crept up the shake roof until only the brick chimney stood out".
- Scene 7 turns Arthur around through action under pressure, which is the story's best escalation: "If I trip the crest flumes now, the wave reaches the levee bend at Oakhaven in twelve minutes".
- The anticlimax at the hearth is honest and specific to this story: "the iron pot-crane, still pinned in its socket, encrusted in freshwater calc and lime until it resembled a withered, fossilized limb" (Scene 9).

## Out of lane
Teresa speaks as an adult householder at the eviction in Scene 1, but in Scene 8 she was "Eleven", and her cousin is "Myra Vance" while she is "Vane" (consistency).
