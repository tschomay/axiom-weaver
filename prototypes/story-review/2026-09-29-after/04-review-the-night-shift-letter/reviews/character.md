# Character Coherence — The Night Shift Letter

**Score: 3/5** — Arthur's biggest choice (giving up Leeds and handing Martin most of his savings) turns in one paragraph with no groundwork. Julian's reason for writing changes each time he explains it, and Henderson and Martin exist mainly to obstruct.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Arthur abandons his life's goal in a single paragraph
- **Where:** Scene 13, against Scene 1 and Scene 7
- **Quote:** "sixty pounds away from never having to push a laundry cart past the laundry chutes of St. Jude's ever again." (Scene 1) / "confiding his dreams of the Leeds workshop, the sweet resinous smell of freshly planed timber" (Scene 7) / "Yet looking at Martin now... the illusion crumbled. Leeds would not be freedom." (Scene 13)
- **Problem:** Leeds is Arthur's want through twelve scenes, and he dreams of it tenderly as late as Scene 7. It collapses because Martin is impatient, which Arthur has known "since grammar school". Nothing new about Martin happens in this scene. We are told the illusion crumbled; we don't see why it crumbles now.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier crack in the Leeds dream (a doubt, a memory of working under Martin), so that turning it down completes a thought we have watched grow.

### 2. [major] Arthur's gift to Martin has no visible reason
- **Where:** Scene 13 and Scene 14
- **Quote:** "The five hundred is yours... Take it as a gift." (Scene 13) / "A gift, to buy his silence and my freedom." (Scene 14)
- **Problem:** Arthur has scraped for every pound and resents Martin. Yet he hands the brother who bullied him most of his savings, for nothing in return. "Buy his silence" refers to nothing Martin could reveal. It reads as a gesture the scene wanted, not something this cautious, frugal man would do.
- **Reader needed:** A reason Arthur feels he owes Martin (a debt, a promise, guilt), or a sign of what the money buys him.

### 3. [major] Julian's motive for writing changes with every telling
- **Where:** Scene 6, Scene 12 and Scene 15
- **Quote:** "If someone had to carry the rot of how this had started, it would not be Arthur." (Scene 6) / "I meant to burn it... Because I couldn't bear to let the conversation stop." (Scene 12) / "I took the bait note out of the slot and wrote back so he'd have nothing to mock." (Scene 15)
- **Problem:** Julian's reason for answering is protectiveness in Scene 6, loneliness in Scene 12 and tactics against Kieran in Scene 15. The versions could all be true, but he never brings them together, and Arthur never notices the shift. As a result the central deception, the premise's "not who the notes suggest", has no settled inner logic.
- **Reader needed:** One clear account of why Julian first picked up the pen, told the same way each time, even if other motives are layered on top.

### 4. [minor] Arthur's hurt resolves offstage
- **Where:** Scene 12 to Scene 13
- **Quote:** "Arthur unpinned the blue notebook from beneath his wool lapel and hurled it violently across the frozen asphalt." (Scene 12) / "not the sneering quill of a prankster, but the uneven script of a man reaching blindly into the dark to touch another human soul." (Scene 13)
- **Problem:** Arthur goes from fury and humiliation to tenderness on the strength of one speech, and gives no reply to Julian before the scene cuts to Martin. A man who has just been publicly mocked would at least test Julian's story.
- **Reader needed:** Arthur's own response to Julian's confession, whether doubt, a question, or a choice to believe him.

### 5. [minor] Henderson and Martin are plot obstacles
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 2, Scene 9 and Scene 11 (Henderson); Scene 3 and Scene 13 (Martin)
- **Quote:** "St. Jude's head porter did not merely monitor regulations; he weaponised them." (Scene 1) / "the small, avaricious pinch of his mouth" (Scene 13)
- **Problem:** Henderson appears only to announce deadlines and enforce rules, and Martin only to demand money. Neither wants anything beyond blocking Arthur, so the Leeds decision is framed against a cardboard figure.
- **Reader needed:** One glimpse of either man's own stake, such as why Martin wants Arthur in particular as a partner, to give Arthur's rejection some weight.

### 6. [nit] Arthur's sudden defiance is narrated
- **Where:** Scene 11
- **Quote:** "The shame that usually folded him in two had curdled, quite suddenly, into a hard, stubborn spike of fury."
- **Problem:** The narrator states the switch, not the scene. It is believable after what he overheard, but it is told to us, not shown.
- **Reader needed:** A concrete beat that tips Arthur from fleeing to standing his ground.

## What works
- Julian's uncle is set up in Scene 8 before it becomes his objection, so his push-back in Scene 14 feels earned: "I will not let you trade Martin's demands for my despair."
- Julian and Arthur sound different: Julian is controlled and dry ("The laundry doesn't care about your theology, Kieran"), while Arthur is apologetic and hedging ("Coat's already on, Mr Henderson... Not planning on taking up residence").
- Kieran's spite stays consistent from the boiler room to the ramp: "Three pints of stout at the Black Dog that says I could bait the quietest clerk in St. Jude's."

## Out of lane
The cash total keeps shifting (740, "seventy pounds shy", and 300 kept plus 500 given), and Arthur mentions "a dead woman's pen name" that was never set up (consistency).
