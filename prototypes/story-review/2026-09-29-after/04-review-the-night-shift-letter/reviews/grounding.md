# Grounding and Detail — The Night Shift Letter

**Score: 2/5** — The early scenes are well placed, but from Scene 9 on the calendar and clock slip without cues. The central prop (the blue notebook) changes owner and form, and a key accusation refers to something that never happened, so the reader is reconstructing the plot rather than following it.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Scene 11 reads as continuous with Scene 10 but is twelve hours later and in a different room
- **Where:** Scene 10 to Scene 11
- **Quote:** "Arthur waited only until the heavy rubber tread of Henderson's boots had squeaked down toward the laundry chute" (Scene 10, morning muster); "Up through the acoustic throat of the flue, the voices tumbled again... To Arthur, standing frozen at the threshold where the heat of the boilers gave way to the clammy draft of the locker room" (Scene 11)
- **Problem:** Scene 11 opens on the same overheard voices, as if no time had passed. Yet Arthur is now at the locker-room threshold, not in the boiler gallery, and Henderson calls "Seven minutes to seven... Day staff off the premises by seven sharp". That is the evening lockout, after a whole day shift that goes unmentioned. The reader has to reread to work out that it is now evening.
- **Reader needed:** A clear time jump ("All day he carried it...") and Arthur's move back to the locker room.

### 2. [major] New Year's Eve arrives unannounced, and the night vanishes
- **Where:** Scene 13 (and Scene 15)
- **Quote:** "It's New Year's Eve. That cooperage firm from Hunslet is meeting me at the train station tomorrow at nine" (Scene 13); "the great morning bells began their resonant tolling, announcing the seven o'clock shift change" (Scene 15)
- **Problem:** The last dates the reader had were the Friday sweep "three days off" and Martin's deadline "in a fortnight". Nothing marks the days passing up to New Year's Eve. Martin is sitting in Henderson's office by an arrangement made offstage. Scene 13 happens "ten minutes" after the 7 p.m. lockout, yet Scene 15 opens to the morning bells. The whole night, and the fact that the sealed basement opened again, are skipped.
- **Reader needed:** Date cues as the story moves toward the deadline, and a bridge through the night before Scene 15.

### 3. [major] Whose notebook it is, and how the letters change hands, is never clear
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 4, Scene 6, Scene 7 (also Scene 9)
- **Quote:** "the neat, clothbound blue notebook resting on the upper shelf" (Scene 1, as Julian's); "propped against his blue cloth correspondence notebook" (Scene 4, now Arthur's); "rested a clean, cloth-bound notebook bound in dark blue linen" (Scene 7)
- **Problem:** The whole plot runs through this object. It starts as Julian's and becomes Arthur's. Arthur writes in it but leaves "the folded reply". Julian reads "a folded sheet of lined paper", then writes in the notebook. Arthur then finds it as if it were new and "clean". Henderson also carries "the dog-eared blue notebook" in Scene 9. The reader cannot follow how the correspondence physically moves.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent method: a single shared notebook, always on the same shelf, both men writing in it.

### 4. [major] "A dead woman's pen name" was never set up
- **Where:** Scene 12
- **Quote:** "Instead you played him at his own game? Wrote to me under a dead woman's pen name?"
- **Problem:** No pen name, signature or dead woman appears anywhere in the story. The card is "unsigned", and Arthur imagined a male clerk. This accusation lands at the climax of the confrontation and points at nothing the reader has seen.
- **Reader needed:** Either the pen name planted in the letters, or the line removed.

### 5. [minor] The dates between the sweep warning and the inspection don't add up
- **Where:** Scene 3, Scene 8, Scene 9
- **Quote:** "the news that the day relief was hopelessly marooned across town" (Scene 8); "Arthur Finch stood frozen before Locker 44" at "six forty-five in the morning... the exact Friday moment" (Scene 9)
- **Problem:** Scene 3 has no time cue, although it seems to be the same evening as Scene 1, after the day-staff lockout. Scene 8's blizzard strands the day shift, yet the Friday dawn inspection finds Arthur on duty. The reader cannot tell which night the blizzard was, or how Arthur got in.
- **Reader needed:** A day marker on Scenes 3, 7 and 8, and a clause on the day shift getting through.

### 6. [minor] The money gap closes offstage
- **Where:** Scene 13 (against Scenes 1, 3, 11)
- **Quote:** "Not yet the eight hundred... but sixty pounds away" (Scene 1); "peeling away three hundred pounds in crisp banknotes... The remaining bundle—five hundred pounds" (Scene 13)
- **Problem:** Earlier scenes build the whole squeeze on Arthur being sixty short. Then he holds at least £800 with no explanation. The envelope's location also drifts (spare boot, flue gap, worn boot, "carried up from the locker").
- **Reader needed:** A beat on where the last sixty came from and where the envelope actually is.

### 7. [minor] How Julian learned of Kieran's prank is never shown
- **Where:** Scene 5 (and Scene 6)
- **Quote:** "is for you to drop the little game you're playing with Locker 44." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Julian knows the card was Kieran's dare, and even his plan to post Arthur's answer on the noticeboard (Scene 12), but the reader never sees how he found out. Scene 5 leans on a "game" the reader hasn't met yet.
- **Reader needed:** One line on how Julian discovered the dare.

### 8. [minor] Recaps of what the reader was just shown
- **Where:** Scene 4, Scene 12, Scene 14
- **Quote:** "Kieran had planted the initial romantic card as a workplace prank, an idle wager to mock Arthur's reclusiveness" (Scene 12, immediately after Kieran says so)
- **Problem:** This is chronic re-explaining. Scene 4 opens by restating Martin's terms from Scene 3. Scene 12 paraphrases Kieran's confession. Scene 14 has Arthur retell the prank to the man who stopped it.
- **Reader needed:** Nothing more. Cutting these would let the scenes move.

## What works
- Scene 1 sets up the lockout rule so the later seal can land: "electronic basement doors deadbolt automatically at 07:00 and 19:00, and if that timer trips while you're still tying your laces, you'll be sleeping down here".
- Some openings anchor time and place in a single phrase: "The basement locker room at two in the morning possessed all the charm of a drained canal" (Scene 6).
- Julian's uncle is introduced cleanly, with place and reason, before he becomes load-bearing: "In the corner room, under the relentless hum of the steam radiator, his bedridden uncle lay propped against thin institutional pillows." (Scene 8)

## Out of lane
The locker key hangs from a wire clip, a wire ring, a nail, a magnetic clip, a bent pin and a bracket in turn, and Julian says he "took the bait note out of the slot" though Arthur found and kept it (consistency).
