# Logical Consistency — The Night Shift Letter

**Score: 2/5** — several major contradictions: the clock flips from morning to evening mid-scene, a November week becomes New Year's Eve, £740 turns into £800, and the cash and the notebook keep moving around. By Scene 13 the reader has stopped trusting the numbers and positions the plot depends on.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Morning muster turns into the 7 p.m. lockout within one continuous moment
- **Where:** Scene 10 → Scene 11 (and Scenes 13–15)
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "precisely at six forty-five in the morning … Supervisor Henderson launched his rounds" → Scene 10: "Arthur waited only until … Henderson's boots had squeaked down … He bolted for the stairs." / Scene 11: "High on the wall, the warning horn bleated its three-minute caution: eighteen fifty-seven."
- **Problem:** Scene 11 picks up the same overheard flue conversation ("the voices tumbled again"), yet it is suddenly 6:57 in the evening, with "Day staff off the premises by seven sharp". Then after the lockout, Scene 13 ("ten minutes later") and Scene 14 read as continuous, but Scene 15 opens with "the great morning bells … announcing the seven o'clock shift change". About twelve hours vanish twice.
- **Reader needed:** Clear markers for how much time passes between Scenes 10 and 11, and between Scenes 14 and 15.

### 2. [major] A few days in November end on New Year's Eve
- **Where:** Scene 2 (and Scenes 4, 11, 13)
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "just how personal an insult a November gale could be" … "Friday dawn was three days off." / Scene 13: "It's New Year's Eve."
- **Problem:** The events run Tuesday to Friday in consecutive shifts: card, reply, reply, blizzard, Friday inspection, lockout, Martin. Yet the Friday inspection's day ends on 31 December. Scene 4's "a fortnight" and Scene 11's "six more weeks of scraped wages" give two more deadlines that don't agree with either. The romance is also described as lasting "weeks" ("spend weeks forging a soul on paper", Scene 11; "For weeks Arthur had clung", Scene 13), but on the page it is two exchanges over about two days.
- **Reader needed:** One calendar: either the correspondence runs for weeks and the story says so, or the deadline sits within the story's actual few days.

### 3. [major] The savings change amount and location
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scenes 11, 13, 14, 15)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "Seven hundred and forty pounds. … sixty pounds away" / Scene 13: "peeling away three hundred pounds in crisp banknotes … The remaining bundle—five hundred pounds"
- **Problem:** Arthur never earns the missing £60, but £740 splits into £300 plus £500, which is £800. Scene 11 also has him "seventy pounds shy". In Scene 14 the £300 is "in my pocket", but in Scene 15 the envelope, "slimmer now by five hundred pounds", is back in the locker, which he has only just opened.
- **Reader needed:** Totals that add up from the £740 set in Scene 1, and the remaining cash in one place.

### 4. [major] The cash envelope is in a different place in almost every scene
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scenes 3, 6, 7, 9, 11, 13)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "Arthur swiftly shoved the envelope deep into the dark gap beside the flue's lower vent" / Scene 6: "the spare work boots … faithfully concealing the brown paper envelope resting deep in the toe"
- **Problem:** The envelope moves from the spare boot in the locker (Scene 1), to Arthur's left boot (Scene 3), to the flue gap (Scene 3), back to the spare boot in the locker (Scene 6), to the boot he is wearing (Scene 7, then "his right work boot" in Scene 9 and "his left work boot" in Scene 11), and finally to "a thick brown paper parcel" tied with twine that "he had carried up from the locker" (Scene 13). The Friday inspection's tension depends on where the money is.
- **Reader needed:** The envelope moved only when the prose shows it being moved.

### 5. [major] The thrown notebook is picked up twice
- **Where:** Scene 12 (and Scene 13)
- **Quote:** Scene 12: "Julian walked out onto the tarmac, knelt with stiff deliberation, and gathered the blue notebook into his chapped hands" / Scene 13: "At his boots … lay the blue notebook … He bent stiffly … and picked it up."
- **Problem:** Julian is holding the notebook when Scene 12 ends, yet it is lying at Arthur's feet in the very next paragraph.
- **Reader needed:** Julian hands it back, puts it down, or never picks it up.

### 6. [major] Arthur accuses Julian of something the story never set up
- **Where:** Scene 12
- **Quote:** "Instead you played him at his own game? Wrote to me under a dead woman's pen name?"
- **Problem:** No pen name, signature or dead woman appears anywhere. The card was "unsigned" (Scene 4), and Arthur pictured his correspondent as a male clerk (Scene 7). Arthur is acting on knowledge neither he nor the reader was ever given.
- **Reader needed:** Either the pen name is shown earlier, or the accusation matches what Arthur actually saw.

### 7. [minor] Julian's account of how the letters began changes
- **Where:** Scene 15 (and Scenes 4, 5, 12)
- **Quote:** Scene 15: "I took the bait note out of the slot and wrote back so he'd have nothing to mock." / Scene 12: "I took your letter out before he ever read a word of it."
- **Problem:** Arthur found and read Kieran's card himself and wrote first (Scene 4). Julian then took Arthur's reply (Scene 5), which is what Scene 12 says. Scene 15 swaps the order. Kieran's plan also shifts from "the noticeboard by the laundry wash-house" (Scene 12) to "read your answer aloud in the mess hall" (Scene 15). Scene 13's "the living, exhausted porter who had stood quietly beside him on the flagstones" contradicts Scene 1, where Arthur "had never seen [Julian] in the flesh".
- **Reader needed:** The same sequence each time the origin is retold.

### 8. [minor] Who owns the notebook, and where the key is hidden, keep changing
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scenes 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 11, 15)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "the neat, clothbound blue notebook resting on the upper shelf" [signalling Julian's presence] / Scene 4: "propped against his blue cloth correspondence notebook" / Scene 7: "rested a clean, cloth-bound notebook bound in dark blue linen"
- **Problem:** The notebook is Julian's in Scene 1 and Arthur's in Scene 4. In Scene 7 it is introduced as a new, "clean" object even though Arthur wrote in it the day before. Scene 6 says "where Julian had tucked it". It sits on the upper shelf in Scene 6 and on the "lower shelf where Julian had left it" in Scene 9. The key gets a different hiding place almost every scene: in Arthur's trouser pocket (Scene 1), on a wire ring (Scene 3), on a nail (Scenes 6, 7), on a magnetic clip (Scene 9), on a bent pin (Scene 11), behind a bracket (Scene 15).
- **Reader needed:** One owner and one shelf for the notebook, and one hiding place for the key.

## What works
- The shift-seal rule is planted early and paid off: "electronic basement doors deadbolt automatically at 07:00 and 19:00" (Scene 1) → "Seven o'clock precisely. … the motorized deadbolts drove home" (Scene 11).
- The blackmail over the ethanol carries cleanly into later scenes: "Henderson finds this crate sitting directly on his desk" (Scene 5) → Kieran "still smarting under the thumb of Julian's discovery regarding certain purloined spirits" (Scene 8).
- Julian's uncle is set up before the confession, so it doesn't arrive from nowhere: "his bedridden uncle lay propped against thin institutional pillows" (Scene 8) → "since my uncle fell ill" (Scene 12).

## Out of lane
Henderson carries a "dog-eared blue notebook" (Scene 9) in the scene where the reader most needs to follow the other blue notebook. That is a clarity issue for grounding.
