# Prose Craft — The Night Shift Letter

**Score: 2/5** — The arch comic narrator has real charm in the opening scenes, but across fifteen scenes the key objects keep changing, whole passages are repeated, one bodily cliché recurs about eight times, and the voice lurches from comic to earnest, so the reader is pulled out again and again.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The central props change form and location from scene to scene
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 3, 6, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "Arthur fished out the small brass key hooked over a wire clip" / Scene 9: "He retrieved the little brass key from its hidden magnetic clip" / Scene 11: "the brass key, hitched to its small bent pin"
- **Problem:** The locker key hangs on a wire clip (Scene 1), a wire ring behind the door flange (Scene 3), a nail (Scenes 6, 7), a magnetic clip (Scene 9), a bent pin (Scene 11) and a rusted bracket (Scene 15). The cash moves even more. It starts in the toe of the spare boot (Scene 1), then is hidden beside the flue (Scene 3). In Scene 9 it's in the boot he is wearing ("down in the toe of his right work boot… dug painfully into his arch"). By Scene 11 it's in his "left work boot" and "seventy pounds shy". In Scene 13 a twine-tied parcel yields "three hundred" plus "five hundred" from the original "seven hundred and forty". Even the blue notebook migrates: in Scene 9 it's on the "lower shelf", and Henderson has "the dog-eared blue notebook tucked permanently under his arm". Every scene reinvents the objects the romance depends on.
- **Reader needed:** One fixed hiding place, one sum and one notebook, so the locker feels like a real shared space.

### 2. [major] Passages are repeated or replayed across the scene seams
- **Where:** Scene 10 → Scene 11 (also Scenes 3/10, 12/14/15)
- **Quote:** Scene 10: "He could walk away. He ought to have bolted right then" / Scene 11: "He could walk away. In fact, every sensible preservation instinct… screamed at him"
- **Problem:** Scene 11 opens by replaying the overheard flue conversation Scene 10 had already finished, and repeats its sentence of deliberation word for word. The flue's "speaking trumpet" acoustics are explained in Scene 3 and again in Scene 10 ("as neatly as a speaking tube"). Julian's confession about his dying uncle is given in Scene 12 ("Nursing a dying man by day…") and again in Scene 14 ("My uncle is dying in the infirmary annex"). The Kieran-card explanation is given three times (Scenes 12, 14, 15). Setting and character re-introductions pile up the same way: "the sub-basement loading bay—" with a descriptive appositive in Scenes 2, 8 and 12, and "Martin, his elder brother" in Scene 13 after Scene 3 had introduced him.
- **Reader needed:** Each beat told once, with later scenes picking up where the last left off.

### 3. [major] The heart and ribs cliché is used about eight times
- **Where:** Scenes 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "Arthur Finch stood frozen before Locker 44, his heart hammering against his ribs."
- **Problem:** The same body shorthand recurs across the story: "his heart doing an unpleasant sideways stutter against his ribs" (Scene 3), "his heart knocking against his ribs" (Scene 4), "an ache so sharp and tender beneath his ribs" (Scene 5), "The pressure of confession leaned hard against his ribs" (Scene 6), "Arthur’s heart gave a distinct, undignified hop against his ribs" (Scene 7), "burning against his ribs like a stolen ingot" (Scene 10), and "burning against Arthur’s ribs" (Scene 11).
- **Reader needed:** Emotional states rendered in more than one register, especially in a courtship story that depends on feeling.

### 4. [minor] Comic similes for Henderson, and scenes all opening the same way
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 2, 9, 11 (openings: Scenes 3, 6, 7, 9, 10)
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "pawing through coat hooks with the grim enthusiasm of a dyspeptic badger turning over river stones for grubs."
- **Problem:** Henderson gets a new comic animal or prop comparison in nearly every appearance: "clipboard raised like a warrant" (Scene 1), "clipboard pinned like a breastplate" and "an elderly gander" (Scene 2), a "badger" (Scene 9), "an indignant bantam rooster" and "a bailiff" (Scene 11). His peppermint lozenges are re-announced in Scenes 2, 9 and 11. Scenes open on the same arch generalisation built around "possessed": "possessed all the charm of a drained canal" (Scene 6), "possessed none of the gilded, hopeful quality" (Scene 7), "possessed an uncanny knack for curdling" (Scene 9). Scene 10 opens with "Muster, as any sensible soul… knew".
- **Reader needed:** A comic voice that varies its moves, so the running jokes build rather than reset.

### 5. [minor] The tone lurches from comic narrator to sincere melodrama
- **Where:** Scenes 1–9 vs Scenes 12–15
- **Quote:** Scene 7: "the steam pipes groaned awake like a choir of arthritic bulldogs grumbling over their rheumatism" / Scene 14: "If you stay, you stay to watch a man slowly break his back between night shifts and deathbed vigils. I will not let you trade Martin’s demands for my despair."
- **Problem:** The wry, Dickensian narrator of the first half disappears once the plot turns, and the last scenes are played entirely straight in heightened speech. It reads as two writers rather than one voice that deepens.
- **Reader needed:** Some of the narrator's wryness kept in the ending, or some weight in the early comedy, so the two halves join up.

### 6. [minor] Dialogue used to deliver exposition
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 12, Scene 13
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "Hospital security regulations mandate that electronic basement doors deadbolt automatically at 07:00 and 19:00, and if that timer trips while you're still tying your laces"
- **Problem:** Henderson recites a rule the reader needs for later, not one Arthur needs to hear. Kieran lays out the whole prank for the reader in Scene 12 ("Three pints of stout at the Black Dog that says I could bait…"). In Scene 13 Martin restates the deadline, the buyer and the sum from Scene 3.
- **Reader needed:** Plot information that comes out of conflict, not announced as if to an audience.

### 7. [minor] Decisions and endings are summarised for the reader
- **Where:** Scene 13, Scene 15 (also Scene 4, Scene 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 15: "The secret letters were finished, but as Julian met his gaze with a faint, hopeful smile, Arthur knew the work had only just begun."
- **Problem:** The last line states the theme outright. Scene 13 spells out Arthur's change ("the illusion crumbled. Leeds would not be freedom."). Scene 4 declares "It was sincere; of that he had no doubt whatever." Scene 6 explains Julian's choice in paragraph-long reasoning before he acts.
- **Reader needed:** Let the shared cup and the notebook on the shelf close the story without a narrator's gloss.

### 8. [nit] Garbled or redundant sentences
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 6, 7, 14, 15
- **Quote:** Scene 15: "a battered tin thermos wrapped in peeling tartan tinware… clattering like loose spoons in a tin drawer as he loosened the cork."
- **Problem:** Tea does not clatter, and the cap was already unscrewed. The "loose spoons in a tin drawer" simile is also recycled from Scene 4. Other examples: "push a laundry cart past the laundry chutes" (Scene 1), "a clean, cloth-bound notebook bound in dark blue linen" (Scene 7), "the ash shovel's smoothed ash handle" (Scene 14), and the envelope that is "faithfully concealing the brown paper envelope resting deep in the toe of Arthur's work boots" (Scene 6, where the boots conceal the envelope).
- **Reader needed:** A line edit, so the tender final scene isn't undercut by nonsense images.

## What works
- The opening voice is confident and funny: "Arthur Finch had reduced it to four greasy twenties and an elastic band that had given up on snapping back."
- Sharp social observation in the narrator's aside: "a delicate slip of cream cardstock—the sort prudent bachelors admire through stationers' windows but rarely buy".
- Arthur's plainest line is his best: "If your days are ash and your evenings are an infirmary cot, then that is where we begin."

## Out of lane
Arthur's accusation "Wrote to me under a dead woman’s pen name?" (Scene 12) refers to something never set up, which is a question for the plot-logic lens.
