# Prose Craft — Understudy

**Score: 2/5** — Line by line the sentences are competent and sometimes vivid, but the same scene-opening formula, body-language tics and re-told exposition keep coming back, and props and names change from scene to scene. The seams between separately written scenes pull the reader out again and again.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Nearly every scene opens with the same list of smells
- **Where:** Scene 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12, 15, 16, 17 (11 of 19 scenes)
- **Quote:** "smells unfailingly of boiled milk, damp baize, and the sulphurous coal dust" (Scene 3); "smells of boiled cabbage, wet macintoshes, and the sulfurous coal grit" (Scene 4); "smells of damp horsehair, floor wax, and the stale river mist" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Scene after scene opens with "[Place] smells of A, B, and C." The same items keep coming back: "damp distemper" appears in both Scene 9 and Scene 12, "greasepaint" leads the list in Scenes 2, 9, 16 and 17, and sulphurous coal shows up in back-to-back scenes. By the middle of the story a reader can predict each opening, and the formula makes every scene read like a fresh start.
- **Reader needed:** Scene openings that vary in what they reach for (action, dialogue, a single sensory detail), so that setting feels like part of an ongoing story rather than a template filled in again.

### 2. [major] The same objects and names change between scenes
- **Where:** Scenes 2, 6, 10, 11, 14, 15, 16
- **Quote:** "the annotated rehearsal ledger, its black velvet binding" (Scene 2) vs "the velvet rehearsal ledger bound in plum cloth" (Scene 6) vs "worn smooth at the gilt corners" (Scene 16); "heavy crimson skirts" (Scene 11) vs "the heavy green silk of Lady Corisande's coronation gown" (Scene 14)
- **Problem:** Each scene describes the props from scratch, so their details drift. The ledger is black, then plum, then gilt-cornered. The gown is a "green velvet robe" in Scene 10, crimson in Scene 11 and green silk in Scene 14. The play is *The Last Corisande* in Scene 4 and *The Glass Scythe* in Scene 10. Helen becomes "Mrs. Vane" in Scene 10, and the wig of "coiled dark hair" becomes "synthetic tresses" a few paragraphs later in Scene 15. An attentive reader stops to check whether these are different objects.
- **Reader needed:** One fixed description per recurring prop and name, carried the same way through every scene.

### 3. [minor] Characters and places are re-introduced as if the reader has not met them
- **Where:** Scenes 6, 8, 12, 15, 16, 19
- **Quote:** "Maren Faulkner—her wardrobe supervisor through five damp touring seasons" (Scene 6); "Five seasons of touring had worn the surprise out of Maren" (Scene 12); "For five touring seasons she has swallowed every reprimand" (Scene 15)
- **Problem:** Maren's five seasons are mentioned in Scenes 2, 6, 12 and 15. Toby is re-introduced as "the young juvenile who had shrunk beneath her scolding" (Scene 8). Julian's past is repeated: "went into receivership eight winters ago" (Scene 4) becomes "steered two touring companies into liquidation eight winters ago" (Scene 19). The Dunford green room gets its full furniture list again in Scene 16 ("a sagging horsehair settle, a low deal table, and the cold iron snout of an unlit two-ring gas burner"), matching Scene 3 almost item for item. Every one of these shows where one scene ends and a new one was written.
- **Reader needed:** Trust that the reader remembers who Maren, Toby and Julian are and what the green room looks like, and carry them forward without repeating the introduction.

### 4. [minor] The same exposition is spoken again, sometimes in nearly the same words
- **Where:** Scene 7, Scene 15, Scene 16; Scene 4 and Scene 10; Scenes 4, 15, 16, 19
- **Quote:** "the appointed understudy must perform two full solo matinees before the final clearance cheque is released" (Scene 4) vs "The regional grant requires two full solo matinees from the alternate before a single penny of the winter subsidy clears" (Scene 10)
- **Problem:** The story of Kenneth's cut scene and the lost pension is told three times in full: by Arthur (Scene 7), by Maren (Scene 15, "You forced Arthur Penn out of his acting scenes eleven years ago"), and by Helen in both narration and dialogue (Scene 16, "Twenty-four weeks"). Julian explains the grant rule twice. "A magnificent run" is said by Julian (Scene 4) and Arthur (Scene 16), "A magnificent one" by Maren (Scene 15), and "A magnificent evening" by Reese (Scene 19), so a phrase that could have been a deliberate echo just reads as repetition. The dialogue drifts into "as you know" speeches.
- **Reader needed:** Each piece of backstory told once, fully, and later scenes that refer back to it briefly instead of repeating it.

### 5. [minor] Stock body-language tics
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 6, 8, 9, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19
- **Quote:** "his knuckles whitening" (Scene 8); "knuckles whitening as air refuses to pass" (Scene 9); "Helen's knuckles whiten around the handle of the brush" (Scene 15); "his knuckles whitening" (Scene 18)
- **Problem:** Whitening knuckles show up six times (Scenes 8, 9, 14, 15, 16, 18). Tightening or setting jaws show up four times (Scene 1 "his jaw sets", Scene 6, Scene 13, Scene 19 "Julian's jaw tightens"). "Murmurs softly" and "murmurs quietly" appear four times (Scenes 14, 15, 16, 19), and both adverbs repeat what "murmur" already says. Every character tenses in the same way.
- **Reader needed:** Physical reactions specific to each character, like Arthur's swollen wrist in Scene 1, instead of shared defaults.

### 6. [minor] Repeated "does not X" and "not X; it is Y" constructions, plus a copied decision beat
- **Where:** Scenes 3, 5, 9, 11, 13, 15, 16 (and "Helen does not…" in Scenes 3, 4, 5, 6, 11, 12, 15)
- **Quote:** "It is not an ordinary cough; it is the shuddering recoil" (Scene 9); "the silence rising from the darkness below is not cowed terror; it is grief" (Scene 11); "the spasm is not the dry paralysis …; it is the raw ache of humiliation" (Scene 16)
- **Problem:** The "not X; it is Y" pivot also drives Scene 3 ("not the victory … but the dry, baffling frustration"), Scene 5 ("does not fracture; it tightens") and Scene 11 ("not the fury of an outraged queen, but"). "Helen does not sit" appears in both Scene 3 and Scene 15. Clara's turn in Scene 13 ("the girl weighs her silence … and then, with a sharp exhale, she makes her choice") is repeated almost exactly for Maren in Scene 15 ("as she measures the cost of speaking … and she makes her choice").
- **Reader needed:** Fewer defining-by-negation sentences, and turning points that each happen in their own way rather than following one template.

### 7. [minor] Scenes end with the narrator stating the emotional conclusion
- **Where:** Scene 11, Scene 13, Scene 16, Scene 19 (also Scene 8)
- **Quote:** "the horrifying truth settles over her like frost: Clara has not ruined the role. She has made it human, and in doing so, has made Helen entirely obsolete." (Scene 11)
- **Problem:** The scenes already dramatise these moments (the silent house, Clara leaving with the book, the ledger handed over), and then the narration spells out what they mean. Examples: "paralyzed by the clean, terrible weight of a grace she had done nothing to deserve" (Scene 13); Arthur's line is followed by a tag explaining he sees her "as an equal who has finally stepped out of her own shadow" (Scene 16); "feeling the heavy burden of forty years slip from her shoulders" (Scene 19).
- **Reader needed:** Stop on the dramatised image or gesture and let the reader draw the conclusion.

### 8. [nit] Tense slips and grammar errors
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 3, Scene 19
- **Quote:** "a small, polite bow of deference that conceded nothing at all" (Scene 3); "Reese murmur softly, bowing his head" (Scene 19)
- **Problem:** "Conceded" is past tense inside present-tense narration. "Reese murmur" is missing its verb ending. "An habitual nod" (Scene 1) is an archaic article choice that reads like an error.
- **Reader needed:** Clean present tense and correct agreement throughout.

## What works
- The bell-pull mechanism is set up in precise, physical prose and pays off in the same rhythm at the climax: "One, two, three seconds. A dull, mechanical stutter." (Scene 1), echoed by "Helen's arm draws back, taking the slack from the cable. Twenty-six. Twenty-seven. / She pulls." (Scene 18). This is the best sustained piece of writing in the story.
- Short lines placed after long build-ups land well: "Clara does none of these things." (Scene 5) and Arthur's one-word refusal, "No," he says. (Scene 7).
- Some images are sharp and exact: "the dry, baffling frustration of a blade striking sand" (Scene 3); "she inspects it as one might inspect a chipped cup in an otherwise complete tea service" (Scene 1).

## Out of lane
Helen learns about Clara's September refusal from Clara herself in Scene 13, but in Scene 17 she says "Arthur told me what you did in September."
