# Grounding and Detail — Understudy

**Score: 3/5** — Every room is vividly furnished and most scenes open with a clear place and POV, but the tour's route and calendar never come together, and at least one load-bearing event (the "Dunford showcase" that earned Clara her notices) happens offstage and is only named, so the reader has to reconstruct the plot's timeline.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The tour's route and calendar never add up
- **Where:** Scene 6 (and Scenes 10, 14)
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "You have postered the promenade with an understudy. The Dunford run does not conclude until Saturday." Scene 10: "That was the stipulation, and today is the first." Scene 14: "The final week brings them back beneath the three gilded tiers of the King's Theatre in Dunford"
- **Problem:** We jump from Dunford to Redcar with no transition, and there the Dunford run is still said to be going on. We then jump to Burnley for "the first" matinee, although Scene 4 said the matinees were "Thursday and Saturday afternoon". Then we are back in Dunford for a "final week". No scene says what day it is or how far we have travelled, so the reader cannot tell whether the company is on a circuit, on a round trip, or in two towns at once.
- **Reader needed:** A one-line time-and-place cue when each scene changes town (for example "Wednesday, Redcar" or "the following week"), so the move from Dunford to Redcar to Burnley and back reads as a route.

### 2. [major] Clara's reviewed "showcase" happens offstage and is never set up
- **Where:** Scene 10 (setup expected in Scenes 5–9)
- **Quote:** "The notices from the Dunford showcase arrived on the morning post," Julian says … "The *Manchester Guardian* calls her portrayal nuanced, immediate, and emotionally resonant."
- **Problem:** The only time we saw Clara play Corisande before this is the empty-house blocking rehearsal in Scene 5. No public performance by Clara in Dunford is shown or mentioned. Yet these reviews are what Julian uses to take Helen off the bill, so the reader has to invent a performance to explain them.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier mention that Clara played a showcase or performance in Dunford that critics attended, so the reviews feel like something that happened and not just something Julian says.

### 3. [minor] Scene 16's time of day points to late night, but it is just before the show
- **Where:** Scene 16
- **Quote:** "smelling of greasepaint, damp brickwork, and the stale tobacco of stagehands long gone home" … "the old theater's floorboards creak above them with the departing steps of the cleaners" … "The girl is about to take her beginner's call"
- **Problem:** Scene 14 put us two hours before the house opens. The start of Scene 16 then tells us the building has emptied for the night, and its last line puts us back just before the show. The reader has to reread to work out when the dagger theft happens.
- **Reader needed:** One clear cue that this is the hour before curtain on closing night, with the building full of people getting ready.

### 4. [minor] The locked-away ledger turns up on the Burnley stage with no explanation
- **Where:** Scene 13 (set up in Scene 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "Helen closes the cedar lid, turns the small brass tumbler until the internal bolt drops home … and slips the trunk key into the silk lining of her bodice." Scene 13: "On the table between them rests the plum rehearsal ledger and the silver-hilted dagger"
- **Problem:** Scene 6 makes a point of Helen locking the ledger away and keeping the key on her body. Two towns later it is lying on the prop table, and nothing says how it got out.
- **Reader needed:** A line saying Helen unlocked the trunk and handed the ledger over, or that the company took it.

### 5. [minor] Events referred to as if the reader already knows them
- **Where:** Scene 17 (and Scene 19)
- **Quote:** Scene 17: "the very cues that had saved Helen's final tour from ruin when her voice had faltered in the damp October chill". Scene 19: "Lowe tells me the local gazette had reporters in the stalls"
- **Problem:** We never saw Clara's cues rescue a performance when Helen's voice failed, and the story is set in November. "Lowe" appears several paragraphs before Reese Lowe is introduced as the theatre manager. (Julian is similarly first-named in Scene 2, "silk that Julian will never pay to replace", before his role is given in Scenes 3–4.)
- **Reader needed:** Either a brief earlier scene where Clara's cues saved a performance or no mention of it, and each new name introduced with its role at first mention.

### 6. [minor] What happened to Helen's third-act entrance?
- **Where:** Scene 8 into Scene 9
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "waiting for the fanfare that marks her third-act entrance". Scene 9: "Helen retreats to the dressing room, pulling the heavy door shut against the muffled roar of the curtain calls."
- **Problem:** Scene 8 leaves Helen in the wings, about to go on. Scene 9 opens after the show has ended. The reader cannot tell whether she played the rest of the show, and it matters because her voice gives out in Scene 9.
- **Reader needed:** A clause saying she got through the performance, or how it went, before the coughing fit.

### 7. [minor] The Kenneth backstory is told again and again
- **Where:** Scene 16 (also Scene 15)
- **Quote:** Scene 16: "She remembers standing in the manager's office in Newcastle, demanding sole top billing … She had cut forty lines from his part … twenty-four weeks short of his full union pension credits."
- **Problem:** Arthur tells this in full in Scene 7. Maren repeats it in Scene 15. In Scene 16 Helen remembers it in full and then apologises using the same details ("Twenty-four weeks"). The memory paragraph slows the scene's key confrontation to repeat facts the reader already has.
- **Reader needed:** Nothing new here. The apology alone would carry the moment.

## What works
- The bell-pull mechanics are set up in plain detail in Scene 1 and pay off exactly in Scene 18, so the ending needs no explanation: "the hammer strikes three seconds after the pull. The delay is in the house, not the crew."
- The ring's loose catch is explained concretely in Scene 2 ("If you don't seat the pin inward toward the flesh of the palm … it springs wide"), so the snag in Scene 14 and the fitting in Scene 17 read as shown, not claimed.
- Scene openings reliably place the reader through smell and room, e.g. Scene 4: "The lounge of the Station Hotel smells of boiled cabbage, wet macintoshes, and the sulfurous coal grit that drifts in whenever the shunting engines brake outside the Dunford platform."

## Out of lane
Consistency: in Scene 17 Helen says "Arthur told me what you did in September", but Clara told her herself in Scene 13, and the play is called *The Last Corisande* in Scene 4 but *The Glass Scythe* in Scene 10.
