# Logical Consistency — Understudy

**Score: 2/5** — several major contradictions (props locked in a trunk that reappear elsewhere, a revelation credited to the wrong person, a bargain the plot has already broken, a run-and-venue timeline that cannot be squared, and the play changes its title), plus drifting colours and numbers, add up to a reader who stops trusting the details.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Helen credits Arthur with a revelation she heard from Clara
- **Where:** Scene 17 (and Scene 13)
- **Quote:** Scene 17: "“Arthur told me what you did in September. Refusing his contract until he guaranteed I would finish the regional dates.”" / Scene 13: "\"Julian offered me the full contract in September,\" Clara says... \"I told him no,\""
- **Problem:** Clara tells Helen this herself, face to face, in Scene 13. Arthur never mentions it in Scene 16, which is about the Kenneth cut and the dagger. So in Scene 17 Helen names the wrong source, and she says it to Clara, the one person who knows where Helen really heard it.
- **Reader needed:** Helen should credit Clara, or the reader needs to see Arthur telling her.

### 2. [major] Clara's "unhindered farewell tour" bargain contradicts what has already happened
- **Where:** Scene 13 (against Scenes 4 and 10; echoed in Scene 17)
- **Quote:** Scene 13: "I told him I would not speak a single line of Corisande unless you had your farewell tour. Unhindered. Every house, every curtain call you were promised, from Dunford to the end." / Scene 10: "\"Strike Mrs. Vane from the afternoon call. Put Clara Oaks in as Lady Corisande.\""
- **Problem:** By this point Julian has already handed Helen a termination (Scene 4), put Clara on the posters (Scene 6), struck Helen from a performance, and Clara has played the matinee that same afternoon. The deal Clara describes has plainly been broken, yet she presents it as if it held. No one in the story notices, and Scene 17 repeats it as though Helen did "finish the regional dates."
- **Reader needed:** Either an acknowledgement that Julian broke the deal, or a deal that fits what the reader has seen happen.

### 3. [major] The dagger and ledger escape a locked trunk
- **Where:** Scene 6 (then Scenes 12, 13, 15)
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "she lifts the silver dagger prop, laying it flat across the felt trays, and beside it places her personal prompt, the velvet rehearsal ledger... slips the trunk key into the silk lining of her bodice." / Scene 13: "On the table between them rests the plum rehearsal ledger and the silver-hilted dagger"
- **Problem:** In Redcar, Helen locks both objects in her trunk and keeps the only key on her body. Two scenes later, in Burnley, the dagger is on Clara's make-up table (Scene 12) and then both objects sit on the prop table (Scene 13). Nothing says how they got out, and they never go back in. (Scene 1 also leaves the dagger with Arthur for cleaning, so it has no settled keeper.)
- **Reader needed:** A line showing the trunk was opened or the props handed over, or the lock should not be made so final.

### 4. [major] The Dunford run, the tour route and the matinee dates do not fit together
- **Where:** Scene 6, Scene 10, Scene 14 (against Scene 4)
- **Quote:** Scene 6 (in Redcar): "\"The Dunford run does not conclude until Saturday.\"" / Scene 10 (in Burnley): "\"The notices from the Dunford showcase arrived on the morning post... today is the first.\"" / Scene 14: "The final week brings them back beneath the three gilded tiers of the King's Theatre in Dunford"
- **Problem:** Scene 4 sets Clara's two matinees for Thursday and Saturday, after which she takes over the role. The story then says the Dunford run is still going while the company plays Redcar and Burnley. Reviews of Clara's "Dunford showcase" reach Julian before her *first* matinee, and the company later goes back to Dunford for a closing week. Scene 17 also says her voice "faltered in the damp October chill", but the story is set in November (Scenes 3 and 4).
- **Reader needed:** One clear order of venues and days, with Clara's reviews arriving only after she has actually performed.

### 5. [major] The play changes its title
- **Where:** Scene 4 and Scene 10
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "the third act of *The Last Corisande* together across eleven seasons" / Scene 10: "\"Audiences do not pay for tea-table naturalism in *The Glass Scythe*.\""
- **Problem:** The production Helen has headlined for eleven years has two different names, both used as its title. The character also shifts between "an unmarried viscountess in the Scottish highlands" (Scene 4) and "an outraged queen" with a "coronation" (Scenes 11 and 14).
- **Reader needed:** One title and one rank for Corisande.

### 6. [minor] The colours of the costume and the ledger keep changing
- **Where:** Scenes 2, 6, 10, 11, 13, 14, 16
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "its black velvet binding worn bald at the spine and corner-tapped with fraying cloth tape" / Scene 6: "the velvet rehearsal ledger bound in plum cloth" / Scene 11: "the heavy crimson skirts gathered in soft folds" / Scene 14: "the heavy green silk of Lady Corisande’s coronation gown"
- **Problem:** The ledger goes from black to plum and back to "black velvet ledger... worn smooth at the gilt corners" (Scene 16). The Corisande gown is "green velvet" (Scene 10), "crimson" (Scene 11), "velvet" (Scene 13) and "green silk" (Scene 14). The ring is a "heavy silver band" (Scene 2) and then a "pinchbeck mount" in the same paragraph. The trunk is "iron-banded", then has a "cedar lid" (Scene 6).
- **Reader needed:** Each named object should keep one colour and material.

### 7. [minor] Arthur's years of service and Julian's past failure drift
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 5, 10 (and Scenes 4 and 19)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "twenty seasons in drafty provincial wings" / Scene 5: "the dull, long-suffering vigilance that forty touring seasons have carved into his face" / Scene 4: "his first West End revival went into receivership eight winters ago" / Scene 19: "steered two touring companies into liquidation eight winters ago"
- **Problem:** Arthur's career is twenty seasons, then forty, then "twenty years" (Scene 10). Julian's failure eight winters ago is a single West End revival in Scene 4 and two touring companies in Scene 19.
- **Reader needed:** The same number, and the same event, each time it comes up.

### 8. [minor] Where the ring tore the gown, and where everyone stood, shifts
- **Where:** Scene 14 and Scene 15
- **Quote:** Scene 14: "the ring’s faulty silver safety catch snags viciously into the heavy silk of her forearm sleeve" / Scene 15: "You saw her fumble the dagger and hook the ring directly into the train—" / "She nearly ripped the entire side panel away."
- **Problem:** The tear moves from sleeve and cuff to "the torn hem", "the train" and "the side panel". Helen says Maren stood "three feet away", but Maren came "from the opposite wing". Maren says Helen stood "ten inches behind her in the wings", but Clara was downstage centre and Helen was watching from the shadows. Helen's anger might explain her exaggerating, but Maren's own words and the narration drift too.
- **Reader needed:** One location for the tear and one set of positions.

## What works
- The bell's three-second delay is set up exactly and pays off exactly: Scene 1 "the hammer strikes three seconds after the pull", then Scene 18 "Twenty-six. Twenty-seven. She pulls." so the cloth lands "on the exact mark of thirty."
- The Kenneth backstory holds its key figure. Scene 7 "I had twenty-four weeks left on the boards to vest my union acting pension" matches Scene 16 "Twenty-four weeks. I knew it then", and "eleven years" stays fixed throughout.
- The ring's faulty catch is kept consistent: "The safety catch has been loose since Newcastle" (Scene 15) matches "slack, as it has been since Newcastle" (Scene 17). The Scene 2 warning comes true in Scene 14.

## Out of lane
Scene 19's line "Reese murmur softly" is a grammar slip for the line editor.
