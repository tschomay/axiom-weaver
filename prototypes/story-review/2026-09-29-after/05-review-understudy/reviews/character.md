# Character Coherence — Understudy

**Score: 3/5** — Most people are distinct and act from visible wants. But the choice the turn depends on, Clara's September refusal, contradicts what Clara does on the page, and Helen goes back to open cruelty straight after her epiphany with no reason shown.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Clara's bargain contradicts what she does
- **Where:** Scene 13 (against Scenes 6, 10, 11; repeated in Scene 17)
- **Quote:** "I told him I would not speak a single line of Corisande unless you had your farewell tour. Unhindered. Every house, every curtain call you were promised, from Dunford to the end." (Scene 13), against "Strike Mrs. Vane from the afternoon call. Put Clara Oaks in as Lady Corisande." (Scene 10)
- **Problem:** Clara says she threatened Julian so that Helen could finish every house. Before she says it, she has already let herself be billed over Helen in Redcar and has played the Burnley matinee while Helen was barred from the wings. She never objects, and she doesn't mention the broken promise when she tells Helen. In Scene 17 Helen thanks her for "Refusing his contract until he guaranteed I would finish the regional dates," as if the guarantee had held. The act of grace that turns Helen around is contradicted by Clara's own behaviour, so it reads as something the plot needs rather than something Clara did.
- **Reader needed:** Either some sign that Clara was overruled and paid a price for it, or a version of the bargain that fits what she has actually done.

### 2. [major] Helen's epiphanies don't stick, and her relapse has no reason
- **Where:** Scenes 11–13, then Scene 14 (and Scene 16)
- **Quote:** "unable to move, unable to speak, paralyzed by the clean, terrible weight of a grace she had done nothing to deserve." (Scene 13), then "'Look at her!' Helen screams… 'She lacks the technical precision to wear the emerald ring!'" (Scene 14)
- **Problem:** Scenes 11, 12 and 13 each end with Helen shaken by the narrator's summary: Clara "has made it human," "Every page is an altar," "grace." Scene 14 then opens with her screaming at Clara in front of the company, and Scene 16 has her hiding the prop. Nothing on the page links the two states. There's no denial or rationalising, and no new provocation. Her arc reads as a series of announced realisations that reset, and then one sudden turn in Scene 16.
- **Reader needed:** One visible beat between Scenes 13 and 14 showing why Helen pushes back harder after being given grace, such as a new wound, a spoken rationalisation, or panic.

### 3. [minor] What Helen knew about Arthur's pension shifts to suit the scene
- **Where:** Scene 7 and Scene 16
- **Quote:** "She had carried the memory of that cut for a decade as a triumph of economy… To see it stripped to petty bookkeeping and a ruined old man's lost stipend leaves her feeling suddenly, horribly exposed" (Scene 7), against "You lost your pension because of my vanity. Twenty-four weeks. I knew it then, and I did it anyway." (Scene 16)
- **Problem:** In Scene 7 the cost is news to Helen and shocks her. In Scene 16 she says she knew all along. The confession needs her to have been knowingly guilty; the earlier scene needed her to be ignorant. She can't be both.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent account of what Helen understood at the time, so the confession reads as honesty rather than a new fact.

### 4. [minor] Arthur forgives her, but his memory is then used against her
- **Where:** Scene 16 and Scene 19
- **Quote:** "Then, with a soft exhalation that sounds almost like forgiveness, he reaches out." (Scene 16), against "Arthur Penn has a long memory. If word travels south that you were found by the property hampers before curtain with the silver dagger in your fist" (Scene 19)
- **Problem:** Julian's threat implies that Arthur passed the dagger incident on to him, hours after Arthur quietly forgave Helen and sent her to the wings. That undoes the dignity of the green-room scene, and nothing about Arthur explains it.
- **Reader needed:** Some sign of how Julian learned about the dagger that doesn't make Arthur a quiet informer, or a reason for Arthur to have told him.

### 5. [minor] Julian exists only as the voice of money
- **Where:** Scenes 4, 6, 10, 19
- **Quote:** "It is commercial necessity, Helen. Nothing more personal than the price of paraffin." (Scene 6)
- **Problem:** Every Julian line is budgets, subsidy terms or percentages, and he never wants or fears anything beyond the ledger. His last-scene offer of three hundred pounds, from a company "bleeding four hundred pounds a week," is only there to give Helen something to refuse. He works as an obstacle but has no interior life.
- **Reader needed:** One moment where Julian wants something for himself (pride, fear of the syndicate, an old grudge against Helen) so that his closing bribe comes from him.

### 6. [minor] Toby only reacts and has no inner life of his own
- **Where:** Scene 8 (also Scenes 1, 3, 11, 18)
- **Quote:** "the young juvenile who had shrunk beneath her scolding during the Dunford run-throughs" (Scene 8)
- **Problem:** Every time Toby appears, he flinches, freezes, blanches or stares in awe. He is a gauge that shows how Helen or Clara affects the room. The scolding the line refers to was aimed at Clara, not Toby, so even his fear is borrowed.
- **Reader needed:** One thing Toby wants or does for himself, so he is a person rather than a mood indicator.

### 7. [nit] Maren's big speech repeats Arthur's
- **Where:** Scene 15 (against Scene 7)
- **Quote:** "You forced Arthur Penn out of his acting scenes eleven years ago so you could have sole billing above the title" (Scene 15)
- **Problem:** Maren's outburst mostly retells Arthur's grievance in his own terms. Her own complaint, five seasons of swallowed reprimands, is only summarised by the narrator and never voiced, so her turn loses its own weight.
- **Reader needed:** Maren's accusation built from her own experience of Helen rather than from Arthur's.

## What works
- Helen's voice at the start is sharp and her own, and the ending pays it off. "Pain is merely a lack of devotion to the tempo." (Scene 1) comes back when she pulls the bell early for Clara in Scene 18: "Helen's arm draws back, taking the slack from the cable. Twenty-six. Twenty-seven."
- Arthur's refusal is earned by its specific grievance: "I had twenty-four weeks left on the boards to vest my union acting pension. Twenty-four weeks, Helen." (Scene 7)
- Clara's calm under attack is shown through what she does, not just named: "her chin tilts upward into the line of the blade rather than cowering away from it." (Scene 5)

## Out of lane
Helen says "Arthur told me what you did in September" (Scene 17), but Clara told her herself in Scene 13; that's a continuity slip for the consistency reviewer.
