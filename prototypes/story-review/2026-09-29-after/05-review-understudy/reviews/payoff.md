# Setup and Payoff — Understudy

**Score: 3/5** — The object setups pay off well (the bell delay, the loose ring catch, Arthur's lost scene), but the second big reveal, Clara's September refusal, was never planted, the events the reader has already seen argue against it, and a later scene then says Arthur revealed it instead of Clara.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Clara's September bargain is unplanted, and earlier events argue against it
- **Where:** Scene 13 (against Scene 4 and Scene 10)
- **Quote:** "I told him I would not speak a single line of Corisande unless you had your farewell tour. Unhindered. Every house, every curtain call you were promised, from Dunford to the end." (Scene 13)
- **Earlier:** "But this is the end of the tour for you." (Scene 4); "Arthur. Strike Mrs. Vane from the afternoon call. Put Clara Oaks in as Lady Corisande." (Scene 10)
- **Problem:** Before Scene 13 nothing hints at a deal between Clara and Julian. The reader has also watched Julian fire Helen partway through the tour and bar her from the stage. So the reveal that Clara won Helen an "unhindered" farewell tour contradicts what the reader saw, and it is never reconciled. A reader will stop and ask what Clara's threat actually got Helen.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier sign of friction or a deal between Clara and Julian, and some acknowledgment of how the promised "every house" fits with Scenes 4 and 10.

### 2. [major] The same reveal is credited to Arthur, as if Scene 13 never happened
- **Where:** Scene 17 (against Scene 13)
- **Quote:** "Arthur told me what you did in September. Refusing his contract until he guaranteed I would finish the regional dates." (Scene 17)
- **Earlier:** "Julian offered me the full contract in September," Clara says … "I told him no," (Scene 13)
- **Problem:** Clara told Helen herself, face to face, two scenes earlier. Nothing shown in the Scene 16 talk with Arthur covers it. The thank-you in Scene 17 rests on a revelation the story seems to have forgotten it already made.
- **Reader needed:** Helen answering what Clara told her in Scene 13, not a second telling of the same news from a new source.

### 3. [minor] The Arthur backstory is told three times, and the third contradicts the first
- **Where:** Scene 7, Scene 15, Scene 16 (twice in Scene 16)
- **Quote:** "To see it stripped to petty bookkeeping and a ruined old man's lost stipend leaves her feeling suddenly, horribly exposed" (Scene 7) vs. "You lost your pension because of my vanity. Twenty-four weeks. I knew it then, and I did it anyway." (Scene 16)
- **Problem:** Scene 7 is a strong reveal. Maren restates it in Scene 15. Scene 16 then walks through it again, first as Helen's memory and then in her confession. The confession also says she knew about the pension all along, which empties out the shock Scene 7 was built on.
- **Reader needed:** The confession should build on the Scene 7 reveal (Helen finally owning something she learned there), not re-narrate it or rewrite what she knew.

### 4. [minor] Clara's help in October is stated as a payoff for an event never shown
- **Where:** Scene 17
- **Quote:** "the very cues that had saved Helen's final tour from ruin when her voice had faltered in the damp October chill."
- **Problem:** The narration treats Clara saving Helen's performances as an established fact. The reader never saw it: Helen's voice fails in Scene 9 and Helen smashes the help Clara offers. The connection is asserted, not earned.
- **Reader needed:** An on-page moment where Clara's notes or cues actually carried Helen through, or no claim that they did.

### 5. [minor] Julian's leverage relies on something he could not know, and it quietly undoes Arthur's forgiveness
- **Where:** Scene 19 (against Scene 16)
- **Quote:** "If word travels south that you were found by the property hampers before curtain with the silver dagger in your fist, neither of us gains anything." (Scene 19)
- **Problem:** In Scene 16 only Arthur saw Helen with the dagger, in the green room, and he forgave her. Julian's knowledge suggests Arthur reported it, which undercuts that scene, and the story never addresses this.
- **Reader needed:** Some way Julian learned of it that doesn't betray Arthur, or leave the incident out of Julian's threat.

### 6. [minor] Helen's damaged voice is simply declared healed
- **Where:** Scene 19 (setup in Scenes 9, 10, 14)
- **Quote:** "her voice clear and level, without the hoarseness that had choked her lines." (Scene 19)
- **Earlier:** "that jagged register that neither medicine nor silence has smoothed away" (Scene 14)
- **Problem:** The story gave the ruined voice a lot of weight: the syrup, the cough, the "failing vocal cords". In the last scene it recovers just because the narrator says so, as a stand-in for Helen's inner change.
- **Reader needed:** Either the voice stays hoarse while her manner changes, or the recovery is tied to something shown (the glycerine hint in Scene 17 could carry it).

### 7. [nit] Weighted props that go nowhere: the locked trunk and the dry-rot cornice
- **Where:** Scene 6; Scene 1
- **Quote:** "turns the small brass tumbler until the internal bolt drops home with a muted thud, and slips the trunk key into the silk lining of her bodice." (Scene 6); "The ceiling cornice has dry rot, Miss Vane. The damp in this town shakes it loose whenever the stalls stamp." (Scene 1)
- **Problem:** Helen locks the ledger away with ceremony, then it turns up on an open table in Scene 13 and the key is never mentioned again. The dry-rot cornice is set up to shake loose "whenever the stalls stamp", and the finale has "stamping feet" in the same theatre with no echo of it. The sealed severance envelope from Scene 4 is also never opened or mentioned again.
- **Reader needed:** Either a small callback, or less weight on these details when they are introduced.

## What works
- The bell delay is a model plant: "the hammer strikes three seconds after the pull" and "Then you pull three seconds earlier" (Scene 1) pay off when Helen pulls at twenty-seven for Clara's thirty-second freeze: "Helen's arm draws back, taking the slack from the cable. Twenty-six. Twenty-seven. She pulls." (Scene 18). Here Helen does the job she once told Arthur to do.
- The loose ring catch runs the whole length of the story: set up in Scene 2 ("it springs wide. The prong sits upright"), paid off in the Scene 14 snag, turned back on Helen by Maren in Scene 15 ("you refused to let me take it into town to have the pin reset"), and resolved in Scene 17 ("Turn it flat against the skin. Like this.") and Scene 18 ("safely turned inward so no claw catches the weave").
- Some quieter echoes land without any comment from the narrator. Helen shutting Clara out of the footlights in Scene 1 is reversed when she "steps back into the shadows, smiling" (Scene 18). "Keep your hush money, Julian" (Scene 4) returns as "Keep your money, Julian" (Scene 19). Clara smelling "the glycerine on her breath" (Scene 17) quietly shows that Helen took the syrup after all.

## Out of lane
Consistency: the ledger's binding changes (black velvet in Scene 2, plum cloth in Scene 6), and "Crane's father" in Scene 16 conflicts with "Julian's predecessor" in Scene 7.
