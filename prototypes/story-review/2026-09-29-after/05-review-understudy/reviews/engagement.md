# Engagement and Believability — Understudy

**Score: 3/5** — The opening and the finale are genuinely gripping, with a setup and payoff that are earned. But the middle keeps humbling Helen at the same pitch, the central revelation doesn't fit what the reader has just watched happen, and the question of who plays Corisande is settled offstage. The story is readable but slack in the places that should carry it.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Clara's September bargain doesn't square with events the reader has already seen
- **Where:** Scene 13 (against Scene 4, Scene 10, Scene 11; echoed in Scene 17)
- **Quote:** "I told him I would not speak a single line of Corisande unless you had your farewell tour. Unhindered. Every house, every curtain call you were promised, from Dunford to the end." (Scene 13)
- **Problem:** Clara says this minutes after playing the Burnley matinee in Helen's role. That same afternoon Julian barred Helen from the wings ("Strike Mrs. Vane from the afternoon call", Scene 10), and he had handed her a "Termination of Principal Services" (Scene 4). The farewell tour Clara claims to have secured plainly did not happen. So the reveal meant to reframe the whole story reads as untrue or toothless, and Helen accepts it without once noticing the contradiction. Scene 17 makes it worse: it credits Clara's notes with cues "that had saved Helen's final tour from ruin when her voice had faltered in the damp October chill", which the reader never sees, and it has Helen say "Arthur told me what you did in September", although Clara told her herself.
- **Reader needed:** Either Clara's bargain has to visibly hold (or visibly fail, and she admits it), or the reveal has to account for the replacement the reader has just watched.

### 2. [major] Helen's breakdown is re-staged five times at the same pitch, so the final turn feels like attrition, not a choice
- **Where:** Scene 8, Scene 11, Scene 13, Scene 15, Scene 16 (also Scene 12)
- **Quote:** "paralyzed by the clean, terrible weight of a grace she had done nothing to deserve." (Scene 13), then "It has made you entirely alone, Helen." (Scene 15)
- **Problem:** Each of these scenes ends with Helen stripped bare: alone in the wings, made "obsolete", undone by grace, told she is alone, caught red-handed. Scene 13's revelation should be the turn. Instead the next scene opens with her screaming at Clara, then trying to hide the dagger, with nothing to explain why the grace failed to land. Scene 16's confession also restates Arthur's pension story from Scene 7 almost word for word ("Twenty-four weeks"), so the climactic apology tells the reader nothing new. The pressure doesn't build. It resets.
- **Reader needed:** A reason Helen relapses after Scene 13, or fewer and steeper humiliations, so each one costs her more than the last.

### 3. [major] The story's central question is decided offstage
- **Where:** Scene 4, Scene 9, Scene 14, Scene 18
- **Quote:** "I will play Corisande until the final curtain falls in this wretched town" (Scene 4), and "I will play Corisande tomorrow, and Friday, and Saturday, full-throated, until the final curtain falls on this tour." (Scene 9)
- **Problem:** Helen twice vows to hold the role. After Scene 1 she never plays it again. By Scene 14 Clara is simply in the gown for the final Dunford week, and by Scene 18 she is playing closing night. The reader never sees the moment Helen loses (or gives up) the fight her vows promised.
- **Reader needed:** An on-page moment where Helen's claim to closing night is lost or surrendered, so that Scene 17's handing over of the ring is a sacrifice rather than a formality.

### 4. [minor] The ending replays Scene 4's refusal at lower stakes, and it quietly undercuts Arthur's forgiveness
- **Where:** Scene 19 (against Scene 4 and Scene 16)
- **Quote:** "Dunford has ears, and Arthur Penn has a long memory. If word travels south that you were found by the property hampers before curtain with the silver dagger in your fist" (Scene 19)
- **Problem:** "Keep your money, Julian" echoes "Keep your hush money, Julian" from Scene 4, so the final confrontation is a beat the reader has already had. The threat also implies that Arthur reported her to Julian straight after the tender forgiveness in Scene 16, which sours that scene, and the story never acknowledges this.
- **Reader needed:** A final exchange that tests something new in Helen, and some clarity on whether Arthur betrayed her confidence.

### 5. [minor] Convenient ambush: Arthur waiting in the dark
- **Where:** Scene 16
- **Quote:** "A brass desk lamp snaps into life atop the deal table, casting a harsh pool of amber across the oilcloth. Arthur Penn sits behind it"
- **Problem:** Just before curtain on closing night, the stage manager sits with the lamp off in an empty green room, exactly where Helen chooses to hide the prop. It works as a stage effect, but no reason is given for him being there, so it reads as authorial staging.
- **Reader needed:** One line on why Arthur is there, or why he suspected her.

### 6. [minor] The Scene 1–3 stretch establishes the same tyranny three times before the premise arrives
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 2, Scene 3
- **Quote:** "Helen feels the sting of it behind her eyes—not the victory of an acknowledged master, but the dry, baffling frustration of a blade striking sand." (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** Arthur, Maren and Clara each take a dressing-down in turn before Julian's notice in Scene 4. Each scene plants something useful (the bell delay, the ring catch, Clara's composure), but the reader waits three scenes to learn what Helen stands to lose.
- **Reader needed:** The threat of replacement sooner, or Scenes 2 and 3 compressed, so the stakes land while the tyranny is still fresh.

### 7. [nit] The ruined voice heals when the plot needs it to
- **Where:** Scene 9, Scene 14, Scene 19
- **Quote:** "her voice clear and level, without the hoarseness that had choked her lines." (Scene 19)
- **Problem:** Her damaged cords are treated as a serious decline in Scenes 9–10 and as "that jagged register that neither medicine nor silence has smoothed away" in Scene 14. Then they simply clear for the farewell line.
- **Reader needed:** Either a cost that lasts or a nod to why her voice has come back.

## What works
- The bell-delay setup and payoff is excellent and entirely Helen's doing. Scene 1: "Then you pull three seconds earlier. A mechanism has no will, Arthur, and neither should you." Scene 18: "Twenty-six. Twenty-seven. / She pulls." Her own cruel rule becomes her gift. Keep both halves intact.
- Arthur's reveal in Scene 7 reframes Helen's past with a hard, specific cost: "I had twenty-four weeks left on the boards to vest my union acting pension. Twenty-four weeks, Helen."
- The ring runs as a thread from Scene 2's warning to the snag in Scene 14 to the handover in Scene 17 ("Turn it flat against the skin. Like this."). It is a concrete object that carries the whole arc.

## Out of lane
Where the dagger is found, "property hampers" in Scene 19 versus the green-room settle in Scene 16, is a consistency slip.
