# Logical Consistency — The Borrowed Boat

**Score: 3/5** — one major contradiction about where Arthur spent his last months and who was with him, plus several minor slips (a sail that was cut down but hangs again, a bill of sale in two places, tools promised to David and then sold).

## Findings

### 1. [major] Where Arthur died, and how long Cleo nursed him, changes
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scenes 3, 5, 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "Arthur had spent his final winter coughing into a wool blanket by the dying hearth … she had spent three months washing his spoons." / Scene 3: "the months he spent berthed at Lowood while his health failed" … "You stayed with him in Lowood those last three weeks."
- **Problem:** Scene 1 reads as Cleo nursing him at home for three months. Scene 3 has him dying in Lowood, with Cleo there for three weeks. Scene 5 adds "Arthur wrote to me three weeks before he took to his bed", which suggests she wasn't with him at all. Meanwhile the Cormorant "rotted through three rainy winters" on the Skiff Haven slip (Scene 6), so he can't have been "berthed" in Lowood aboard her. The mystery hinges on where Arthur was, so the reader can't settle it.
- **Reader needed:** One account of where Arthur spent his last months and how long Cleo was with him, used the same way by both sisters.

### 2. [minor] The mainsail was cut down, but later hangs from the spar
- **Where:** Scene 2 (and Scene 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "The line parted with a sound like a rifle crack. The gaff plunged down. The heavy canvas collapsed into a sodden heap across the cabin roof" / Scene 5: "The sail hung limp from the pine spar, useless now that the wind had died behind the headland."
- **Problem:** The halyard was severed and nobody re-rigged it, so the sail can't be hanging from the spar, or have been usable until the wind died.
- **Reader needed:** The sail should still be down, or someone should be shown re-rigging it.

### 3. [minor] The bill of sale is in two places
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scenes 3, 4, 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "reached into her pocket and touched the folded corner of the bill of sale" / Scene 5: "He produced the conditional bill of sale from his folder, drew a thick stroke of red ink across the signature line, and handed the defaced sheet to Ruth."
- **Problem:** Ruth has carried the only contract we've seen since Scene 1 ("slid it into her oilskin sleeve"). Scene 2 also puts "the buyer's packet" down in the cabin. Then the surveyor pulls the bill of sale out of his own folder and hands it to her, as if she didn't already have it.
- **Reader needed:** Show that the surveyor has a second copy, or have him take the copy from Ruth.

### 4. [minor] Arthur's tools are left to David, then sold for the slip fees
- **Where:** Scene 5 (and Scene 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "He wanted her brought here, to David. The boat, the tools, and what was left of him." / Scene 6: "Keep the anchor, Toby. Sell the tools to pay the slip fees."
- **Problem:** Cleo, who carried out Arthur's wishes, gives away the tools he meant for David, and nobody remarks on it.
- **Reader needed:** Make clear that these are different tools (the Skiff Haven bench versus the tool roll aboard), or have someone acknowledge the reversal.

### 5. [minor] When they get back to Skiff Haven doesn't fit
- **Where:** Scene 4 (and Scene 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "the evening mists pooling white over mudflats" / Scene 6: "It was the same sour basin they had fled at daybreak" … "In the dim afternoon light" … "'Back already,' Toby said."
- **Problem:** They reach Lowood in the evening, yet arrive back at Skiff Haven by carrier in the afternoon. That has to be at least the next day, but "fled at daybreak" and "Back already" read like the same day.
- **Reader needed:** A marker that a night has passed.

### 6. [minor] Ruth's backstory conflicts with her years in the city
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "Two years in the city had made her sharp-featured" … "before you took the train from the capital" / Scene 5: "She had starved through three winters to keep the council from the cottage"
- **Problem:** Ruth has been in the capital for two years, yet she spent three winters holding on to the Skiff Haven cottage.
- **Reader needed:** Numbers or places that fit together, or a hint that she kept the cottage while living away.

### 7. [nit] Buried or cremated, and what the keel is made of
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scenes 4, 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "Arthur went into the ground owing Skiff Haven three full seasons" / Scene 6: "a pair of girls who couldn't keep a pine keel off the granite"
- **Problem:** Toby says Arthur "went into the ground", but he was cremated and the sisters are carrying his ashes. Toby calls the keel pine, while Scenes 4 and 5 twice call it oak. Both lines are dialogue and may just be loose talk.
- **Reader needed:** Nothing essential. A careful editor would align them.

### 8. [nit] David's knowledge of the boat is out of date
- **Where:** Scene 3 (and Scene 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "'She's fouled with driftweed and the shaft is gone,' David said"
- **Problem:** Cleo cleared the driftweed in Scene 3, and the surveyor's inspection makes no mention of it.
- **Reader needed:** Drop the driftweed, or give David a reason to think it is still there.

## What works
- The anchor thread holds exactly across the whole story, from the loss to the kedge failing to Toby still holding it: "Fenn had taken their sixty-pound storm anchor … this light kedging hook was all Toby had left them" (Scene 4) → "He had propped Arthur's forfeited storm anchor—sixty pounds of pitted iron—against a piling" (Scene 6).
- The contract clause set up in Scene 1 is paid off precisely: "If the motor fails, or if the drive shaft bends … the buyer keeps the deposit" → "The purchase is void … The deposit returns to the timber syndicate".
- The debt stays the same throughout: "Twelve pounds four" in Scene 1 and again twice in Scene 6.

## Out of lane
Ruth, who froze at the helm in Scene 2 and quarrelled with Cleo in Scenes 3 and 4, walks off side by side with her in Scene 6. That is a character-arc question for another reviewer.
