# Character Coherence — The Borrowed Boat

**Score: 3/5** — Cleo's secret doesn't fit what she does for most of the voyage, and the sisters' estrangement and Cleo's rejection of the workshop are settled offstage or told to us, not dramatised.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Cleo works for the sale she secretly opposes
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 3 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** "She will run... And father goes to Lowood Bight." (Scene 1) / "Without it, the cylinder head would crack within three minutes, and the buyer's contract in Ruth's oilskin pouch would become scrap paper." (Scene 3) / "He didn't want the Cormorant sold to the timber yard, Ruth. He wanted her brought here, to David." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Cleo has known since before the trip that Arthur wanted the boat to go to David and not be sold. Yet she backs the sale, scalds her hands to keep the engine (and so the contract) alive, and never shows any conflict between the two aims. Once the secret comes out, her earlier effort reads as whatever the plot needed.
- **Reader needed:** A glimpse of what Cleo actually intended for the contract (to let it fail, to hand over the money, to tell Ruth at the dock), so her choices on the voyage fit her secret.

### 2. [major] The sisters' reckoning happens offstage
- **Where:** Scene 3 and Scene 5, against Scene 6
- **Quote:** "You stayed with him in Lowood those last three weeks. You cut these pages out." (Scene 3) / "She stepped up beside Cleo, her shoulder brushing her sister's arm without pulling away." (Scene 6)
- **Problem:** Ruth accused Cleo of theft, then learned Cleo had hidden a half-brother and their father's second life from her. We never see Ruth react to Cleo's deception. She walks off alone in Scene 5, and by Scene 6 she is leaning into Cleo. The emotional core the premise promises (estranged sisters) resolves between scenes.
- **Reader needed:** At least one exchange where Ruth confronts Cleo about keeping the secret, or chooses to let it go, before they become allies.

### 3. [major] Cleo's refusal of the workshop is announced, not earned
- **Where:** Scene 6
- **Quote:** "For ten years she had thought that bench was the only future permitted to her. She looked down at her blistered palms... and felt nothing of the old binding weight."
- **Problem:** Nothing earlier shows the bench as a trap Cleo wants out of. In Scene 1 she is the loyal one who "spent three months washing his spoons". The "ten years" of feeling bound and her release both arrive in one sentence of narration, just in time for "We're done with the water."
- **Reader needed:** An earlier sign that Cleo feels chained to her father's trade, so that saying no to Toby completes a change we have watched happen.

### 4. [minor] Cleo claims a choice that was Ruth's
- **Where:** Scene 5 and Scene 6
- **Quote:** "Ruth drew a breath of the salt air, stepped across the thwart, and laid Arthur's bronze urn into David Miller's outstretched palms." (Scene 5) / "We gave the vessel to his son" (Scene 6)
- **Problem:** Cleo was the one carrying out Arthur's wish, yet at the key moment she sits silent on the engine casing while Ruth decides, out of bitterness. Cleo's passivity there, and her calm "we" afterwards, are never looked at.
- **Reader needed:** Cleo's response to Ruth doing, out of spite, the thing Cleo came to do out of loyalty.

### 5. [minor] Toby softens very fast
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 6
- **Quote:** "He was arthritic, slow-footed, and remarkably stubborn when an account sat open on his slate." (Scene 1) / "His voice had softened, shedding its edge of official greed." (Scene 6)
- **Problem:** Toby goes from confiscating their storm anchor, which leaves them in danger, to offering a cheap fresh start within a few lines. His surprise about Martha is a trigger, but the narrator names the softening before we see what moved him.
- **Reader needed:** A beat of what Toby feels about Arthur (guilt about the anchor, old friendship) behind the offer.

### 6. [nit] Ruth's grievance is out of scale with her established life
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** "Two years in the city had made her sharp-featured and crisp about money" (Scene 1) / "She had starved through three winters to keep the council from the cottage" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Ruth's bitterness is suddenly rooted in a sacrifice we never saw, and it sits awkwardly with the city life the story gave her.
- **Reader needed:** The source of Ruth's resentment set up before the moment that relies on it.

## What works
- Ruth's freeze in the squall suits a city-coated sister who "handl[es] the wheel as though it were a wagon brake": "Terror had turned her to stone."
- Cleo's deflection under accusation is in character, and so is the tension it creates: "Ruth, start the engine... We have twenty minutes before Gull Shoal turns against us."
- Ruth handing over the urn is bitter and in her voice: "He's yours... The boat, the debt, and the dust. Take all of it."

## Out of lane
Ruth "starved through three winters" at the cottage, which conflicts with "Two years in the city" (consistency).
