# Prose Craft — The Borrowed Boat

**Score: 3/5** — The sea-action scenes are taut and physical, but scenes contradict each other's physical details, earlier information keeps getting recapped, and the prose leans on triplets, glosses and a small stock of repeated words that a reader will notice.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Scenes disagree about physical facts
- **Where:** Scene 2 → Scene 5; Scene 1 → Scene 5 / Scene 6
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "The gaff plunged down. The heavy canvas collapsed into a sodden heap across the cabin roof" / Scene 5: "The sail hung limp from the pine spar, useless now that the wind had died behind the headland."
- **Problem:** A halyard cut in Scene 2 somehow has the sail hoisted again in Scene 5. The same drift runs through the story. The workshop smells of "pine shavings, stale tallow, and cold stove ash" (Scene 1) but its "familiar ghost" is "cedar shavings, linseed oil, and old tobacco" (Scene 6). The keel is "oak" (Scene 4) and then "a pine keel" (Scene 6). Cleo carries the urn aboard and it is stowed in the cabin (Scenes 1–2), yet Ruth is suddenly "gripping the cold bronze urn" by the mast (Scene 5). Ruth had "Two years in the city" (Scene 1), then "starved through three winters to keep the council from the cottage" (Scene 5). Each scene rebuilds the boat and the family from scratch.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent boat, workshop and family history carried across scenes.

### 2. [minor] Scenes recap earlier scenes as if the reader wasn't there
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 4, Scene 6
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "In Skiff Haven, Fenn had taken their sixty-pound storm anchor to settle their father’s slip dues; this light kedging hook was all Toby had left them."
- **Problem:** The anchor loss was dramatised in Scene 1 and is explained again here. Scene 2 restates Toby's warning ("slack water at Gull Shoal lasted twenty minutes"). Scene 6 re-introduces a character we already know: "Toby Fenn, the arthritic harbourmaster who had dogged their departure". Scene 6 also has Cleo retell the whole Lowood revelation to Toby ("Arthur maintained a second family there for twenty years, Toby"). These are the seams of scenes written separately.
- **Reader needed:** Trust that the reader remembers the anchor, the warning and Toby. At most, a glancing callback.

### 3. [minor] Triplets and repeated rhetorical patterns
- **Where:** Scene 4, Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "The boat, the tools, and what was left of him." … "He was Arthur’s blood, Arthur’s secret, and Arthur’s heir" … "The boat, the debt, and the dust."
- **Problem:** Scene 5 has three triplets, two of them built on the same "The boat, the ___, and ___" frame. Scene 4 adds "no time for blame, no time for the five hundred pounds, no time for contracts." Other stock turns: "It was not a gradual freshening. It dropped…" (Scene 2) and "announced itself not with a roar, but with a heavy, sucking murmur" (Scene 4). Scenes 2, 3 and 4 each have a one-line "Then…" paragraph ("Then the squall fell on them", "Then the instrument panel began to shriek.", "Then came the strike.").
- **Reader needed:** The emotional peak in Scene 5 should carry its weight on its own, without rhetorical scaffolding that sounds generated.

### 4. [minor] A handful of words recycled across scenes
- **Where:** Scenes 3–6
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "She looked down at her blistered palms, raw and stinging from engine brine"
- **Problem:** "Blistered" is attached to Cleo's hands at least six times (Scene 3 "blistered hand", Scene 4 "blistered palms" twice and "blistered knuckles", Scene 5 "blistered palms", Scene 6). The same goes for "kindling" (Scene 1 "lumber for kindling", Scene 4 "open like kindling", Scene 5 "ribs and kindling"), Toby's "rheumy eyes" (Scenes 1 and 6), "cold bronze urn" (Scenes 1 and 5), and characters walking off without looking back (Scene 1 "not bothering to look back", Scene 5 "did not look back").
- **Reader needed:** Variation, or a deliberate refrain, rather than the same default words.

### 5. [minor] The narrator states emotions and meanings outright
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 3, Scene 5, Scene 6
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "To hold the urn was to keep carrying the corpse of a dead man's neglect. To let it go was to walk away with empty pockets and two bruised hands."
- **Problem:** Ruth's choice is explained in aphorisms right before she makes it, next to "It would be justice, bitter and thin as sea water." The same happens with "Terror had turned her to stone." (Scene 2, after her fear has already been shown), "the suspicion hardened, cold and unmoving" (Scene 3), and "felt nothing of the old binding weight" (Scene 6).
- **Reader needed:** Let the gesture of handing over the urn carry Ruth's decision.

### 6. [minor] Dialogue and technical terms explained on the page
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 3, Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "'Luff her!' Cleo shouted against the roar, calling for Ruth to turn the bow into the wind and spill the canvas."
- **Problem:** The narrator translates the shout right after it's given. Scene 3 does the same with an appositive glossary ("the sea-chest, the grated intake chamber set below the waterline that fed cold brine through the cooling jacket"). In Scene 5 Cleo's reveal comes as a spoken list ("A house behind the lime kilns, a woman named Martha Miller, and a boy who grew up in his workshop…").
- **Reader needed:** Jargon made clear by the action around it, and revelation that sounds like speech rather than briefing.

### 7. [nit] Similes that don't hold together, and a broken sentence
- **Where:** Scene 3, Scene 5, Scene 6
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "the brass buttons of his wool coat caught a dull gleam, steady as tallow wick and candlelight." / "It was the same sour basin they had fled at daybreak, smelled of rotting bladderwrack"
- **Problem:** "steady as tallow wick and candlelight" makes no sense as a comparison, and the second sentence is ungrammatical ("smelled" where "smelling" is needed). Elsewhere: kelp "like frayed brocade" (Scene 3), "the weary precision of a clerk inspecting a dead mule" (Scene 5), and "sixty pounds of forged iron, forged to bite" (Scene 1).
- **Reader needed:** Images that fit this rough, working coast, and clean syntax.

## What works
- The squall is told in short, driving sentences that build real tension: "Cleo sawed at the three-strand hemp with three fierce, tearing strokes. / The line parted with a sound like a rifle crack."
- An exact, earned image at the end of the rip: "The tearing roar of the rip water dropped behind them like a shut door."
- Toby's one line of reaction says more than the exposition around it: "Lord above. He always did keep two logs for every voyage."

## Out of lane
The Scene 4 claim that the slack window "slipped past" off Crow Point sits awkwardly against the Scene 3 dialogue "We have twenty minutes"; that's for the plot-logic lens.
