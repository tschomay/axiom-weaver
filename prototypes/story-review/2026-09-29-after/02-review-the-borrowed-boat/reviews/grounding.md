# Grounding and Detail — The Borrowed Boat

**Score: 3/5** — The passage scenes are well placed and their mechanics are clear. But the load-bearing backstory (where Arthur spent his last year, the missing pension, the Lowood family) arrives mid-argument as if the reader already knew it, and the reader has to piece together the father's life from pieces that don't fit.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Arthur's last months and the pension are offstage and never placed
- **Where:** Scene 3 (set up in Scene 1, paid off in Scene 5)
- **Quote:** "Arthur had spent his final winter coughing into a wool blanket by the dying hearth... and she had spent three months washing his spoons." (Scene 1); "Five hundred pounds of father's railway pension... Gone from his account before he died in Lowood." (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** Scene 1 places Arthur's decline at a home hearth, with Cleo nursing him. Scene 3 then brings in, through Ruth's accusation, a Lowood berth "while his health failed", a death in Lowood, and a missing £500, none of which the reader has met before. Scene 5 adds that he "packed his chest and left for the western reaches". The plot and the reveal depend on where Arthur lived and died, and the reader has to reconstruct it from claims that pull against each other.
- **Reader needed:** One early, plain statement of where Arthur spent his last months and what Ruth already knew about the pension, so that the accusation and the reveal build on known ground.

### 2. [minor] How the slack window was missed is asserted, not tracked
- **Where:** Scene 4 (against Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "We have twenty minutes before Gull Shoal turns against us." (Scene 3); "The twenty-minute shoal tide window had slipped past while she was choking on bilge brine off Crow Point" (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Toby sets up the twenty-minute slack as the key clock, but the reader never learns how far Crow Point is from the shoal or how long the repair took. So the missed window is announced rather than felt, and it seems to contradict Cleo's own count moments before.
- **Reader needed:** A sense of distance or elapsed time between Crow Point and the shoal.

### 3. [minor] The trip back to Skiff Haven has no time anchor
- **Where:** Scene 6
- **Quote:** "watching the familiar slate quays of Skiff Haven Harbour slide into view... It was the same sour basin they had fled at daybreak"
- **Problem:** Scene 5 ends at evening in Lowood, with Ruth walking off alone up the road. Scene 6 opens with both sisters on a "coastal carrier" in "dim afternoon light". The reader has to guess whether this is the same day or the next, and how the sisters found each other again after Ruth left without looking back.
- **Reader needed:** A time cue (the next day's packet) and one clause on the sisters coming back together.

### 4. [minor] The buyer, the delivery point and the surveyor are never set up
- **Where:** Scene 5 (against Scene 1)
- **Quote:** "The yard in Lowood will break her up for her copper nails if we ask them to" (Scene 1); "A third figure approached along the timber jetty—a man in a stiff woollen frock coat... The marine surveyor" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Scene 1 never says clearly who the buyer is or that delivery and survey happen in Lowood. The Lowood yard reads as an alternative to the sale, not the buyer. So a surveyor waiting on the jetty, and "the timber syndicate", appear unannounced.
- **Reader needed:** One line in Scene 1 naming the buyer and saying the boat must reach Lowood for inspection.

### 5. [minor] Gull Shoal's position relative to Lowood Bight is never given
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "Borne by the last of her dying momentum, the boat drifted into the quiet, glassy shallows of Lowood Bight."
- **Problem:** Nothing earlier tells the reader that Gull Shoal guards the mouth of the destination. The boat drifting straight into Lowood from the reef comes as a geographic surprise.
- **Reader needed:** Toby's warning in Scene 1, or Scene 2's description of the Reach, could say that Gull Shoal is the last passage before Lowood.

### 6. [minor] POV drifts mid-scene without a cue
- **Where:** Scene 4 (also the Scene 2 to Scene 3 handoff)
- **Quote:** "her restarted engine vibrating through Cleo's blistered palms" (opening of Scene 4); "Ruth stared down through the hatch... Cleo had saved their skins, but the buyer's clause was broken" (closing of Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Scene 4 opens behind Cleo's eyes and closes in Ruth's judgment, with no break. Scene 3 switches to Ruth without signalling it either. It can be recovered, but the reader keeps re-checking whose view this is.
- **Reader needed:** One POV per scene, or a clear hinge where the view changes.

### 7. [nit] Recap of the anchor seizure in the middle of the action
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "In Skiff Haven, Fenn had taken their sixty-pound storm anchor to settle their father's slip dues; this light kedging hook was all Toby had left them."
- **Problem:** This repeats Scene 1 in the middle of the anchoring beat. The previous sentence already calls it "the spare kedge anchor".
- **Reader needed:** Nothing more. The reader remembers the anchor.

## What works
- The squall is blocked clearly enough to follow each move: "She reached the mast partner, wrapped one leg around the pin rail, and dragged her rigging knife from her belt." (Scene 2)
- Mechanics are explained at the moment they matter: "the sea-chest, the grated intake chamber set below the waterline that fed cold brine through the cooling jacket" (Scene 3).
- Scene 1 plants both later pressures in concrete terms, the lost anchor and the slack window: "You have a twenty-minute slack window, not a minute more, before the ebb turns".

## Out of lane
The bill of sale sits in three places (Ruth's oilskin sleeve, "the buyer's packet" in the cabin, the surveyor's folder), and Ruth's "two years in the city" clashes with her "three winters" keeping the cottage (consistency).
