# Setup and Payoff — The Borrowed Boat

**Score: 4/5** — the anchor, the slack window, the engine clause and the Lowood secret are all fairly planted and fully paid off, but the razor-cut log pages, the story's clearest mystery object, are never explained.

## Findings

### 1. [minor] Who cut the log pages, and what they said, is never answered
- **Where:** Scene 3 (never resolved in Scenes 5–6)
- **Quote:** "Neatly sheared away along the binding with a razor, leaving only a row of ragged white stubs." / "You stayed with him in Lowood those last three weeks. You cut these pages out."
- **Problem:** The story lingers on the missing pages and Ruth accuses Cleo directly. Scene 5 explains the money, but nobody says whether Cleo, or Arthur, cut the pages, or where they are. Toby's "He always did keep two logs for every voyage" (Scene 6) hints at an answer and then drops it.
- **Reader needed:** One line settling the pages, whether Cleo admits cutting them, Arthur did it, or the pages turn up with David, so the accusation is answered as well as the money.

### 2. [minor] Cleo's tie to the workbench is paid off without being set up
- **Where:** Scene 6
- **Quote:** "For ten years she had thought that bench was the only future permitted to her."
- **Problem:** Cleo's refusal is framed as the end of her arc, but the story never showed her feeling trapped by, or longing for, the workshop. Scene 1 opens in the workshop without giving her any weight there, so the refusal is explained after the fact rather than earned.
- **Reader needed:** An early beat, perhaps in the Scene 1 workshop, showing Cleo's feelings about the bench and lathe, so that "No" in Scene 6 lands as a choice the story has prepared.

### 3. [nit] The secret is told again to Toby
- **Where:** Scene 6 (after Scene 5)
- **Quote:** "Arthur maintained a second family there for twenty years, Toby. If you want your twelve pounds four, take the morning packet across the channel and ask his boy for it."
- **Problem:** Cleo repeats almost all of the Scene 5 reveal (son, Martha, key, face, twenty years) to a new listener. It isn't a true double reveal, but it spends a paragraph on news the reader already has.
- **Reader needed:** Only Toby's reaction is new. A shorter handover would let "two logs for every voyage" carry the scene.

### 4. [nit] Ruth's threat to wreck the boat is dropped
- **Where:** Scene 1
- **Quote:** "But if the engine seizes, I am turning the helm straight into the nearest breaker."
- **Problem:** The engine does fail twice, and each time Ruth does the opposite. The threat is never recalled, so her change goes unmarked.
- **Reader needed:** A glance back at this line when Ruth steadies the wheel in Scene 3 or hands over the urn in Scene 5.

## What works
- The confiscated storm anchor is a textbook plant. "useless against a surging tide" (Scene 1) pays off exactly at the shoal: "the light fluke skittering helplessly over slick shale and weed, unable to bite without the lost anchor's weight" (Scene 4). It then returns as the last thing Cleo gives up in Scene 6.
- Toby's "twenty-minute slack window" (Scene 1) is kept in view ("slack water at Gull Shoal lasted twenty minutes", Scene 2; "We have twenty minutes", Scene 3), so missing it in Scene 4 feels like a consequence rather than bad luck.
- The Lowood secret is fairly planted: "father goes to Lowood Bight" and Arthur's "last plea" to Cleo (Scene 1), plus "the months he spent berthed at Lowood" (Scene 3), all come together when David appears with the key (Scene 5).

## Out of lane
The mainsail halyard is cut in Scene 2, yet in Scene 5 "the sail hung limp from the pine spar" (consistency).
