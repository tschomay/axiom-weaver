# Prose Craft — The Apprentice’s Bill

**Score: 3/5** — The kitchen detail is vivid and the whisk scene lands, but the prose is often overwrought, it keeps falling into the "not X; it was Y" construction, and details that don't agree between scenes show where they were stitched together.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Details that disagree between scenes show the seams
- **Where:** Scene 3 and Scene 4 (also Scene 1)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "A young commis—a girl whose knuckles were still pink from peeling root vegetables" / Scene 4: "Just as I took the blame for the boy's shattered tray at seven"
- **Problem:** The apprentice changes sex between scenes. The same drift shows up elsewhere. Julian's labour is "developed over three years in the back larder" (Scene 1) but "five years of labor" (Scene 3). Kane has "forty years of coal-smoke" but boasts of "thirty years at the range" (Scene 4). He "towered" (Scene 1) but has a "broad, stooped back" (Scene 3). The kitchen doors are "heavy brass… thick mahogany" (Scene 1), then "swinging oak doors" (Scene 3). Each scene is describing its own copy of the room.
- **Reader needed:** Physical and numeric details that stay the same from scene to scene, so the reader trusts that one narrator is watching one kitchen.

### 2. [major] Simile and metaphor stacked on nearly every character entrance
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 2, Scene 3
- **Quote:** "his gaze settling upon Julian with the slow, leaden indifference of a monument that had long ceased to regard the weeds at its base."
- **Problem:** The same "with the [adjective] [noun] of a [figure]" pattern keeps coming back: "with the heavy, unhurried pressure of slaughterhouse hands" (Scene 1), "with the measured indifference of a bailiff come to inventory an estate" (Scene 2), "with the silent certainty of a ghost returning to a sacked house" (Scene 3). On top of these come "like an embezzled crown" and glances "weighed and delivered on silver chargers" (Scene 2). That's at least six ornate figures in about 1,500 words, and they read as a template.
- **Reader needed:** Fewer and plainer images, so the few that remain can do real work.

### 3. [minor] Recurring "not X — it was Y" / reversal construction
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 3, Scene 4
- **Quote:** "It was not a chef addressing a rival, nor a master pleading with a thief; it was an animal watching its own flesh fail."
- **Problem:** The same model tic appears in every scene after the first. Scene 2 has "The service would not end in silence. It would end in the dining room, on Arthur’s knees." Scene 3 has "He expected frenzy. He expected… Instead, he found…". Scene 4 has "The mastery was not in the name upon the brass awning outside; it was in the balance of the pan."
- **Reader needed:** More varied sentence shapes at the key moments, so the turns don't all sound machine-made.

### 4. [major] The climax's meaning is explained, not dramatised
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "By stepping into the heat, seizing the whisk, and mastering the curdle with cold acid, he had not merely saved Kane's service—he had definitively reclaimed the craft as entirely his own."
- **Problem:** The narrator states the lesson outright right before Julian's line of dialogue, which already carries it. The same happens to Kane: "his shoulders bowing under the sudden, absolute recognition of his own retirement. He was a relic clutching a shadow." Scene 2's "True retribution required a far more intimate violence" is a smaller case of the same thing.
- **Reader needed:** Trust that the refused spoon and "It doesn't matter anymore" make the point without a narrator's summary.

### 5. [minor] The same technical rule is recited in two scenes
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 3
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "the damson plum reduction curdles and scorches if its temperature exceeds seventy-two degrees Celsius without rapid whisking." / Scene 3: "would curdle into bitter, scorched tar the moment it crossed seventy-two degrees without rapid, ceaseless agitation."
- **Problem:** Scene 1 shifts into a present-tense, spec-sheet sentence ("curdles and scorches if") inside past-tense narration. Scene 3 then restates the same fact almost word for word. It reads like the same line was dropped into two separately written scenes.
- **Reader needed:** The threshold planted once in Julian's voice, then simply trusted when the pan boils over.

### 6. [minor] Villain dialogue that announces itself
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 4)
- **Quote:** "He will taste perfection, Vance. He will taste my genius, recorded in tomorrow's gazette, while you scrounge for dish shifts behind the docks."
- **Problem:** Kane speaks in declarative monologue, and Julian's opening ("That I am permitted to work") sounds like a legal brief. In Scene 4, Kane's confession recaps earlier events for the reader ("Just as I took the blame for the boy's shattered tray at seven, because…").
- **Reader needed:** Speech that sounds like two cooks at a pass rather than characters explaining the premise.

### 7. [nit] Doubled words and a pan that changes identity
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 4
- **Quote:** "The silence in the dining room had the hollow, hollowed-out ring of an emptied church."
- **Problem:** "hollow, hollowed-out" (Scene 4) and "hot heat lamps" (Scene 1) are careless doublings. In Scene 1 the reduction sits in "the copper pan", then in "the small enameled saucepan", then in "the copper pan" again, which makes the reader wonder whether there are two pans.
- **Reader needed:** One name for the pan and no redundant modifiers.

### 8. [nit] Stock closing gestures
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 4
- **Quote:** "stepping out into the clean, night air without looking back."
- **Problem:** Scene 1 ends on "his hands clenching into fists" (after an earlier "his jaw tightening"), and the story ends on the stock walk-away-without-looking-back.
- **Reader needed:** Endings built from this story's own objects (the spoon, the stained fingers) rather than stock gestures.

## What works
- The rescue at the stove is concrete, physical and fast: "He poured three measures of the pale, cold acid directly into the blistering purple pool. The thermal shock hissed violently, billows of scented steam engulfing them both."
- Julian's plain final line cuts through the ornament well: "Eat it yourself, Arthur," Julian said softly. "Or throw it out. It doesn't matter anymore."
- A quiet, precise image: "The kitchen doors did not swing so much as drift open."

## Out of lane
Kane's tremor and his covering for the apprentice both appear with no setup before Scene 3, which is a question for the plot/payoff lens.
