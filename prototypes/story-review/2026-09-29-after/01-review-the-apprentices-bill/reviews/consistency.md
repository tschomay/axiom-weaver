# Logical Consistency — The Apprentice's Bill

**Score: 3/5** — no single fact breaks the plot, but there are several minor slips: the commis changes sex in a callback, and the kitchen doors and the reduction's pan keep changing what they are made of.

## Findings

### 1. [minor] The commis who dropped the tray changes from a girl to a boy
- **Where:** Scene 3 (and Scene 4)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "A young commis—a girl whose knuckles were still pink from peeling root vegetables—stood paralyzed over the ruins of a porcelain charger" / Scene 4: "Just as I took the blame for the boy's shattered tray at seven"
- **Problem:** Kane's callback in the climactic confession gets wrong the one detail of the scene it points back to. Scene 3 also calls her "the terrified apprentice" and uses "her station".
- **Reader needed:** The callback should use the same person the reader saw in Scene 3.

### 2. [minor] The kitchen doors are brass, then mahogany, then oak
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "The heavy brass doors clicked shut behind them, sealing off the heat of the ranges behind thick mahogany." / Scene 3: "Just before the swinging oak doors, he halted in the shadow of the pantry stack."
- **Problem:** The same doorway between the dining room and the kitchen is brass, then mahogany in the same sentence, then oak two scenes later.
- **Reader needed:** The dividing doors should be made of one material throughout.

### 3. [minor] The reduction's pan keeps changing
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "pointing at the copper pan resting beside the service pass" / "Beside Kane's elbow, the small enameled saucepan simmered on the corner of the flat-top." / Scene 3: "the rich, dark mash was foaming violently against the stainless steel" … "dragged the heavy saucepan half off"
- **Problem:** Within one scene the reduction sits in a copper pan and in a small enameled saucepan. Then in Scene 3 it is in a heavy stainless saucier. A reader can't tell whether this is one pan or several, or which one Kane turned the heat up under.
- **Reader needed:** One pan for the reduction, or a clear sign that the reduction was moved or split between pans.

### 4. [nit] How long Julian worked there doesn't quite add up
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scenes 3, 4)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "developed over three years in the back larder" / Scene 3: "a man who had scraped Julian's name off five years of labor" / Scene 4: "You surpassed me three years ago"
- **Problem:** "Three years" and "five years" can both be true (five years in the kitchen, three of them on the reduction), but side by side they read like a number that drifted.
- **Reader needed:** Anchor the two numbers to each other, or use one.

### 5. [nit] The tart shell changes
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "an unfired pastry ring holding the par-baked shell of a tart" / Scene 3: "Julian drew a pre-baked sablé shell toward him"
- **Problem:** "Unfired", "par-baked" and "pre-baked" describe different states of the shell. Only a careful reader would notice.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent state for the shell.

## What works
- The acid-and-temperature rule set up in Scene 1 is kept exactly in Scene 3, which is what makes the rescue believable: "the damson plum reduction curdles and scorches if its temperature exceeds seventy-two degrees Celsius without rapid whisking" → "driving the temperature down past the scorch threshold".
- The seating ledger holds. Laurent is at table four and Julian at table nine, and Scene 4 returns Julian to his seat: "Julian sat where he had been pushed hours before".
- The critic's praise matches what the reader actually saw Julian do: "He asked to commend the master who balanced the verjuice against the skins."

## Out of lane
Nobody in a full brigade notices Julian re-spiking a ticket at the pass. That is a plausibility question for grounding or character, not a contradiction.
