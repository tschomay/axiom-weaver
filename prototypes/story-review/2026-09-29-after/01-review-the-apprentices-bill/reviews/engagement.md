# Engagement and Believability — The Apprentice's Bill

**Score: 3/5** — The opening sets up strong, concrete stakes, but the central reversal comes out of nowhere and the ending drops the blacklist the story was built around, so the payoff feels inert.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Kane's tremor, the story's hinge, is never set up
- **Where:** Scene 3 (against Scene 1)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "Arthur picked up the sheet of paper with two thick fingers... and tore it cleanly in half. He folded the pieces together and tore them again" / Scene 3: "A severe tremor had seized the joint of his right wrist, twisting his fingers into a rigid, bird-like claw"
- **Problem:** Scene 1 gives us a man with steady, powerful hands. The crippling tremor that turns the whole story arrives as a surprise, with no earlier flicker. It reads as the author stepping in to change the direction rather than as a secret coming out. Kane's weeping plea follows straight after, so the villain collapses in a single paragraph.
- **Reader needed:** One small, easy-to-miss sign of the tremor earlier (a hand kept out of sight, a dish Kane won't plate himself), so the reveal lands as "of course" rather than "where did that come from?"

### 2. [major] The ending drops the blacklist, the stakes the story opened on
- **Where:** Scene 4 (against Scene 1)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "You will notify the Guild and every kitchen from here to the coast that your black mark was retaliatory. That I am permitted to work." / Scene 4: "a room of his own, a lease signed with his own name, an independent kitchen built from the stone floor up"
- **Problem:** Julian's concrete goal is getting the blacklist lifted. The critic still credits Kane, the confession is made in private, and nothing is retracted. The ending swaps that goal for an inner victory and a future kitchen, and never says how a blacklisted, penniless chef gets a lease. "It doesn't matter anymore" waves away the exact thing that did matter.
- **Reader needed:** Either some outcome on the blacklist and the credit (Kane agrees to speak, refuses, or Julian chooses to walk away from it knowing what that costs), or a clear reason the new kitchen is now possible.

### 3. [major] Julian gets onto the line unchallenged
- **Where:** Scene 3 (against Scene 1)
- **Quote:** "Julian slid through the service opening. His fingers were quick and practiced. He unhooked the order for table four... and jammed it onto the active firing spike"
- **Problem:** An hour earlier Kane had two commis march this man out of the kitchen, and the floor staff have been watching him since. Now he walks through the pass during a six-table rush, re-orders tickets and stands behind the pastry partition, and nobody notices. The sabotage also turns out not to matter, because the tremor causes the crisis anyway.
- **Reader needed:** A reason the brigade misses him (the spill pulled everyone's attention, he's in whites, the staff know him from before), and a sabotage that actually feeds into what goes wrong.

### 4. [minor] Kane's kindness to the commis is too convenient to count as setup
- **Where:** Scene 3 (paid off in Scene 4)
- **Quote:** "Arthur told the floor manager that he had knocked the tray himself... waved the terrified apprentice back to her station"
- **Problem:** This is the only hint that Kane is more than a villain, and it lands a few paragraphs before his collapse. Scene 4 then has Kane explain what it meant ("because a kitchen does not fall apart before the room is fed"). It feels planted to soften him on schedule, not shown as part of who he is.
- **Reader needed:** A hint of Kane's code earlier, in Scene 1 or 2, so his change of heart grows out of the character.

### 5. [minor] The villain's full confession clears away the last obstacle
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "My hands are finished. You surpassed me three years ago, Julian, and I was too cowardly to let the city see it."
- **Problem:** Kane goes from tearing up the retraction to confessing everything, without being pushed. Julian never has to force the truth out of him. The confrontation the vow promised doesn't happen, because the antagonist gives in on his own.
- **Reader needed:** Some leftover resistance or cost in Kane's admission, so Julian's response is a real choice under pressure.

### 6. [minor] The resolution is explained instead of dramatised
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "To force the reduction between Kane's lips now would be the act of an apprentice still begging for a master's validation. But Julian was no longer an apprentice."
- **Problem:** The emotional turn is delivered as a paragraph of internal thesis, closing on "The mastery was not in the name upon the brass awning outside; it was in the balance of the pan". This spells out the meaning instead of letting Julian's gesture carry it, and it dulls the scene.
- **Reader needed:** Trust in the action ("Eat it yourself, Arthur") to carry the meaning without the explanation around it.

### 7. [minor] Scene 2 stays at one pitch
- **Where:** Scene 2
- **Quote:** "He would force Kane to taste what he had stolen. Julian swore it into the velvet dusk of the alcove"
- **Problem:** The whole scene is Julian sitting and vowing. The critic's entrance adds atmosphere but no new pressure, so the tension from the end of Scene 1 goes slack until Julian gets up.
- **Reader needed:** One new complication while he waits (the tart gets announced to Laurent, Julian's bill arrives, a staff member recognises him).

## What works
- The tearing of the retraction is a sharp, specific act of contempt that sets the stakes fast: "tore it cleanly in half. He folded the pieces together and tore them again, letting the white squares flutter into the bin" (Scene 1).
- Kane's cruelty in making Julian a paying diner is inventive and particular to this story: "He will watch the plates leave the pass, course by course, and he will pay Kane's prices for every mouthful." (Scene 1).
- The rescue is Julian's own doing, and his craft carries it, so the climax belongs to the protagonist: "He poured three measures of the pale, cold acid directly into the blistering purple pool." (Scene 3).

## Out of lane
The commis spill is "a girl" in Scene 3 but "the boy's shattered tray" in Scene 4 (consistency).
