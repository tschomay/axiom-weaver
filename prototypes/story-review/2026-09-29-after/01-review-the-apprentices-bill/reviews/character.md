# Character Coherence — The Apprentice's Bill

**Score: 3/5** — Kane's move from monument to weeping confessor carries the whole ending, and the page never sets it up. Julian drops the thing he came for without a word, and his change of heart is told to us in narration.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Julian's stated want disappears
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 4
- **Quote:** "You will sign the retraction... You will notify the Guild and every kitchen from here to the coast that your black mark was retaliatory. That I am permitted to work." (Scene 1) / "Eat it yourself, Arthur... Or throw it out. It doesn't matter anymore." (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Julian comes in wanting the blacklist lifted so he can work. At last he has a broken, confessing Kane in front of him, and he never asks for the one thing that would let him earn a living. He then walks out planning "a lease signed with his own name" as if the blacklist had stopped existing.
- **Reader needed:** Julian either asks for the retraction or visibly decides he no longer needs it, and the reader needs to see why.

### 2. [major] Kane's collapse and confession come from nowhere
- **Where:** Scene 3 and Scene 4, against Scene 1
- **Quote:** "Arthur Kane towered in double-breasted white drill... the slow, leaden indifference of a monument" (Scene 1) / "Instead, he found Arthur standing over the heavy saucier pan, weeping." (Scene 3) / "You surpassed me three years ago, Julian, and I was too cowardly to let the city see it." (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** In Scene 1 Kane tears the paper "cleanly" with steady fingers and gloats. Nothing on the page hints at the tremor, the fear or the self-knowledge. He goes from bully to weeping supplicant to humble confessor in one service. The tremor explains why he can't cook the sauce. It doesn't explain why he suddenly tells his hated rival the truth.
- **Reader needed:** Some earlier sign of the failing hands or the hidden fear, so that the collapse and the confession feel like the real Kane showing through and not a new character.

### 3. [major] Julian's arc is announced, not dramatised
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "By stepping into the heat, seizing the whisk, and mastering the curdle with cold acid, he had not merely saved Kane's service—he had definitively reclaimed the craft as entirely his own."
- **Problem:** The move from vengeance to release is explained to us in a paragraph of narration. Julian says one line and leaves. His rage "suddenly felt thin, dry as ash", and all we have is the narrator's word for it.
- **Reader needed:** An action or exchange in which Julian's release happens in front of us, not a summary of what it means.

### 4. [minor] The sabotage doesn't serve the vow
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 3
- **Quote:** "he would choke down the very plum reduction he had claimed as his own, mouthful by burning mouthful, in the full sight of his house" (Scene 2) / "He unhooked the order for table four... and jammed it onto the active firing spike" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** Julian swears a very specific, physical revenge, then carries out a different one (rushing a ticket so the tart fails). We never see him connect the two, so his plan reads as whatever the plot needed next.
- **Reader needed:** One beat showing how Julian thinks rushing the ticket leads to Kane eating his own reduction.

### 5. [minor] Kane's chivalry is unexplained until the end
- **Where:** Scene 3 (explained in Scene 4)
- **Quote:** "Arthur told the floor manager that he had knocked the tray himself... He absorbed the sour reprimand without blinking" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** Kane is a man who snaps his fingers for henchmen and humiliates Julian as a paying diner. Here he quietly takes a subordinate's blame, and the only reason given comes later, in his own speech ("a kitchen does not fall apart before the room is fed"). Julian calls it an "anomaly", and so will the reader.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier hint that Kane's arrogance sits alongside a fierce loyalty to the kitchen itself.

### 6. [nit] Julian's resolve to wait flips at once
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 3
- **Quote:** "He would not move until the heat began to turn." (Scene 2) / "Julian rose from table nine before his amuse-bouche had even cooled" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** He is settled and patient, then moves at once with no trigger.
- **Reader needed:** A cue that "the heat began to turn", or no stated resolve to wait.

## What works
- Julian's switch at the stove comes from a trait we believe: "an older, involuntary horror: the sight of perfection about to spoil upon the iron." His craft pride outranks his vengeance, and that is a real motive.
- Kane's voice changes register in a way that fits the change in his state: from "He will taste my genius, recorded in tomorrow's gazette" to "My hands are finished."
- Kane's cruelty in Scene 1 is specific and in character: "He will be a paying diner tonight... and he will pay Kane's prices for every mouthful."

## Out of lane
The commis who drops the tray is "a girl" in Scene 3 but "the boy" in Scene 4 (consistency).
