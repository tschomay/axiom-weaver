# Setup and Payoff — The Apprentice's Bill

**Score: 3/5** — the plum-reduction rule, the cold-acid ratio and the vow all pay off cleanly, but the reversal everything turns on (Kane's crippling tremor) is never planted, and the bill promised in the title is never presented.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Kane's tremor is a reveal from nowhere
- **Where:** Scene 3 (against Scene 1 and Scene 2)
- **Quote:** "A severe tremor had seized the joint of his right wrist, twisting his fingers into a rigid, bird-like claw that could no longer grip the balloon whisk lying spent on the floor tiles."
- **Problem:** The whole climax turns on this, yet in Scene 1 Kane handles things easily ("picked up the sheet of paper with two thick fingers... tore it cleanly in half. He folded the pieces together and tore them again") and nothing in Scenes 1–2 hints at failing hands. Scene 4 then describes it as the "arthritic tremor that had crippled him", as if it were a known condition. The one hint, "You don't even know how to hold it on the line", points to skill, not to his body.
- **Reader needed:** One small earlier sign of trouble with Kane's hands (a steadying grip, a hand kept out of sight, a job handed to someone else) so the collapse at the stove feels like a revelation, not a way out for the plot.

### 2. [major] The title's bill never arrives
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and the title), never paid off
- **Quote:** "He will be a paying diner tonight. He will watch the plates leave the pass, course by course, and he will pay Kane's prices for every mouthful."
- **Problem:** The story is called "The Apprentice's Bill", and Kane sets up the humiliation of making Julian pay. But no bill is presented, paid, refused or waived. Julian simply "rose from the banquette" and leaves. The obvious last beat, where the reader expects this to turn around, is missing.
- **Reader needed:** The bill should come back at the end, whether Julian pays it, Kane tears it up to mirror the torn retraction, or someone presents it, so that the title's promise is kept.

### 3. [minor] The blacklist, the story's opening demand, is never resolved
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 4
- **Quote:** "You will notify the Guild and every kitchen from here to the coast that your black mark was retaliatory. That I am permitted to work." (Scene 1); "a room of his own, a lease signed with his own name, an independent kitchen" (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Kane confesses privately, but the black mark stays in place. Julian's plan to open his own kitchen skips over the ban the story set up as the thing stopping him from working at all.
- **Reader needed:** A line showing whether the confession changes the blacklist, or that Julian no longer needs it lifted.

### 4. [minor] The central turn is paid off by the narrator's assertion
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "By stepping into the heat, seizing the whisk, and mastering the curdle with cold acid, he had not merely saved Kane's service—he had definitively reclaimed the craft as entirely his own."
- **Problem:** The paragraph explains what the scene has already shown. Kane's confession ("You surpassed me three years ago, Julian, and I was too cowardly") likewise hands over the theft's motive in one line instead of letting it be discovered, so the meaning is stated rather than realised.
- **Reader needed:** Trust the refused spoon and "Eat it yourself, Arthur" to carry the recognition. The explaining paragraph is not needed.

### 5. [nit] The ticket sabotage leads nowhere
- **Where:** Scene 3
- **Quote:** "compressing forty minutes of delicate thermal balance into ten frantic, unforgiving minutes."
- **Problem:** Julian's one active scheme is described carefully, then overtaken by the tremor. Nobody notices it, and it doesn't affect the outcome or come up in Scene 4.
- **Reader needed:** A brief acknowledgement that the rush he caused is part of why the pan nearly burned, so his guilt or complicity has some weight.

## What works
- The 72-degree rule is planted precisely and pays off at the stove: "the damson plum reduction curdles and scorches if its temperature exceeds seventy-two degrees Celsius without rapid whisking" (Scene 1) returns in Scene 3, where Kane having "turned the heat higher" becomes the danger.
- The cold-acid ratio Julian claims in Scene 1 ("The ratio of cold acid to low-simmered skins") is exactly what saves the pan ("chilled verjuice... cold acid"), and it is what the critic praises. That fairly proves the recipe is his.
- The vow to make Kane "choke down the very plum reduction" (Scene 2) is turned around well by Kane offering the spoon and Julian declining it. The commis-tray kindness (Scene 3) is also fairly planted as a sign of Kane's hidden decency before the confession.

## Out of lane
The commis is "a girl" in Scene 3 but "the boy" in Scene 4 (consistency).
