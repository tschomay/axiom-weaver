# Grounding and Detail — The Apprentice's Bill

**Score: 4/5** — Place, time and POV are set within the first sentences of every scene. The one real gap is in the Scene 3 kitchen: who is present and how the ticket trick works blur just when the plot turns on them, but a reader can recover from context.

## Findings

### 1. [minor] The kitchen empties at the crisis: who is present?
- **Where:** Scene 3
- **Quote:** "He waited until the pass groaned under a six-table rush, the kitchen tickets fluttering along the brass rail like dry leaves before a squall. In the crosscurrent of runners calling for venison and turbot, Julian slid through the service opening."
- **Problem:** The kitchen is full of runners and brigade in the middle of a rush. A few lines later Julian finds "Arthur standing over the heavy saucier pan, weeping", then crosses "the line in two strides" and takes over the stove, and no cook, runner or pastry hand is mentioned. The same is true of the two commis who threw him out in Scene 1. Julian is a banished, blacklisted man walking into a working kitchen, and nobody reacts or is even placed in the room.
- **Reader needed:** One line on where the rest of the brigade is (on the far line, heads down, or the pastry corner walled off by the partition), so it makes sense that Julian is alone with Kane.

### 2. [minor] How the ticket swap forces Kane onto pastry is claimed, not shown
- **Where:** Scene 3
- **Quote:** "He unhooked the order for table four... and jammed it onto the active firing spike, wedged ahead of the savory entrées. The sudden ticket would force Arthur to work the pastry station under the full, suffocating weight of the dinner rush"
- **Problem:** Nothing explains why moving one ticket would make the head chef himself work pastry, rather than a pastry cook, or why nobody notices a stranger re-ordering the rail. The narration states the result instead of showing how it happens. The next time we see Kane he is already at the pan.
- **Reader needed:** A clause saying Kane guards the stolen reduction himself (nobody else is allowed to make it), so a fired ticket pulls him to the pan.

### 3. [minor] Kane's tremor has no groundwork before it drives the climax
- **Where:** Scene 3 (and Scene 4)
- **Quote:** "A severe tremor had seized the joint of his right wrist, twisting his fingers into a rigid, bird-like claw" (Scene 3); "fingers that still jerked with the involuntary, arthritic tremor that had crippled him at the stove" (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Scene 1 gives Kane nothing but strength ("towered", "leaning his bulk", "two thick fingers"). The reader first meets the condition fully formed in Scene 3, and it is only named as "arthritic" afterward. The reader has to work out on the spot whether this is a sudden fit or a long-hidden illness.
- **Reader needed:** Some earlier sign in the prose, or at least a clear statement at the moment it appears, that this is a chronic condition Kane has been hiding.

### 4. [minor] Julian's walk back to table nine and the tart being served happen offstage
- **Where:** Scene 3 to Scene 4
- **Quote:** "set the tart under the pass heat lamp for table four." (end of Scene 3); "Julian sat where he had been pushed hours before, his fingers still faintly stained" (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Scene 3 ends with Julian at the stove in front of the brigade. Scene 4 opens hours later with him back at his table. How he got out of the kitchen, and whether staff or Laurent saw him, are left out, and those questions bear on the Scene 4 claim that Laurent "thought it was mine."
- **Reader needed:** One bridging sentence on Julian leaving the kitchen unremarked and on the tart reaching table four.

### 5. [minor] Scene 4 spells out the theme after the gesture has already shown it
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "By stepping into the heat, seizing the whisk, and mastering the curdle with cold acid, he had not merely saved Kane's service—he had definitively reclaimed the craft as entirely his own."
- **Problem:** This recaps Scene 3's action and states its meaning outright, just before Julian's line "Eat it yourself, Arthur" makes the same point through action.
- **Reader needed:** Nothing more. The paragraph could be much shorter.

### 6. [nit] "The Guild" and "black mark" arrive as if already known
- **Where:** Scene 1
- **Quote:** "You will notify the Guild and every kitchen from here to the coast that your black mark was retaliatory."
- **Problem:** The Guild is never introduced. Context carries it: the next paragraph explains the blacklist across "the regional restaurant circuit".
- **Reader needed:** A word placing the Guild (a chefs' guild) would be enough.

## What works
- The scene openings anchor place and time firmly: "The last patrons were gone, table four stood cleared of M. Laurent's discreet silver" sets Scene 4's time and room in one sentence.
- The sauce's failure point is planted early, so the rescue is legible when it comes: "the damson plum reduction curdles and scorches if its temperature exceeds seventy-two degrees Celsius without rapid whisking" (Scene 1).
- The rescue is shown step by step rather than asserted: "dragged the heavy saucepan half off the fierce direct flame to the cooler ring, and reached for the chilled verjuice" (Scene 3).

## Out of lane
The spilled tray is "a girl" in Scene 3 but "the boy's shattered tray" in Scene 4, and Julian's labor is "three years" in one place and "five years" in another (consistency).
