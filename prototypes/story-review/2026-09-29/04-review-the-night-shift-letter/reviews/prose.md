# Prose Craft — The Night Shift Letter

**Score: 2/5** — The comic voice has real wit in places, but almost every scene is built from the same parts. Each one gets a weather-tantrum simile, a domestic-kitchen comparison on nearly every noun, and a fresh re-introduction of the envelope, the locker and the tin. The repetition pulls the reader out again and again, and name, pronoun and object details drift between scenes.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The weather-tantrum simile appears in all 16 scenes
- **Where:** Scene 5 and Scene 16 (near-verbatim). The same move recurs in Scenes 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 and 15.
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "delivering a vicious blast of sleet with all the spiteful precision of a cook tossing cold dishwater at a stray tomcat" / Scene 16: "tossing spiteful spits of sleet against the corrugated awning like an irritable scullery-maid hurling cold dishwater at a stray tomcat"
- **Problem:** Every scene stops for "Outside, the November [rain/sleet/wind/fog] was [throwing a petulant tantrum / sulking / in a foul temper] … like a [servant/animal] …". Scene 2 has "throwing a petulant tantrum", Scene 6 "throwing a petulant fit", Scene 12 "having a furious, childish tantrum" and Scene 14 "throwing a petulant tantrum" again. "Petulant/petulance" appears in at least eight scenes (2, 4, 6, 8, 11, 13, 14, 15). By Scene 5 the reader can predict it, and by Scene 16 it repeats itself word for word.
- **Reader needed:** Weather that appears only when it matters, described differently each time, so the device doesn't read as a slot every scene has to fill.

### 2. [major] Stacked domestic-kitchen similes, with repeated crockery images
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 7, Scene 12. The same kind of stacking also runs through Scenes 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13 and 15.
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "standing around like a stack of chipped teacups on a greasy draining board" / Scene 7: "a state resembling a stack of chipped saucers teetering in a greasy sink" / Scene 12: "laid out like a tea tray piled high with chipped saucers and burnt toast crumbs"
- **Problem:** Almost every noun gets a scullery or animal comparison, and often two in one paragraph. Scene 8 goes from "a fat hamster denied a second helping of oats" to "burned lard congealing along the rims of neglected muffin tins" to "a petulant housemaid" in three sentences. The roasting-pan image appears twice ("unscrubbed roasting pan", Scene 6; "cracked roasting tin", Scene 15). Emotional beats get the same treatment: Neil's revelation in Scene 12 is compared to a tea tray. The comic distance kills the tension.
- **Reader needed:** Fewer, sharper figures, with the comic register held back when the scene turns painful.

### 3. [major] Each scene re-introduces the same props and people as if the story had just begun
- **Where:** Scenes 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 14 and 15 (envelope). Scenes 4, 6, 9, 15 (locker). Scenes 10, 13, 14 (characters).
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "the thick relocation envelope full of bank notes resting in his inside breast pocket" / Scene 15: "the one whose inside pocket had carried that thick relocation savings envelope earmarked for timber yards and an escape to Hull"
- **Problem:** The envelope is re-explained with its full tag in about nine scenes. "Locker B-14 … bottom ventilation louver" and "the blue tin cigar box" get the same treatment almost everywhere. Scene 10 re-describes Gable as if she were new ("whose … administrative zeal had terrorized three successive generations of orderlies"). Scene 13 re-introduces Gary ("Gary, that lanky, perpetually grinning former porter"). Scene 14 introduces Clara from scratch ("stood Clara Miller—Julian's elder sister by fourteen tempestuous months"), although she already had a full scene in Scene 3. This is the clearest sign of the stitching.
- **Reader needed:** Trust that the reader remembers the envelope, the locker and the cast, so later scenes can refer to them in a word or two.

### 4. [major] Names, pronouns and physical details change between scenes
- **Where:** Scene 1 vs Scene 5. Scene 1 vs Scene 4. Scene 2 vs Scene 16. Scene 4 vs Scene 5.
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "I know Julian Fromm's tidy little hand when I see it" (elsewhere "Julian Miller"). Scene 16: "If he finds you here while you're under suspension" (Gable is "she" everywhere else).
- **Problem:** The tin is decorated with "Caribbean palm fronds" in Scene 1 and "Havana tobacco leaves" in Scene 4. Julian's savings are "five-pound notes" in Scene 1 and "twenty-pound notes" in Scene 3. His handwriting is "clumsy" in Scene 4 but "remarkably neat" in Scene 5. A careful reader stops at each change and wonders whether it is a clue.
- **Reader needed:** One stable name, pronoun and description for each person and object from start to finish.

### 5. [major] A broken sentence at the end of Scene 10
- **Where:** Scene 10
- **Quote:** "hurried straight up the service stairs, tucked beneath the bottom shelf of locker B-14 where he could finally slide the salvaged tin onto the quiet desk and draw a ragged breath."
- **Problem:** The sentence has Julian hurrying upstairs *and* tucked under a locker shelf *and* at a desk all at once. It reads like two drafts spliced together, and it stops the reader cold.
- **Reader needed:** One clear place where Julian ends up.

### 6. [minor] The narrator states the reveal and the feelings outright
- **Where:** Scene 12, Scene 13, Scene 14 (also Scene 15)
- **Quote:** Scene 12: "It was unmistakably Neil who had taken Gary's abandoned locker-slot and kept answering every folded sheet in that urgent, secret hand, concealing his lowly porter's badge behind borrowed phrases" / Scene 13: "A profound, sick betrayal washed through him"
- **Problem:** At the story's biggest moments, the narration summarises what the reader has just seen or is about to see. Scene 13 repeats the same summary: "He had concealed his identity as a mere uncredentialed night porter, sustaining the romantic correspondence that Gary had started in jest". Scene 14 does it again: "And it was, he realized with a sharp, hollowing certainty, a total retreat." The feelings are named rather than dramatised.
- **Reader needed:** Let the characters' actions and dialogue carry the reveal and the betrayal, without narrator recap.

### 7. [minor] Expository and recapping dialogue, with repeated verbal tics
- **Where:** Scene 16 (also Scenes 2, 6, 3)
- **Quote:** Scene 16: "Julian, Vance wrote that first note as a joke! And I kept it up because I was terrified you'd see I was only a greasy night porter"
- **Problem:** Neil explains to Julian things they both learned in Scene 13. Gable uses the same odd phrase twice: "Let me be entirely transparent" (Scene 2) and "Am I making myself adequately transparent" (Scene 6). The narration picks it up again in Scene 9 ("made a man feel entirely transparent"). Tags lean on adverbs: "cut in smoothly", "added gently", "said quietly".
- **Reader needed:** Dialogue that moves the exchange forward instead of restating the plot, with each character's catchphrase used once.

### 8. [nit] Word-level repetition inside sentences
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 11, Scene 14 (also Scene 12)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "the damp, subterranean dignity of a root cellar … the faint, persistent sulfur of subterranean boiler pipes" / Scene 14: "the sour pique of a cook discovering sour milk"
- **Problem:** Scene 11 has "scrawling a sharp, tearing stroke … before tearing it free". Scene 12 has "subterranean arteries" and "subterranean grating" in consecutive paragraphs. "Damp" shows up in nearly every scene's setting description.
- **Reader needed:** Word-level variety, so the texture doesn't feel machine-repeated.

## What works
- The dry, specific comic voice works when it is used sparingly: "a brass key that looked like it had been chewed on by an apologetic dog" (Scene 1).
- Some dialogue has snap and character: "Well, well. Look at Romeo amongst the acid drums." (Scene 5), and the closing line "Then you can buy me a thoroughly unpoetic cup of tea across the street before Gable wakes up." (Scene 16).
- The wren is described with concrete, tender detail that earns its later weight: "each feather suggested by light cross-hatched incisions along the flanks, its belly sanded to a warm, buttery smoothness" (Scene 7).

## Out of lane
Plot continuity breaks: Scene 14 has Julian refusing Clara the envelope but Scene 16 has her taking a decoy one, and the inspection is "next Thursday" in Scene 6 but "less than an hour" away in Scene 9.
