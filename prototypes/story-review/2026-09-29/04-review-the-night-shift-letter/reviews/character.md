# Character Coherence — The Night Shift Letter

**Score: 3/5** — Julian's central choice (to go from feeling betrayed to giving up Hull) happens off the page with nothing to trigger it, and Gable works purely as an obstacle. Neil, Gary and Clara are distinct people with their own voices.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Julian gets over the betrayal and gives up Hull between scenes, with nothing on the page to cause it
- **Where:** Scene 13 → Scene 14 (and Scene 16)
- **Quote:** Scene 13: "Julian looked down at the creased paper, but he could not bring himself to touch it… Julian could only stand beneath the yellow dock lamps, entirely undone." Scene 14: "And it was, he realized with a sharp, hollowing certainty, a total retreat."
- **Problem:** Julian refuses Neil's confession and leaves the scene "undone". In the very next scene he is ready to give up a two-year plan for Neil, and the narrator simply announces the realisation. Nothing comes between the two scenes to change his mind: he doesn't read the letter, doesn't talk to anyone, and remembers nothing new. The reason he gives relies on "Neil's tears on the loading bay", but Scene 13 shows no tears, only Neil flushing and looking at the floor. By Scene 16 the betrayal has "shriveled from a tragedy into something distinctly absurd", which again is stated, not shown.
- **Reader needed:** One thing on the page that moves Julian from hurt to curiosity, such as reading the confession or recalling a particular line of Neil's, before he risks the lease on it.

### 2. [major] Julian secretly lets Clara lose the lease without a flicker, which goes against both Scene 14 and who he has been
- **Where:** Scene 16 (against Scenes 3 and 14)
- **Quote:** Scene 16: "Clara took an envelope stuffed with yesterday's manifest duplicates… the lease expired at midnight. I forfeited it, Neil." Scene 14: "'I can't give it to you, Clara,' Julian said."
- **Problem:** In Scene 14 Julian refuses Clara openly and honestly. In Scene 16 we learn he fooled her with a fake envelope, and he tells Neil this with "a wry, crooked twitch of his mouth." This is the same man who gave Clara "my word" (Scene 3) and guarded her deposit like a sacred trust. Letting her lose her own three hundred pounds without a word is a large betrayal of her. Julian shows no guilt about it, and the story never shows Clara's reaction.
- **Reader needed:** A reason Julian would deceive Clara instead of telling her, and some sign that he feels what it costs her.

### 3. [minor] Neil knows things he had no way of learning
- **Where:** Scene 16
- **Quote:** "'You have to. The workshop. Clara took the envelope—'"
- **Problem:** Neil wasn't in the dispatch office in Scene 14, and nothing shows him meeting Clara or hearing about the handover. He talks as if he watched it happen.
- **Reader needed:** Some route by which Neil heard about Clara and the envelope, or a line where he only guesses at it.

### 4. [minor] Gable is only an obstacle and turns up wherever the plot needs her
- **Where:** Scene 2 (repeats in Scenes 6, 10 and 11)
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "Gable—the day-side terror… halted mid-stride, her clipboard instantly poised like a shield." Scene 11: "You lost track of your employment, which is rather more serious."
- **Problem:** Every time Gable appears, she either states or enforces the lockout rule. She is a day supervisor, yet she is patrolling the basement near 2 a.m. (Scene 6), the dispatch office after eight (Scene 11) and the dawn raid (Scene 10), and no reason is given. The narration piles on similes for her but never gives her a want, a weakness or a moment of doubt. She exists to get in Julian's way.
- **Reader needed:** One touch of Gable's own motive or pressure on her, such as the committee leaning on her, that explains why she is everywhere.

### 5. [minor] Gary breaks his sworn promise without noticing he's doing it
- **Where:** Scene 13 (against Scene 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "Mum's the word. I won't breathe a whisper to Julian or anyone else." Scene 13: "'The note, mate! Locker fourteen!' Gary beamed… 'I left that purple, flowery nonsense jammed behind the mesh…'"
- **Problem:** In Scene 5 Neil's desperate plea visibly shakes Gary ("the easy smirk… wavering into something closer to discomfort"). In Scene 13 he tells Julian everything the moment he sees him, with no hesitation and no memory of his promise. The Gary who can be moved has gone.
- **Reader needed:** A beat where Gary thinks the secret is already out, or catches himself too late, so that his blurting fits the man who promised.

### 6. [minor] Julian stops caring about the money whenever the plot needs him in the building
- **Where:** Scene 11 and Scene 12 (against Scenes 3–4)
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "Julian kept one hand clamped firmly over the thick relocation envelope… all the way down the stairs." Scene 12: "Julian did not leave. Instead, he slipped downward…"
- **Problem:** Julian is still ninety-five pounds short and waiting for Friday's wages, and he has been told a single breach means dismissal. Even so, he waits two hours past lockout. Then, already suspended, he ignores a direct order that risks dismissal "without references", and he doesn't weigh Clara or the deposit once until the damage is done.
- **Reader needed:** A moment where Julian weighs the risk to the deposit and chooses the correspondent anyway.

### 7. [minor] Julian turns suave and quippy in the last scene
- **Where:** Scene 16 (against Scenes 2, 10 and 11)
- **Quote:** Scene 16: "'He can suspend me until the plumbing rots,' Julian cut in smoothly… 'Splendid,' Julian murmured." Scene 11: "'Ma'am,' Julian choked out… 'I was—there was a late requisition slip for the north ward—'"
- **Problem:** Until now Julian has been weary, flustered and a poor liar ("an innocence so strenuous it bordered on theatrical"). In the final scene he is suddenly cool and witty, and he even borrows Gable's word "Splendid". The narrator's scorn for "theatrical gestures" gets handed to him too. It reads as a different man, not one who has grown.
- **Reader needed:** Julian's new ease showing through some leftover awkwardness, so it feels like his own.

## What works
- Neil's plea in Scene 5 gives Gary an inner life for a moment and makes Neil's feelings real before the reveal: "Gary blinked, the easy smirk on his face wavering into something closer to discomfort… 'Bloody hell, Neil. You're serious.'"
- Neil's guilt is shown through his hands and the wren, not just named: "He stroked the cedar wing once more, enveloped in the soft fragrance of shavings that smelled hopelessly of sunlit timber" (Scene 8).
- Clara's voice is sharp and her own: "I won't just kill you, Julian—I'll make you build your own pine box first" (Scene 3).

## Out of lane
Consistency: Clara is a business partner in Scene 3 but his sister in Scenes 7 and 14; Julian is "Miller" and also "Fromm"; his handwriting is "clumsy" (Scene 4) and "remarkably neat" (Scene 5); Neil calls Gable "he" (Scene 16).
