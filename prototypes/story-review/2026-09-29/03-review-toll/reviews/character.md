# Character Coherence — Toll

**Score: 3/5** — the climactic choice (Hettie tripping the scour valve) is unmotivated on the page: Scenes 6–9 methodically take her reasons away, and nothing in Scene 10 gives them back.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Hettie drains the lake after every scene has argued her out of it
- **Where:** Scene 10 (setup in Scenes 6, 7, 8, 9)
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "Hettie closed her hand around the cold brass pin in her coat, unable to answer." Then Scene 10: "She set the pin against the linkage shear-collar and swung the heavy lead mallet."
- **Problem:** The story spends four scenes wearing down her grievance. Arthur turns out to be poor and dutiful (Scene 6). He "had signed to buy them daylight" (Scene 7). He is terrified for "forty miles of downstream settlement" (Scene 8). Clara tells her plainly that "Five thousand souls" will lose their water (Scene 9). Hettie is left speechless. Then, with no scene of decision and no new pressure, she does the thing anyway. We never learn whether she is getting revenge in spite of all that, or releasing water to stop the crest overtopping. Scene 11 calls it "any decree of vengeance", so the flood-relief reading isn't intended.
- **Reader needed:** One visible beat in Scene 10 showing why she chooses this now, against what she has just learned. It could be spite that wins out, a twisted reason, or a misjudgment, but it has to be hers.

### 2. [major] Arthur helps the act he has most feared
- **Where:** Scene 10 (setup in Scene 8)
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "every orchard, every bridge, the municipal intakes—will be carried into the gorge". Scene 10: "Arthur did not lunge for the trip-lock… He wasn't fighting the release".
- **Problem:** The Arthur of Scene 8 shakes with fear for the downstream towns and their intakes. The canal needs "at least two hundred meters" of pool (Scene 4). Yet when she empties the reservoir he makes no attempt to stop her. He only damps the surge, and the next morning he takes the blame. His non-resistance is asserted, not explained, and so is his silence about the five thousand now without water.
- **Reader needed:** Some sign of Arthur's reasoning in the moment: guilt, exhaustion, a judgment that the trip is already irreversible, or relief that the jammed spillway now has an outlet.

### 3. [minor] Arthur's "recognition" contradicts what he already knows
- **Where:** Scene 10 (and Scenes 1, 8)
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "The log was yours from yesterday's micrometer sweep, Rann". Scene 10: "I am Hettie Rann." / "The long years of vigil collapsed in his eyes into a single, terrible recognition".
- **Problem:** Arthur called her "Hettie" to her face in Scene 1 and "Rann" in Scene 8. The reveal plays as though he never knew who she was. The reader can't tell what Arthur knew or suspected, so his big reaction doesn't land.
- **Reader needed:** Clarity on whether Arthur had failed to connect "Rann" with Low Meadow, or had known all along. Either way, a reaction that fits.

### 4. [minor] The grievance is rewritten at the climax
- **Where:** Scene 10 (and Scenes 3, 11)
- **Quote:** Scene 10: "And my father drowned his team trying to drag the hay wagons out of the back forty before your sluices opened." Scene 11: "her mother had boiled bitter tea and her brother had mended ox harness".
- **Problem:** For twenty years her motive has been the land: "her grandfather's barn" and "four generations". A father who died in the flood, and a brother, appear only now, at the moment of accusation. That makes her wound look assembled for the scene rather than carried through the story. It also cuts against Scene 3, where no family death is mourned.
- **Reader needed:** The father's death, if it drives her, planted and felt early. Otherwise it has no place in the climax.

### 5. [minor] Clara appears and changes side offstage
- **Where:** Scene 9 (and Scene 11)
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "stood Clara, her elder sister, who had … sworn never to cast a backward glance at the drowning valley." Scene 11: "Clara has your transit pass."
- **Problem:** Clara's want is to move on and never look back, yet she is standing in the dam's control room, having gone through Hettie's sea-chest, with no reason given for being there. In Scene 11 she is working with Arthur to get the sister whose act she condemned out of the country, and we see none of that turn.
- **Reader needed:** One line explaining why Clara came to the dam, and some trace of what she felt after the lake was drained.

### 6. [minor] Hettie is unmoved by the cost she was warned about
- **Where:** Scene 11
- **Quote:** "She had broken the lock. She had drained the tomb. Yet the house was still drowned, because the people who had breathed beneath its rafters were dead or dispersed".
- **Problem:** Her realization is only about the lost past. The towns that Clara said would "draw air and yellow mud" are never mentioned. A woman left "unable to answer" by that argument would surely think of them now. The narrator also announces the arc's lesson rather than letting the dropped stone carry it.
- **Reader needed:** Some response from Hettie to the people she harmed, even denial, so her final calm matches the scale of what she did.

### 7. [minor] Orson Dale is a pin dispenser
- **Where:** Scene 5
- **Quote:** "the emergency bypass pin milled to override the sediment scour gates without ministerial key-codes—and laid it upon the damp stone between them."
- **Problem:** Dale's greed is visible, but it's never explained why he would offer an inspector this particular dangerous object as his side of a bribe. He doesn't know what she wants. He exists to put the tool in her hand and then disappears, apart from a passing mention in Scene 8.
- **Reader needed:** A reason Dale thinks the pin is something she'd want, or a reason he wants it gone.

### 8. [nit] Everyone speaks the same carved register
- **Where:** Scenes 3, 9, 11
- **Quote:** Hettie: "I will not sleep under a gift of their paper." Arthur: "It is not martyrdom… It is clerical accuracy." Clara: "They belong to an arsonist."
- **Problem:** Everyone speaks in the same terse, aphoristic, faintly archaic lines. Only Dale's transactional patter and Arthur's technical talk in Scene 8 sound different.
- **Reader needed:** Clearer differences in how each sibling and Arthur talk. Clara in particular could sound more homely and practical.

## What works
- Arthur's reversal in Scene 6 is shown through what Hettie sees, not narrated: "In his lap lay his working coat, its seams split at the elbows … which he drew together with an iron needle and waxed cobbler's thread".
- Hettie burning the voucher in Scene 3 sets up her pride and her vow cleanly and at personal cost: "'That was our roof.' / 'I will not sleep under a gift of their paper.'"
- The archive discovery in Scene 7 dramatizes her doubt through physical detail: "pressed so hard into the soft carbon sheet that the steel nib had torn the blue paper".

## Out of lane
The pin (brass vs. "counterfeit steel"), the hearthstone (basalt, schist, lintel, mantel, "brass hearthstone ring"), and twenty vs. "thirty years" are inconsistent. Handing these to consistency.
