# Logical Consistency — Toll

**Score: 2/5** — Several major contradictions: a recognition scene undone by earlier naming, a drowned father and a brother who were never there, a decree scroll found where it never went, and a keepsake stone that changes material and origin in almost every scene. The reader starts to distrust details.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Arthur "recognises" a woman he has been calling by name all along
- **Where:** Scene 10 (against Scenes 1, 5, 8)
- **Quote:** Scene 10: "\"...before your sluices opened. I am Hettie Rann.\" Arthur's head came up... The long years of vigil collapsed in his eyes into a single, terrible recognition"
- **Earlier:** Scene 1: "\"The diversion sluices upriver drop at dusk, Hettie,\" Arthur said"; Scene 8: "\"The log was yours from yesterday's micrometer sweep, Rann,\" he said"
- **Problem:** Arthur knew her by first name at the clearance, and in Scene 8 he addresses her by her family name. (Dale also calls her "Rann" in Scene 5.) Her revealing her name therefore can't be news to him. Scene 8's mention of her "draft-name" hints at an alias, but the name he actually uses is her real one.
- **Reader needed:** a reason Arthur hadn't connected "Rann" with the Low Meadow Ranns, or for her to be working under a different name.

### 2. [major] The condemnation scroll turns up in the drowned kitchen, though it never entered the house
- **Where:** Scene 11 (against Scene 1)
- **Quote:** Scene 11: "a rot-hardened pigskin tube... the signed valley condemnation scroll... that Arthur Vane had dropped upon the walnut table twenty winters ago"
- **Earlier:** Scene 1: "holding the Signed Valley Condemnation Scroll in a horn cylinder"; "He did not extend the paper toward her"; "the weight of the horn case settling into his coat"
- **Problem:** In Scene 1 Arthur stays in the yard, never offers the scroll, puts the case back in his coat and rides off. The container has also changed from horn to pigskin.
- **Reader needed:** the scroll to have been left at the farm in Scene 1, or not to reappear at the hearth.

### 3. [major] Family members appear after the fact: a drowned father and a brother
- **Where:** Scene 10 and Scene 11 (against Scenes 1–3)
- **Quote:** Scene 10: "And my father drowned his team trying to drag the hay wagons out of the back forty before your sluices opened." Scene 11: "her brother had mended ox harness before the flood took the pastures"
- **Earlier:** Scenes 1–3 show only Hettie and Clara at the farm. Clara loads the only wagon and brings the draft horse up safely ("the panic of the draft horse she had hauled up the switchbacks"). The land is repeatedly called her "grandfather's".
- **Problem:** No father, brother or death appears anywhere in the eviction, the flooding or the sisters' talk on the ridge. Each is first mentioned twenty years later. In Scene 11 Arthur also speaks of "your grandfather... planting winter rye" as if the grandfather were alive and present that day.
- **Reader needed:** the father and brother to be at least present or mentioned in Scenes 1–3, especially a death Clara would surely mention.

### 4. [major] The keepsake stone changes material, source and holder every time it appears
- **Where:** Scene 3; recurs in Scenes 7, 8, 10, 11
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "rough, rectangular hunk of split basalt she had pried from the bread-oven corner before Arthur Vane's bailiffs had set their horses"; Scene 7: "the soot-blackened corner chipped from her father's kitchen lintel"
- **Later:** Scene 10: "the blackened brass hearthstone ring hung against her collar"; Scene 11: "the chipped square of river schist she had pried from the mantel the dawn the sluices first closed"
- **Problem:** The stone moves from basalt (bread-oven) to lintel to brass ring worn at the collar to schist (mantel). The time she took it moves from before the bailiffs arrived to "the dawn the sluices first closed", although the sluices dropped at dusk. Within Scene 11 it is first "in her canvas kit" and then drawn "from her pocket".
- **Reader needed:** one fixed stone, from one place, taken at one moment, and carried in one place.

### 5. [major] "Twenty years later" becomes thirty
- **Where:** Scene 4 and Scene 5 (against Scene 4's opening and Scenes 6–11)
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "Twenty years later..."; "Nobody questioned a woman with thirty years of river geometry in her marrow"; Scene 5: "the pin offered what three decades of petitions could not"
- **Problem:** Everywhere else the gap is twenty years or "twenty winters", so "thirty years" and "three decades" clash with the story's own timeline.
- **Reader needed:** one consistent span.

### 6. [major] The archive telex was put back, yet she produces it, in a different form
- **Where:** Scene 10 (against Scene 7); related slip in Scene 9
- **Quote:** Scene 7: "Hettie folded the tissue back into its card sleeve... she slipped the folder back between the moldered logs"; Scene 10: "pulled out the yellowed paper preserved inside an oilskin sleeve... the purple mimeograph ink... the provincial water decree ordering the expropriation"
- **Problem:** She leaves the telex in the archive, then brings it out in Scene 10 with no scene of her going back for it. The document has also changed: blue carbon tissue telex with a torn signature has become a yellowed, purple mimeographed decree. Similarly, Scene 9's "survey sheets Hettie had smuggled from the records vault" were never shown being taken. Clara then finds them via "the lock on your sea-chest" when they were "hidden beneath the straw pallet".
- **Reader needed:** to see her take the papers, and for each document to keep its description.

### 7. [minor] The bypass pin's material, origin and use keep changing
- **Where:** Scene 6; recurs in Scenes 9, 10, 11
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "a heavy, squared bar of cold brass"; Scene 6: "the twin to the cold counterfeit steel now pressed into her ribcage"; Scene 9: "the heavy brass rod she had fraudulently requisitioned"; Clara: "I know what you took from the maintenance shop"
- **Problem:** Dale hands her a genuine brass pin in exchange for her signature. It is later called counterfeit steel, "stolen", requisitioned, and taken from the maintenance shop. In Scene 11 she carries "the steel wrench she had ground down to slip the bypass pin", a tool never mentioned before. In Scene 10 she struck the pin with a lead mallet, yet Scene 11 says a steel tool "served to strike down forty meters of water".
- **Reader needed:** one pin, obtained one way, driven home with one tool.

### 8. [minor] Water depths, light and elapsed time don't add up
- **Where:** Scenes 3, 9, 10, 11
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "thirty cubits of slack water"; Scene 9: "beneath forty fathoms of dead rain"; Scene 10: "eighty fathoms under the silt"; Scene 11: "twenty hours earlier had fought the scour gate's counterweights" in "raw dawn air"
- **Problem:** The same water ranges from 30 cubits to 40 cubits to 40 fathoms to 80 fathoms, a difference of almost ten times. Scene 3 happens after dusk yet she lifts the stone "into the gray daylight". The trip happens at a "midnight" alarm, and twenty hours after midnight is evening, not dawn.
- **Reader needed:** stable measurements, and elapsed times that match the stated time of day.

## What works
- The one-way scour-valve rule is set up and then honoured. Scene 2: "the ballast would drop free and swing the scour valve irrevocably open"; Scene 10: "once the trip-lever disengaged under full head, the counterbalance dropped and the purge was irrevocable."
- The canal-sill number is fixed and reused correctly. Scene 4: "a reservoir pool elevation of at least two hundred meters"; Scene 9: "She knew the number before Clara spoke it."
- Arthur's refusal of the disbursement in Scene 6 ("Divert to third-tier sluice replacement") fits the protest note on the telex in Scene 7 ("Signed under protest, 11:42 AM").

## Out of lane
Clara's warning in Scene 9 about five thousand people losing water is never answered once the reservoir drains (stakes/character).
