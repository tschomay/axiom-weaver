# Setup and Payoff — Toll

**Score: 2/5** — the mechanical setups (the irreversible scour trip, the stolen pin, the 200-metre sill, the hearthstone vow) are planted with care, but the climax is unearned. Scenes 6–9 take apart Hettie's motive and put a price on the act. Then she does it anyway, with no scene showing her choose. The price (the canal and the five thousand people downstream) is never paid or even mentioned again.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The decision to trip the scour is never earned after the story takes away its reasons
- **Where:** Scene 9 → Scene 10 (and Scenes 6, 7, 8)
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "The silence that followed fell hard and absolute across the dials. Hettie closed her hand around the cold brass pin in her coat, unable to answer." Scene 10: "She set the pin against the linkage shear-collar and swung the heavy lead mallet."
- **Problem:** Three scenes in a row take apart her grievance: Arthur's refused bonus ("The stone in her pocket felt suddenly heavy"), the telex ("less like a weapon now than an unearned verdict") and his terror in the pit. Then Clara names the cost. The next scene opens with Hettie already at the lever. She confronts Arthur with the telex, which vindicates him, and pulls the pin in the same beat, so the act looks less motivated than it was in Scene 3. The flood alarm and jammed spillway gates could have made the release a rescue, but the story never connects them, so the reader cannot tell whether this is vengeance, mercy, or both.
- **Reader needed:** A moment where Hettie weighs what she now knows against the pin, or a clear line showing that the release answers the overtopping crisis rather than her old vow.

### 2. [major] The central cost, the canal running dry for five thousand people, never pays off
- **Where:** Scene 4, Scene 9 → Scenes 10–11
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "If the lake surface dropped a single hand's-breadth below that contour, the canal mouth would starve on thin air, and every ditch between the gorges and the sea would run dry." Scene 9: "Five thousand people downstream will turn their taps and draw air and yellow mud."
- **Problem:** This is the story's most carefully planted consequence: set up in Scene 4, and turned into Clara's whole argument in Scene 9. Then Scene 11 opens with "The basin was empty. The lake was gone." and never says what happened downstream. Arthur's line "I let forty thousand acres drown to spare five thousand throats" reuses the number for a different set of people, which blurs the point further.
- **Reader needed:** Some sign, however brief, of whether the canal starved and what that cost, since the moral weight of the ending depends on it.

### 3. [major] "I am Hettie Rann" is played as a recognition reveal, but Arthur has known her all along
- **Where:** Scene 10 (and Scenes 1, 8)
- **Quote:** Scene 10: "I am Hettie Rann." / "The long years of vigil collapsed in his eyes into a single, terrible recognition". Earlier, Scene 1: "The diversion sluices upriver drop at dusk, Hettie," and Scene 8: "The log was yours from yesterday's micrometer sweep, Rann,"
- **Problem:** Arthur calls her by name in Scene 1 and by her surname in Scene 8, and nothing suggests she is hiding who she is (Scene 8's "draft-name" is never followed up). So the climax reveals something the reader was never told was secret. The beat reads as a double reveal that goes nowhere.
- **Reader needed:** Either an early sign that Arthur doesn't know who she is (an alias, a failure to recognise her), or a Scene 10 beat that doesn't depend on recognition.

### 4. [major] The cavitation crisis builds to a deadline and then disappears
- **Where:** Scene 8 (and Scene 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "Three weeks, at the current head," … "If the thrust bearing shears, the water will drown the lower turbine deck in six seconds"
- **Problem:** A whole scene sets up an imminent turbine failure with a deadline and a named villain, Dale, who wants to run it to freeze-up. It also turns Scene 5's "padded" cavitation fraud into a real danger. None of this comes back: the turbine, the deadline, Dale and the fraud Hettie signed all go unmentioned. The scene works as a character beat for Arthur, but the danger it plants is dropped.
- **Reader needed:** A payoff for the turbine threat (for example, draining the lake defuses it, or it drives the Scene 10 emergency), or a lower-stakes framing for the scene.

### 5. [minor] The father's drowning is a new grievance introduced at the climax
- **Where:** Scene 10
- **Quote:** "And my father drowned his team trying to drag the hay wagons out of the back forty before your sluices opened."
- **Problem:** Scene 1 shows only the two sisters loading the wagon, and Scene 9 mentions only "the ground we buried Mother in." A father who died in the flood would be the heaviest grievance in the story, yet it appears for the first time in the confrontation line, and here "my father drowned his team" leaves unclear whether he died or only his team. It feels added for impact rather than planted.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier mention of the father's loss, so the accusation lands as a callback rather than as new information.

### 6. [minor] The drowned scroll is a callback to an object the story took away
- **Where:** Scene 11 (and Scene 1)
- **Quote:** Scene 11: "the signed valley condemnation scroll, the provincial expropriation decree that Arthur Vane had dropped upon the walnut table twenty winters ago". Scene 1: "He did not extend the paper toward her … the weight of the horn case settling into his coat"
- **Problem:** The image of the decree rotting in the hearth is meant to close the loop with Scene 1, but Scene 1 carefully shows Arthur keeping it. The callback relies on a handover the reader saw not happen.
- **Reader needed:** Either Scene 1 leaves the scroll behind, or Scene 11 finds a different relic.

### 7. [minor] Arthur's motive is revealed three times, and the ending's meaning is told rather than shown
- **Where:** Scene 7, Scene 10, Scene 11
- **Quote:** Scene 7: "He had not signed to conquer them. He had signed to buy them daylight." Scene 11: "I signed the sluice orders because they held dynamite over the gorge." / "She had broken the lock. She had drained the tomb. Yet the house was still drowned"
- **Problem:** The telex reveal lands well in Scene 7, then Hettie repeats it to Arthur in Scene 10, then Arthur repeats it again in Scene 11. The closing lesson is spelled out by the narrator, even though the hearthstone "squelched into the gray ooze" already carries it.
- **Reader needed:** One clear reveal, with the ending left to the dropped stone and "There is nothing here to take back."

### 8. [nit] Two smaller threads are raised and never answered
- **Where:** Scene 3, Scene 7
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "The commissioner's clerk at the tollgate gave me the pair." Scene 7: "records that might reveal whether the old spillway foundation could withstand a sudden, unthrottled hydraulic draw down"
- **Problem:** Hettie burns one slip, but we never learn what happened to the second. She broke into the archive to answer a specific engineering question, and the answer is never given.
- **Reader needed:** Close these off, or cut them.

## What works
- The irreversible scour trip is planted early and paid off exactly. Scene 2 says "the ballast would drop free and swing the scour valve irrevocably open", and Scene 10 delivers "the purge was irrevocable."
- The 200-metre sill is planted by Hettie's own hands, so Clara's argument lands fairly: "She knew the number before Clara spoke it; she had measured the brass sill plate in the inspection gallery with her own calipers."
- The hearthstone vow and its reversal form a clean arc. Scene 3 has "I will not lay down my tools until the hearthstones stand in open air", and Scene 11 answers it with "It did not ring with iron authority; it merely squelched into the gray ooze and vanished from sight."

## Out of lane
The hearthstone keeps changing: basalt from the bread-oven, then the kitchen lintel, then schist from the mantel, then a "ring" at her collar. The pin is brass but also "counterfeit steel". These are consistency issues.
