# Prose Craft — Toll

**Score: 2/5** — The sentences have texture, but a small stock of words and gestures (tallow, guttering, the stone in the pocket) comes back in almost every scene. The names of objects and their materials change from scene to scene, so a reader keeps noticing where one scene was stitched to the next.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The same stone-in-the-pocket beat appears in six scenes in a row, and the stone keeps changing
- **Where:** Scene 4, and again in Scenes 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "The stone in her coat pocket, worn smooth by twenty winters of turning fingers, was cool against her ribs." Scene 6: "The stone in her pocket felt suddenly heavy, cold as a tombstone."
- **Problem:** From Scene 4 to Scene 9, every scene ends on the stone, or comes back to it near the end, and in Scenes 5, 7, 8 and 9 it is pressed against the pin ("behind the smooth weight of the hearthstone", "knocked against the brass pin", "sat wedged against the cold brass", "weighed cold and unforgiving against her father's hearthstone"). Each scene also describes the stone differently. In Scene 3 it is "split basalt she had pried from the bread-oven corner". In Scene 7 it is "the soot-blackened corner chipped from her father's kitchen lintel". In Scene 10 it is a "blackened brass hearthstone ring hung against her collar". In Scene 11 it is "river schist she had pried from the mantel".
- **Reader needed:** The motif should appear rarely enough to keep its weight, and the stone should stay the same object each time it comes back.

### 2. [major] "Tallow" and "guttering" repeated throughout
- **Where:** "Tallow" appears 9 times across Scenes 2, 3 (twice), 4, 6, 7, 9 and 11 (twice). "Guttering"/"guttered" appears 6 times across Scenes 1, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9.
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "quiet as spilled tallow spreading across an uneven floor" … "like tallow sliding down a choked throat". Scene 11: "sinking through the crust into the frigid tallow beneath" … "reduced to tallowy pulp".
- **Problem:** Tallow ends up standing for water, mud, pulp, smell and light alike. Scene 3 uses it twice as a simile within a few lines. Scene 7 has an electric bulb whose glass bell "guttered", and Scene 4 a lamp filament giving "a guttering amber cone". The words get used whether or not they fit, and a reader starts counting them.
- **Reader needed:** Different sensory words for different things, and no simile repeated inside a single scene.

### 3. [major] The period, materials and units change between scenes
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10 and 11
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "a heavy, squared bar of cold brass". Scene 6: "the twin to the cold counterfeit steel now pressed into her ribcage". Scene 10: "the turned brass bypass pin".
- **Problem:** The pin is brass, then steel, then "turned" brass. The decree is carried in a "horn cylinder" in Scene 1 and found in a "pigskin tube" in Scene 11. The flood depth is given as "thirty cubits", "forty cubits", "forty fathoms", "eighty fathoms", "forty meters" and "two hundred feet". Scene 1's horsemen, parchment and "five fathoms" sit beside a "lorry" in Scene 3, "telemetry" in Scene 9, and "turn their taps" and "fifty atmospheres" elsewhere. Each scene sounds as if it had its own style sheet.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent set of words for the story's period and technology, so the physical world stays the same from scene to scene.

### 4. [minor] Labels leak into the prose, and scenes re-introduce people and objects the reader already knows
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 2, 4, 6 and 9
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "holding the Signed Valley Condemnation Scroll in a horn cylinder". Scene 4: "Down in the belly of the Lower Powerhouse and Scour Tunnel Gallery". Scene 9: "stood Clara, her elder sister, who had packed the kitchen pots into straw twenty years ago".
- **Problem:** Title-cased labels like these read like inventory entries, not names people would use (Scene 2's "the Sullivant Dam Crest" is another). Scene 6 re-introduces Arthur ("the ministerial bailiff who had once stamped her family's land into oblivion"). Scene 9 re-explains the pin ("the heavy brass rod she had fraudulently requisitioned to trip the scour gate") and re-introduces Clara as if she had not already appeared. Scene 4 renames the protagonist as "Hettie Rann … a licensed hydraulic maintenance specialist". Every one of these is a point where the reader can see a new model call starting.
- **Reader needed:** Names that sound like what people would actually say, and trust that the reader remembers who and what was introduced earlier.

### 5. [minor] The narrator states Hettie's change of heart instead of dramatising it
- **Where:** Scenes 6, 7, 8 and 11
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "The man she had built in her heart for twenty winters—an opulent executioner growing fat on the drowning of Low Meadow—was nowhere to be found." Scene 11: "She had broken the lock. She had drained the tomb. Yet the house was still drowned".
- **Problem:** The coat-mending image in Scene 6 already makes the point on its own. The narrator then spells it out, and does the same again in Scene 7 ("For twenty years she had carried the image…"), Scene 8 ("the righteous certainty that had once weighted them felt fractured") and Scene 11, where the explanation uses a triplet shape. After four of these, the reader has been told the arc more than shown it.
- **Reader needed:** One of these summaries at most. The concrete images should carry the rest.

### 6. [minor] The "not X; Y" construction becomes a habit
- **Where:** Scenes 6, 7, 8, 10 and 11
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "His gaze was not that of an overseer checking an asset; it was the hollow, staring horror of a sentry who saw the wall giving way." Scene 10: "He wasn't fighting the release; he was straining to brake the violent hydraulic shock wave".
- **Problem:** The same construction returns in Scene 7 ("He had not signed to conquer them. He had signed to buy them daylight."), Scene 11 ("It is not martyrdom … It is clerical accuracy.") and Scene 6 ("He was not drinking wine or counting the spoils"). Scenes 3, 7 and 11 also open the same way: "The black water did not surge", "The cold did not lift", "The mud did not release its dead". The rhythm is easy to predict.
- **Reader needed:** More varied sentence shapes, especially in scene openings and at turning points.

### 7. [minor] Metaphors that tangle, and slips an editor would catch
- **Where:** Scenes 3, 4, 7 and 9
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "a dead gray shoulder of dressed granite that shouldered the mountains apart" and "the river was climbing its upper courses course by course". Scene 7: "leafed past surveyor surveys".
- **Problem:** Other examples are "billions of cubic tons" (Scene 9) and "Above her vaulted skull" (Scene 4). In Scene 3, the dam that was "ashlar and poured lime" in Scene 2 becomes "granite" and is then compared to three bodily images in two sentences. These are the moments that make a reader reread.
- **Reader needed:** One clean image per beat, with no echo words.

### 8. [minor] Dialogue that exists to deliver information
- **Where:** Scenes 3, 9 and 11
- **Quote:** Scene 9 (Clara): "The municipal canal intake sits perched at two hundred meters elevation. Not an inch lower. It relies on the gravity of the full pool to feed every ditch, cistern, and conduit". Scene 11 (Arthur): "I let forty thousand acres drown to spare five thousand throats, and in the end, I built an altar out of fear."
- **Problem:** In a quarrel between sisters, Clara recites a specification. Arthur sums up his own theme out loud. Hettie's vow in Scene 3, "I will not lay down my tools until the hearthstones stand in open air", sounds like an oath from a stage play, not something said by a wet, exhausted woman.
- **Reader needed:** Speech that sounds like these people under this much pressure. Numbers and themes can reach the reader some other way.

## What works
- The small physical gestures that reveal character are sharp and specific: "he held it between his palms like a measure of meal that had come up short" (Scene 1). "pulling each stitch tight with a sharp, habitual jerk of his shoulder" (Scene 6).
- Scene 5 is lean, and it ends on an understated line that is not a portentous one-liner: "The sound went nowhere in the wet vault."
- The final gesture deflates exactly as it should: "It did not ring with iron authority; it merely squelched into the gray ooze and vanished from sight." (Scene 11)

## Out of lane
In Scene 8 Arthur already calls her "Rann" by name, yet in Scene 10 "I am Hettie Rann" is written as a reveal. That is a continuity or payoff problem for another lens.
