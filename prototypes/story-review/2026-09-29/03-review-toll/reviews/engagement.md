# Engagement and Believability — Toll

**Score: 3/5** — The first three scenes are gripping and the premise has a strong hook, but the middle makes the same point about Arthur three times, and the climax drops the dilemma the story built up, so the central act feels arbitrary rather than chosen.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The climactic choice ignores the dilemma the story just set up
- **Where:** Scene 10 (setup in Scene 9; aftermath in Scene 11)
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "Five thousand souls, Hettie. Are their children to choke on dry dust so you can weep over four roods of rotted willow?" / "Hettie closed her hand around the cold brass pin in her coat, unable to answer." Scene 10: "She set the pin against the linkage shear-collar and swung the heavy lead mallet."
- **Problem:** Scene 9 ends on the story's real question: draining the lake will cut off water to five thousand people. In the next scene Hettie trips the valve with no visible reasoning. The jammed-spillway emergency Arthur mentions ("If the crest overtops before dawn…") could make the act a rescue, but the prose never connects her choice to it. So a reader cannot tell whether she is saving the valley, taking revenge, or both. Scene 11 never comes back to the canal or the five thousand people, so the dilemma just disappears.
- **Reader needed:** A clear sense of why Hettie chooses to pull the pin at this moment, given what Clara told her, and what that choice costs the people downstream.

### 2. [major] The "I am Hettie Rann" reveal has no force, because Arthur already knows her
- **Where:** Scene 10 (contradicted by Scene 1 and Scene 8)
- **Quote:** Scene 10: "'I am Hettie Rann.' … The long years of vigil collapsed in his eyes into a single, terrible recognition" / Scene 8: "'The log was yours from yesterday's micrometer sweep, Rann,' he said" / Scene 1: "'The diversion sluices upriver drop at dusk, Hettie,' Arthur said"
- **Problem:** The climax is staged as an unmasking, but Arthur addressed her by first name in Scene 1 and by surname in Scene 8. The turn relies on him not recognising a woman he has worked beside, which makes him look slightly stupid rather than shaken.
- **Reader needed:** Either a reason Arthur never connected "Rann" to Low Meadow, or a confrontation built on something he doesn't already know.

### 3. [major] Three scenes in a row repeat the same beat, "Arthur is secretly good"
- **Where:** Scene 6, Scene 7, Scene 8
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "In his place stood an old man in a threadbare coat… turning a rusted wheel so that strangers' fields would not wash away" / Scene 7: "He had not signed to conquer them. He had signed to buy them daylight." / Scene 8: "the righteous certainty that had once weighted them felt fractured"
- **Problem:** Each scene tells Hettie the same thing and ends with the same beat: the stone in her pocket feels heavier. Pressure does not build here. It sits at one level while the plot waits, and the reader gets ahead of Hettie by the middle of Scene 6.
- **Reader needed:** Each discovery should change something Hettie does or plans, instead of re-softening the same certainty.

### 4. [minor] Key evidence reaches Hettie through convenient spying
- **Where:** Scene 6 (and Scene 7)
- **Quote:** "Through the grime of a small square window… Scrawled across the ledger in Arthur's heavy, angular hand was a single jagged refusal: Divert to third-tier sluice replacement, South Reach canals."
- **Problem:** She reads a handwritten note on a table through a grimy window at dusk, on the same night she watches his secret good deed. The very next scene, the one folder in the archive that clears him is sitting under the logbooks. The evidence feels placed rather than found.
- **Reader needed:** Discoveries that come from Hettie's own digging or from friction with Arthur, not from lucky sightlines.

### 5. [minor] The father's death is introduced at the climax as a new wound
- **Where:** Scene 10 (compare Scene 3 and Scene 11)
- **Quote:** Scene 10: "And my father drowned his team trying to drag the hay wagons out of the back forty before your sluices opened."
- **Problem:** This is the heaviest personal loss in the story. Yet Scene 3's grief is all about the house and the hearth, and nothing hints that a father died. Revealed only at the confrontation, it reads as a raise in stakes made up on the spot, and it undercuts Arthur's "ten hours of wagon egress" without anyone dealing with that.
- **Reader needed:** Some early sign of the father's death, so it lands as a truth finally said aloud rather than new information.

### 6. [minor] The bypass pin is handed over with no difficulty
- **Where:** Scene 5 (setup in Scene 2)
- **Quote:** "He reached into his coat pocket and withdrew a heavy, squared bar of cold brass—the emergency bypass pin milled to override the sediment scour gates without ministerial key-codes—and laid it upon the damp stone between them."
- **Problem:** Scene 2 shows the pin locked "beneath the seal-wire". Here a contractor offers the dam's most dangerous override as a casual kickback, without being asked, and nothing comes of it: no audit and no suspicion from Dale. The one real obstacle to her plan vanishes the moment she meets it.
- **Reader needed:** A cost or risk attached to getting the pin, or a reason Dale would give up something that could get him hanged.

### 7. [minor] Clara appears in the control room with no explanation
- **Where:** Scene 9
- **Quote:** "stood Clara, her elder sister, who had packed the kitchen pots into straw twenty years ago and sworn never to cast a backward glance at the drowning valley"
- **Problem:** Clara has been absent since Scene 3. Now she is inside a secured dam control room at night, after searching Hettie's sea-chest, and the prose admits she swore never to look back. The confrontation matters, but her being there is simply assumed.
- **Reader needed:** One line on how and why Clara is at the dam, and how she learned the plan.

### 8. [minor] The ending tells its meaning outright
- **Where:** Scene 11
- **Quote:** "Yet the house was still drowned, because the people who had breathed beneath its rafters were dead or dispersed, and mud cannot be commanded to grow wheat by any decree of vengeance."
- **Problem:** The image does the work: the dropped hearthstone "merely squelched into the gray ooze", and the line "There is nothing here to take back" lands on its own. The stated lesson before it flattens that. Arthur's cover-up and her escape are also summarised in one speech, so the aftermath arrives as information rather than something the reader feels.
- **Reader needed:** Trust in the image to carry the meaning, and some glimpse of what Hettie's act cost beyond herself.

## What works
- The opening is strong and specific. In Scene 3, Hettie burns the relocation voucher while the chimney goes under ("That was our roof." / "I will not sleep under a gift of their paper"), which gives her a clear, stubborn want early.
- The irreversible scour valve is planted in Scene 2 ("the ballast would drop free and swing the scour valve irrevocably open") and paid off in Scene 10. It is the story's best-earned mechanism.
- The telex note in Scene 7 is a precise, credible detail that re-frames Arthur: "*Signed under protest, 11:42 AM. Sluices to drop in controlled sequence to permit ten hours of wagon egress.*"

## Out of lane
The time spans don't match ("twenty years" vs "thirty years of river geometry" / "three decades of petitions"), and the hearthstone changes material (basalt / schist / "brass hearthstone ring"). These are for the consistency reviewer.
