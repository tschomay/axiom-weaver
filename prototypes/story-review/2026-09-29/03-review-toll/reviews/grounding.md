# Grounding and Detail — Toll

**Score: 3/5** — Almost every scene gives a strong sense of place within its first sentences, but the second half depends on several things the reader has to piece together: how the pin trips the valve, why Clara is at the dam, whether Hettie is working under a false name, and what the draining did downstream.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The climactic mechanism is asserted, not followed
- **Where:** Scene 10 (set up in Scene 2, Scene 5, Scene 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "disengaging that spindle pin meant the ballast would drop free and swing the scour valve irrevocably open" / Scene 10: "She set the pin against the linkage shear-collar and swung the heavy lead mallet. The brass drove home with a deafening ring. The trip dogs sheared"
- **Problem:** Scene 2 explains that the valve opens when a seated pin is *pulled out*. The climax works by *driving a pin in*. Along the way the pin is a brass bar in Dale's pocket in Scene 5 ("the emergency bypass pin milled to override the sediment scour gates"), then "the twin to the cold counterfeit steel" of a pin caged on the crest in Scene 6. The reader can't tell which pin Hettie holds, whether it's real or fake, or how striking it opens the gate. So the story's key act reads as a claim rather than something the reader watched happen.
- **Reader needed:** One clear line on what Hettie's pin is and what driving it into the collar physically does to the counterweight linkage.

### 2. [major] The downstream consequence, set up twice, is left offstage
- **Where:** Scene 10 to Scene 11 (set up in Scene 4 and Scene 9)
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "the canal sill runs dry by sundown. Five thousand people downstream will turn their taps and draw air and yellow mud." / Scene 11: "The basin was empty. The lake was gone."
- **Problem:** The story sets up the 200-meter canal sill with care in Scene 4 and makes it the central objection in Scene 9. It also opens Scene 10 with a jammed spillway threatening "the downstream dikes." Then the lake drains completely, and Scene 11 never says whether the towns went dry, flooded, or were spared. Scene 10's last line ("wrestled the shuddering damper down until the gate seated against its stops, committing the reservoir to the emptying dark") doesn't even make clear whether the damper slowed the release or finished it.
- **Reader needed:** Some sign, even one clause, of what happened below the dam once the pool fell under the sill.

### 3. [major] Clara's presence at the dam and her search of Hettie's things happen offstage
- **Where:** Scene 9
- **Quote:** "stood Clara, her elder sister, who had packed the kitchen pots into straw twenty years ago and sworn never to cast a backward glance" / "You did not change the lock on your sea-chest, Hettie"
- **Problem:** Clara was last seen boarding a lorry twenty years earlier. Now she is standing in the dam's crest control room at night, holding survey sheets she found in Hettie's dormitory chest. The reader is never told why she is at the dam, how she got into a staffed control room, or when she searched the chest. The Scene 9 confrontation depends on all of this.
- **Reader needed:** A sentence on why Clara is at the dam now and how she came to go through Hettie's chest.

### 4. [major] It's never settled whether Hettie is working under a false name
- **Where:** Scene 8 (pays off in Scene 10)
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "a grease-smudged slip bearing her draft-name" / Arthur: "The log was yours from yesterday's micrometer sweep, Rann," / Scene 10: "I am Hettie Rann."
- **Problem:** "Draft-name" hints at an alias that the story never sets up. Scene 4 introduces her openly as "Hettie Rann", and Arthur calls her "Rann" in Scene 8. Yet Scene 10 stages her name as the moment he recognizes her. The reader has to guess whether Arthur knew who she was all along.
- **Reader needed:** An early, plain statement of whether Hettie took the job under her own name and whether Arthur knows her.

### 5. [minor] Dale's bargain arrives with no reason behind it
- **Where:** Scene 5
- **Quote:** "Sign off on the replacement housings, Rann, and the bypass pin stays on this ledger."
- **Problem:** Dale offers Hettie exactly the tool she secretly wants, without being asked, and with no sign that he knows or guesses her aim. "Stays on this ledger" is also unclear: does the pin get recorded as issued, or kept off the books? It reads as a meeting whose setup was cut.
- **Reader needed:** Why Dale thinks she would want the pin, and what the ledger phrase means in practice.

### 6. [minor] The midnight emergency and the returned telex arrive without a bridge
- **Where:** Scene 10 (and Scene 7)
- **Quote:** Scene 10: "the midnight flood alarm was not a siren but an unrelenting subterranean pulse" / "pulled out the yellowed paper preserved inside an oilskin sleeve"; Scene 7: "When she slipped the folder back between the moldered logs"
- **Problem:** The storm crisis and the jammed spillway start without a word of setup. Scene 6's "If a sudden cloudburst struck" is only a hypothetical. It is also unclear whether this is the same night as Scene 9. And in Scene 7 Hettie put the telex back, yet in Scene 10 she has it in hand.
- **Reader needed:** A time cue tying Scene 10 to Scene 9, and a word on when she went back for the document.

### 7. [minor] Load-bearing backstory first appears at the climax
- **Where:** Scene 10 (similar in Scene 7)
- **Quote:** Scene 10: "And my father drowned his team trying to drag the hay wagons out of the back forty before your sluices opened." / Scene 7: "dated three days before the siren had sounded across Low Meadow"
- **Problem:** Scene 1 shows only Hettie and Clara at the farm, with no father and no siren. The father's death is the emotional core of Hettie's accusation, but it appears for the first time in the line where she makes it. The siren is referred to as if the reader had heard it.
- **Reader needed:** Earlier sight of the father (or of his absence) in the Scene 1–3 evacuation.

### 8. [minor] Recurring explanation and re-introduction
- **Where:** Scene 8 (also Scene 2 / Scene 10, Scene 6, Scene 9)
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "Cavitation—the ravenous implosion of vapor bubbles born in low-pressure shear, chewing through hardened alloy until thick metal pitted like rotten bone—was an engineer's cancer."
- **Problem:** Technical glosses pause the dialogue. Scene 10 explains the irreversible counterweight a second time ("She knew the mechanics by heart"). Characters the reader already knows get their roles restated: Scene 6 "Arthur Vane—the ministerial bailiff who had once stamped her family's land into oblivion", and Scene 9 re-describes the pin as "the heavy brass rod she had fraudulently requisitioned". Each instance is small, but together they are close to chronic.
- **Reader needed:** Trust that a mechanic or person already set up once will be remembered.

## What works
- Scene openings anchor place and sensation fast. Scene 1 opens with "The river had already begun to taste of lime and pulverized shale before the horsemen came down through the cut," and names the farmstead, Hettie, and Clara within three sentences.
- The time jump is handled cleanly in one line: "Twenty years later, the dark had merely solidified into four hundred thousand tons of dressed ashlar and poured aggregate." (Scene 4)
- Scene 4 plants the canal threshold concretely and early, which is exactly what later stakes need: "the gravity sill for the downstream intake canal required a reservoir pool elevation of at least two hundred meters to maintain continuous flow."

## Out of lane
Many physical details drift between scenes (the hearthstone is basalt, a lintel corner, a "brass hearthstone ring", then schist; "thirty years" against "twenty years"; a motor lorry in a horse-drawn world), which is for the consistency reviewer.
