# Character Coherence — The Borrowed Boat

**Score: 3/5** — Ruth's motives and the sisters' quarrel are sharp and well shown through Scene 4. The ending, though, depends on two load-bearing changes the page doesn't earn: Ruth's knowledge of the secret shifts mid-story, and Helen drops her plan to sell the boat for no visible reason.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Ruth's knowledge of the Tern Bay secret changes between scenes
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 2 vs Scene 5
- **Quote:** "three lines in her father's tidy copperplate caught her eye... Arthur had never owned land in Tern Bay, not on any deed Ruth had ever seen" (Scene 1); "He gave me these coordinates and made me promise to carry him to this door. A woman named Clara." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Early on, Ruth finds the coordinates as a mystery, and in Scene 2 she is still wondering about "whatever Arthur had hidden at Tern Bay." In Scene 5 she turns out to have known for certain since the deathbed, down to the woman's name and the promise. Her Scene 2 gamble, hiding a cracked stay to reach Tern Bay, reads as curiosity at the time and as keeping a promise in hindsight. Those are different people with different reasons.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent answer to what Ruth knew when she left: either she really discovers it on the voyage, or the early scenes hint that she is keeping a secret.

### 2. [major] Helen gives up the sale without cause
- **Where:** Scene 5 and Scene 6 (set up in Scene 1)
- **Quote:** "We were drowning in his arrears, Ruth. The mast is firewood... What else was there to sell?" (Scene 5); "'The breaker can come looking for his deposit,' she said evenly." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Helen is set up as the practical one, pricing timber in Scene 1 with bailiffs and debts closing in. A few lines after defending the sale as her only option, she drops it, and in Scene 6 she agrees to pay half the drydock rent. The only thing between her defence and her reversal is scattering the ashes. We never see what in that moment changes her feelings about the boat, the debts, or her sister, so the story's resolution happens only because the plot needs it.
- **Reader needed:** A visible reason for Helen's turn, such as something she sees or feels at the cottage or something Ruth says, that outweighs two hundred pounds and the bailiff.

### 3. [major] The sisters' reconciliation is asserted, not played out
- **Where:** Scene 3 → Scene 4 → Scene 6
- **Quote:** "You bought candles to light the room while I sat beside the dying man. You bought yourself clean hands." (Scene 3); "There was no time for resentment, no time for three years of dying old men, and no time for lost skiffs." (Scene 4); "standing shoulder to shoulder with Ruth" (Scene 6)
- **Problem:** Ruth's Scene 3 grievance about years of care and "clean hands" is the story's emotional core. The narrator sets it aside during the storm and never picks it up again: no apology, no acknowledgement, no exchange. By Scene 6 the sisters are splitting rent side by side, as if the storm solved an estrangement of several years.
- **Reader needed:** One exchange after the storm where the Scene 3 wound is spoken to, even partly, so "shoulder to shoulder" is earned.

### 4. [minor] Helen barely reacts to her father's second household
- **Where:** Scene 5
- **Quote:** "Helen stared up the overgrown path... 'He wanted a spectacle,' Helen murmured."
- **Problem:** Helen learns that her father kept a hidden family for twenty years, which overlaps with their mother's lifetime. Her whole response is one murmured line, which is small for news that should unsettle everything she believed about him. The only named sign of feeling is the narrator's comparison of the lawn to tarnished curtains.
- **Reader needed:** A reaction from Helen, of shock, anger or grim recognition, on the scale of the revelation.

### 5. [minor] Ruth never reckons with her own lie about the stay
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 3 and Scene 4
- **Quote:** "'Sound enough,' she called back, her voice level." (Scene 2); "You never could be bothered to tie a thing down." (Scene 3); "her eyes fixed on the stemhead where she had tried to ignore the hairline fracture" (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Ruth rages at Helen for one careless hitch while hiding a crack that goes on to dismast the boat and nearly sink them both. When the stay breaks, she shows no guilt, and Helen never learns or asks. The hypocrisy could be powerful, but the story never shows that Ruth notices it, so she seems oddly untouched by her own near-fatal choice.
- **Reader needed:** Some flicker of Ruth's guilt or self-justification when the tang shears.

### 6. [minor] The meaning of the scattering is unclear
- **Where:** Scene 5
- **Quote:** "He wanted us to do his penance for him... And leave her to weep over what he left behind." / "Not a grain touched the flagstones of the porch."
- **Problem:** Ruth condemns her father's wish, and Helen shares the contempt, yet they carry it out anyway. The only resistance is the detail that no ash touches the porch. It isn't clear whether the sisters are honouring the promise, defying it, or compromising, so the story's key act has no readable intent from either of them.
- **Reader needed:** A sign of what the sisters mean by scattering on the lawn rather than at the door.

### 7. [nit] Helen's confession assumes Ruth knows a secret she has no way of knowing
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** "sign her away before the autumn gales take the roof off the wheelhouse" (Scene 1); "'You had better tell me what you agreed to in Skiff Cove,' Ruth said." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Helen announced the sale openly in Scene 1, yet Ruth reacts to the breaker deal as betrayal "behind my back." Nothing shows how Ruth came to suspect a hidden agreement.
- **Reader needed:** A small clue earlier that Helen had done more than she said.

## What works
- Ruth's decision to hide the crack is well motivated on the page, with the cost of each option laid out: "If they turned back, Donald would see the flaw, Helen would refuse to clear the bar a second time, and whatever Arthur had hidden at Tern Bay would stay drowned in his arithmetic."
- Scene 3 gives the sisters distinct voices and real grievances. Helen's defence is specific and human: "I sent money... You couldn't have bought the paraffin without me."
- Donald keeps a consistent gruff voice, and his late softening is small and in character: "Mondays for the slip rent... And don't leave your shavings on my slip."

## Out of lane
Helen's time away is "five winters" in Inverness (Scene 1) but "three years... in the south" in Scene 3, and the urn is brass in Scene 1 but a "sealed tin urn" in Scene 3 (consistency).
