# Grounding and Detail — The Borrowed Boat

**Score: 3/5** — The sea scenes are richly and physically grounded, but the plot's key facts only arrive in Scene 5, and the reader has to piece them together after the fact: where Arthur asked to be scattered, who Clara is, and Helen's deal with the breaker.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The voyage's destination and Arthur's instruction are never set up
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 5 (also Scenes 2–4)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "We run her down the sound, see the ashes over the side by Tuesday, and sign her away". Scene 1: "three lines in her father's tidy copperplate caught her eye. They were domestic mooring coordinates". Scene 5: "He gave me these coordinates and made me promise to carry him to this door."
- **Problem:** For four scenes the reader doesn't know where the sisters are sailing, or where Arthur asked to be scattered. Helen only says "over the side", and Ruth reads the coordinates as a new discovery. Then Scene 5 reveals that the whole voyage was a deathbed instruction to reach this door. The quest has no stated goal until it's over, so Scenes 2–4 are hard to measure progress against.
- **Reader needed:** Early on, a clear statement of where Arthur said he wanted his ashes, and whether Ruth knew about the Tern Bay coordinates before she found them in the log.

### 2. [major] Clara is named once, never seen, and her situation is left open
- **Where:** Scene 5
- **Quote:** "A woman named Clara. A second household he kept hidden in this creek for twenty years" … "the untended lawn lay drowned under nettles and sea-plantain" … "Not a grain touched the flagstones of the porch."
- **Problem:** Clara first appears in the same line that reveals the story's central secret. She isn't at the cottage, and the neglected lawn and "blind dormers" hint that no one lives there. The reader can't tell whether she's alive, has left, or is watching. So the pointed choice to scatter the ashes on the lawn "not the porch" has no clear target.
- **Reader needed:** Some sign of whether Clara still lives in the cottage, so the sisters' refusal to take the ashes to her door means something specific.

### 3. [major] Helen's sale and Arthur's debts happen offstage and come out of nowhere
- **Where:** Scene 5 (and Scene 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "You had better tell me what you agreed to in Skiff Cove" … "I took a deposit from a breaker at the commercial docks … pay Arthur's timber accounts, and settle the shop debts before the bailiff moved in." Scene 6: "The Tern Bay deposit would have to be mailed back"
- **Problem:** The breaker deal, Arthur's shop, his debts and the bailiff are all new in Scene 5. Nothing shows how Ruth knew there was a deal to ask about. It's also unclear where the deal was made: Ruth says "in Skiff Cove", Helen says "the commercial docks", and Scene 6 calls it "the Tern Bay deposit". The reader has to rebuild both the deal and the family's money trouble backwards.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier hint of Arthur's debts and Helen's buyer (Scene 1 already has her pricing the timber), and one clear place where the deal was made.

### 4. [minor] The jump from Low Water Creek to Tern Bay is unexplained
- **Where:** Scene 4 → Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "the Shearwater drifted into the muddy gut of Low Water Creek, ruined, dismasted, and safe." Scene 5: "Ruth kept her hand firm on the tiller, steering the dismasted hull well clear of the broad granite breakwater that sheltered the commercial wharves of Tern Bay."
- **Problem:** Scene 4 ends with the boat at rest in one named creek. Scene 5 opens with it moving past a different harbour, with no time cue (the same night? the next morning?) and no word on whether Low Water Creek is Tern Bay Creek.
- **Reader needed:** A clause linking the two places and saying how much time has passed.

### 5. [minor] The tender is first mentioned in the scene where it's lost
- **Where:** Scene 3 (recurs in Scene 1 opening)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "The painter line of the little wooden tender was stiff with salt, its hemp biting into her knuckles as she threw a hitch around the iron cleat." Scene 1: "Helen dropped through the companionway hatch"
- **Problem:** The dinghy was never shown being towed or carried on deck, so losing it (and Ruth's "no dinghy, no tender, and nowhere to go ashore") matters less than it should. The same gap in introductions shows up in Scene 1: Helen enters by first name, and the reader only learns from the logline that she's Ruth's sister. The prose doesn't say "sister" until Scene 6.
- **Reader needed:** A glimpse of the tender early on, and one word in Scene 1 that makes Helen Ruth's sister.

### 6. [minor] Scene 1 moves Helen from the cabin to the jetty without showing it
- **Where:** Scene 1
- **Quote:** "Donald is on the jetty with his book." … "He thrust a grease-stained sheet of carbon paper into Helen's ribs before she had both feet on the stone." … "thrust the board back into his coat pocket."
- **Problem:** Helen is below decks, speaking, and in the next paragraph she's stepping onto the stone. Then a "board" is returned that was never shown (only a sheet of carbon paper). The waiver handoff is the story's ticking clock, and its staging is blurred.
- **Reader needed:** One beat of Helen climbing out onto the jetty, and the clipboard named when Donald first holds it out.

## What works
- The crack in the bronze fitting is planted with exact physical detail, so the dismasting pays off: "a hairline fracture split the bronze like a seam in cooling hearth-slag. Under each heavy roll, the crack opened into a dark mouth no thicker than a fingernail".
- The dismasting action is easy to follow step by step: "Helen swung the axe against the spruce stays and crushed lanyards … Ruth kicked the severed gooseneck clear of the deck, and the broken spar slid down into the dark water."
- Scene 1 opens with place, POV and the key object all anchored: "Ruth Miller settled Arthur's ash urn into the narrow locker beneath the Shearwater's chart bench".

## Out of lane
Ruth "finds" the coordinates in Scene 1 but says in Scene 5 that Arthur gave them to her on his deathbed. Helen's Inverness clerkship (five winters) also becomes "three years running railway timetables in the south" (consistency).
