# Logical Consistency — The Borrowed Boat

**Score: 2/5** — several major contradictions (what Ruth knew about Tern Bay, how long Helen was away, and the waiver countdown), plus details about the urn and the boat that keep drifting, so the reader starts to distrust the story's facts.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Ruth "discovers" coordinates she later says Arthur gave her on his deathbed
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scenes 2, 5)
- **Quote:** "three lines in her father's tidy copperplate caught her eye … Arthur had never owned land in Tern Bay, not on any deed Ruth had ever seen" (Scene 1) / "He gave me these coordinates and made me promise to carry him to this door. A woman named Clara." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** In Scene 1 Ruth comes across the coordinates as something new and puzzling. In Scene 2 she still doesn't know what they mean: "whatever Arthur had hidden at Tern Bay". In Scene 5 she says she was told the destination, and about Clara, directly and in person. Both versions cannot be true, and the reveal rewrites the opening.
- **Reader needed:** Either Ruth already knew from the start and Scene 1 shows her checking a promise, or she learns about Clara on the page before she explains it.

### 2. [major] The waiver countdown does not add up
- **Where:** Scene 5 (and Scenes 2, 6)
- **Quote:** "\"And three days left on the waiver,\" Ruth said." … "By the following afternoon, the blunt bow pushed through the entrance of Skiff Cove" (Scene 5) / "Three hours remained on the yellow slipway notice … It had been seven days to the afternoon" (Scene 6)
- **Problem:** At Tern Bay there are three days left. They reach home the next afternoon, which should leave about two days, yet Scene 6 has them arriving with three hours to spare. Scene 2 says "six days left" on the day they sail. Taken together, the voyage seems to last one or two days, not seven.
- **Reader needed:** Day counts that agree with each other, so the last-minute arrival in Scene 6 is earned.

### 3. [major] Helen's time away changes in length, job and direction
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "She had left Skiff Cove Harbour five winters back for a ledger clerk's stool in Inverness" (Scene 1) / "You spent three years running railway timetables in the south, Helen." (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** Five winters becomes three years, a ledger clerk becomes a railway timetable job, and Inverness becomes "the south". This is the central grievance between the sisters, so the reader notices.
- **Reader needed:** One account of when Helen left, where she went and what she did.

### 4. [minor] The urn keeps changing material and place
- **Where:** Scene 1 (recurs in Scenes 2, 3, 5, 6)
- **Quote:** "settled Arthur's ash urn into the narrow locker beneath the Shearwater's chart bench, wedging it firm between two rolled storm jibs" (Scene 1) / "the sealed tin urn rested beside the logbook on the shelf" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** The urn is a brass cylinder in a locker (Scene 1), then a "brass-sealed jar wrapped in canvas" in the cuddy (Scene 2), a tin urn on a shelf (Scene 3), taken "from the shelf behind the companionway" (Scene 5), and remembered as resting "beside the compass" (Scene 6). This is the story's key object, and nobody is shown moving it.
- **Reader needed:** One urn, made of one material, kept in one place, unless someone is shown moving it.

### 5. [minor] The boat reaches "Low Water Creek", then enters "Tern Bay Creek" as if arriving again
- **Where:** Scene 4 (and Scene 5)
- **Quote:** "the Shearwater drifted into the muddy gut of Low Water Creek, ruined, dismasted, and safe." (Scene 4) / "steering the dismasted hull well clear of the broad granite breakwater … she throttled back and swung the bow into Tern Bay Creek" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Scene 4 ends with the boat already inside a creek. Scene 5 has it back outside, passing the Tern Bay breakwater and entering a creek with a different name. The reader can't tell whether these are one place or two.
- **Reader needed:** One creek name, or a line moving the boat from the first creek to the second.

### 6. [minor] Helen knows about the logbook coordinates without being told
- **Where:** Scene 5 (and Scene 1)
- **Quote:** "\"The logbook coordinates,\" Helen called back over the diesel's clatter. \"They were never an offshore fueling stop.\"" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Ruth found the entries alone, before Helen came aboard (Scene 1), and she is never shown telling Helen. Helen is also absent from Ruth's thoughts about them in Scene 2.
- **Reader needed:** A moment where Ruth shows Helen the log or tells her about it.

### 7. [minor] The boat's timber and steering keep changing
- **Where:** Scene 1 (recurs in Scenes 2, 5, 6)
- **Quote:** "so it would not rattle against the spruce hull" … "thirty feet of seasoned larch" (Scene 1) / "through two inches of oak" (Scene 2) / "Ruth kept her hand firm on the tiller" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** The hull is spruce, larch, oak, then fir planking. The spruce mast leaves a "splintered pine stump" in Scene 6. The boat is steered by a spoked wheel in Scenes 2, 4 and 6 but by a tiller in Scene 5.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent boat.

### 8. [minor] "Behind my back", though Helen announced the sale in front of Ruth
- **Where:** Scene 5 (and Scene 1)
- **Quote:** "We run her down the sound, see the ashes over the side by Tuesday, and sign her away" (Scene 1) / "\"You were going to let them scrap her behind my back,\" Ruth said." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Helen said openly in Scene 1 that she planned to sell the boat, so Ruth's charge of secrecy only works if the secret is the breaker and the deposit, and the line doesn't make that distinction. Relatedly, in Scene 1 Helen is already aboard and below deck, yet Donald reaches her "before she had both feet on the stone", as if she were just arriving on the jetty. Helen also "thrust the board back into his coat pocket" when she was handed only a carbon sheet.
- **Reader needed:** Ruth's anger aimed at what was actually hidden (scrapping, the deposit), and Helen clearly placed on the jetty or aboard.

## What works
- The cracked fitting is followed consistently from set-up to failure: "a hairline fracture split the bronze" (Scene 2) → "her eyes fixed on the stemhead where she had tried to ignore the hairline fracture" (Scene 4) → "The bronze tang sheared clean through its scarred grain."
- The seven-day clause is stated clearly at the start and pays off on the slip: "You bring the Shearwater back into Skiff Cove within the week or the harbour board impounds the mooring" (Scene 1) → "drew a thick line through the provisional impound entry" (Scene 6). Only the counting in between is off.
- The lost tender is remembered correctly later: "now we have no dinghy, no tender" (Scene 3) → "no time for lost skiffs" (Scene 4).

## Out of lane
Clara never appears, and the cottage is described as neglected, so it is unclear who the scattering is aimed at (engagement or character).
