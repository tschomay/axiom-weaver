# Prose Craft — The Borrowed Boat

**Score: 3/5** — the prose is competent and often concretely nautical, but the simile habits and the "did not X" and triplet tics are noticeable, and the seams between scenes (the arrival at the creek told twice, details of the urn and of Helen's life that shift) show the story was stitched from separate pieces.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Seam: the creek arrival is written twice
- **Where:** Scene 4 → Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "the Shearwater drifted into the muddy gut of Low Water Creek, ruined, dismasted, and safe." Scene 5: "she throttled back and swung the bow into Tern Bay Creek, a winding gut of brown water choked with yellow weed"
- **Problem:** Scene 4 closes with the boat already inside a creek. Scene 5 then steers past the Tern Bay breakwater and enters a creek with a different name, described with the same word ("gut"). A reader has to stop and work out whether this is one place or two.
- **Reader needed:** Scene 5 to continue from where Scene 4 left the boat, not stage the arrival again.

### 2. [minor] Domestic similes of the same kind in every scene (hearth, tallow, fabric, "empty")
- **Where:** Scenes 2, 3, 4, 5, 6
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "thick and pale as melted tallow"; Scene 5: "scattering like cold sweepings from an empty hearth"; Scene 6: "dim and yellow as a tallow candle guttering in an empty jar"
- **Problem:** Almost every simile at sea comes from indoors. Hearth and flue: "cooling hearth-slag" (S2), "a stepping boot on a hearth-tile" (S3), "soot blown down an uncapped flue" (S4), the empty hearth (S5). Tallow: three times (S2, "rotten tallow" S3, S6). Fabric: "frayed silver fringe on a cast-off shawl" (S2), "soaked damask torn from a banquet table" (S4), "tarnished velvet curtains" (S5), "frayed hem of black silk" (S6). "Empty": "a pistol fired in an empty kitchen" (S4), plus the hearth and jar above. Across six scenes the pattern reads as a tic, not a motif.
- **Reader needed:** Similes drawn from the world the sisters are actually in, or used less often.

### 3. [minor] "She did not X" and "not X but Y" constructions
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 3, 4, 5
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "She did not look at the water. She looked straight at Helen." Scene 4: "The mist did not lift; it blackened." / "The metal gave not with a groan, but with a crack"
- **Problem:** The same negation-then-correction move recurs: "She did not look at the urn. She glanced instead…" (S1), "It did not snap or fray; it simply spilled loose" (S3), "She did not aim for the coal piers" and "She did not climb onto the dock" (S5). It flattens the rhythm into a formula.
- **Reader needed:** Just state the action most of the time, and save the negation for when the expectation really matters.

### 4. [minor] Scenes end on the same triplet cadence
- **Where:** Scene 4, Scene 5, Scene 6
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "ruined, dismasted, and safe." Scene 5: "where they tied up against the weathered pilings safe at last." Scene 6: "leaving the Shearwater settled, upright, and home."
- **Problem:** Three consecutive scenes land on "safe/home", two of them on an adjective triplet. Scene 4 also has "no time for resentment, no time for three years of dying old men, and no time for lost skiffs." The endings echo one another.
- **Reader needed:** Endings with different shapes, so the last "home" feels earned rather than repeated.

### 5. [minor] Details about the urn and Helen's life change from scene to scene
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 3, Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "She had left Skiff Cove Harbour five winters back for a ledger clerk's stool in Inverness"; Scene 3: "You spent three years running railway timetables in the south, Helen."
- **Problem:** Helen's past changes between scenes. Scene 5 then adds "your shop" and "the shop debts". The urn moves from "brass cylinder" (S1, S5) to "brass-sealed jar … wrapped in canvas" (S2) to "sealed tin urn" (S3). The mast goes from spruce (S2) to "pine stump" (S6) to "a stick of fir" (S6). Each voice described the objects its own way.
- **Reader needed:** One fixed description of each recurring person and object, carried across scenes.

### 6. [minor] Expository, stagey dialogue in the reveal and the quarrel
- **Where:** Scene 5 (also Scene 3)
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "A woman named Clara. A second household he kept hidden in this creek for twenty years, cloaked in false entries in his coastal fuel book."
- **Problem:** Ruth explains the plot in narrator's diction ("cloaked in false entries"). Scene 3's "You spent three years running railway timetables in the south, Helen" is an as-you-know line. Adverb-style tags pile up: "Ruth mimicked, flat and venomous", "Ruth spat", "she confessed, her voice thin".
- **Reader needed:** Lines that sound like what one sister would actually say to the other, with plainer tags.

### 7. [minor] Clichés and stated emotion at the high points
- **Where:** Scene 3 (also Scenes 3 and 4)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "her heart hammering against her ribs like a trapped bird." / "though she hated herself for the defense."
- **Problem:** Stock phrases fill the quarrel. White knuckles appear twice ("Her knuckles were white" S3, "her knuckles white on the brass rim" S4), and "teeth" is reused for rocks and wind ("wet teeth of the skerries" S1, "basalt teeth of Morren Reach" S3, "In the teeth of the wind" S4).
- **Reader needed:** A specific gesture in place of the stock one.

### 8. [nit] Summary where the story reaches the return voyage
- **Where:** Scene 5
- **Quote:** "they ran northward through the clearing weather. By the following afternoon, the blunt bow pushed through the entrance of Skiff Cove, gliding into the familiar stillness of their home harbor"
- **Problem:** A whole passage is compressed into one summary sentence, then Scene 6 reopens at the same harbour. Small echoes also slip by: "Donald Crane stood waiting beneath the rusted crane", and "yellow glaze … dim and yellow" (S6).
- **Reader needed:** Either cut the summary and let Scene 6 carry the return, or give the return one concrete beat.

## What works
- A sharp, unsentimental opening image: "in the end he had shrunk to four pounds of grey clinker and a brass seal that would not take polish."
- An exact, physical detail that carries the tension: "the crack opened into a dark mouth no thicker than a fingernail, weeping rust before biting shut again."
- Donald's dry closing line gives the ending its warmth without stating it: "Mondays for the slip rent … And don't leave your shavings on my slip."

## Out of lane
The logline says the sisters scatter the ashes "where he said", but Scene 5 scatters them on the lawn, "not a grain" on the porch. The plot/intent reviewer may want to check that.
