# Engagement and Believability — The Borrowed Boat

**Score: 3/5** — Scenes 2 to 4 build real pressure: a hidden crack, a lost tender, a vicious sibling fight and a dismasting. But the central revelation contradicts what Scene 1 showed, the big secret is delivered as a monologue with no one to confront, and the money and deadline pressures dissolve on contact at the end.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Ruth's deathbed knowledge contradicts her discovery in Scene 1
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** "three lines in her father's tidy copperplate caught her eye… Arthur had never owned land in Tern Bay, not on any deed Ruth had ever seen" (Scene 1); "He gave me these coordinates and made me promise to carry him to this door. A woman named Clara." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Scene 1 shows Ruth finding the coordinates and puzzling over them. Scene 5 reveals that Arthur gave them to her on his deathbed and told her about Clara. It's also the first time Clara is named. The reveal doesn't pay off a set-up. It rewrites the set-up, and I went back to Scene 1 to see whether I'd misread. It also undercuts the premise that "neither believes him," since Ruth already knew exactly what he meant.
- **Reader needed:** Either Ruth's Scene 1 reaction is knowing and guarded (hiding what she knows), or the Clara truth is discovered at the creek and not recited.

### 2. [major] The climax secret is told, not confronted
- **Where:** Scene 5
- **Quote:** "A second household he kept hidden in this creek for twenty years, cloaked in false entries in his coastal fuel book. He wanted us to carry his remains to her step and dump his ghost in her lap."
- **Problem:** The quest's payoff (the second family, the cottage, Clara) comes in one speech. Then the sisters scatter the ashes on the lawn and leave. Clara never appears, the cottage looks abandoned, and nobody knocks, looks in, or wonders whether she is alive. The story's biggest emotional question gets less stage time than the dismasting.
- **Reader needed:** Some contact with the hidden life (a face at the window, an object on the porch, evidence she is gone) so the choice to scatter on the lawn costs something.

### 3. [minor] Ruth's lie about the stay is never reckoned with
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 3 and Scene 4
- **Quote:** "'Sound enough,' she called back, her voice level." (Scene 2); "You never could be bothered to tie a thing down." (Scene 3); "her eyes fixed on the stemhead where she had tried to ignore the hairline fracture" (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** This is the best dramatic irony in the story. Ruth savages Helen over a slipped painter while hiding the fault that dismasts them. Helen never learns of it, and Ruth never admits it. The sisters reconcile in Scenes 5 and 6 without the one confession that would earn it.
- **Reader needed:** A beat where the hidden crack comes out between them, or where Ruth visibly chooses to keep it.

### 4. [minor] The debt pressure vanishes with no cost
- **Where:** Scene 5 and Scene 6
- **Quote:** "We were drowning in his arrears, Ruth… What else was there to sell?" (Scene 5); "The Tern Bay deposit would have to be mailed back with an apology and a short explanation, but that was paper work, and paper work could wait." (Scene 6)
- **Problem:** Helen's motive (arrears, shop debts, the bailiff) is dropped the moment the plot needs harmony. Keeping a dismasted boat on the hard adds rent and timber costs, and the story treats the money as solved.
- **Reader needed:** An acknowledgement of what keeping the boat will cost them, so the choice reads as a sacrifice and not a free happy ending.

### 5. [minor] Helen's "confession" was never a secret
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** "We run her down the sound, see the ashes over the side by Tuesday, and sign her away before the autumn gales" (Scene 1); "'You were going to let them scrap her behind my back,' Ruth said." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Helen announced the sale openly in Scene 1, so her Scene 5 confession and Ruth's outrage fall flat as a reveal.
- **Reader needed:** Something truly hidden, such as selling to a breaker rather than a buyer or taking cash already, flagged as news.

### 6. [minor] The seven-day clock never bites, and the return is told twice
- **Where:** Scene 5 and Scene 6
- **Quote:** "'And three days left on the waiver,' Ruth said." / "tied up against the weathered pilings safe at last." (Scene 5); "Three hours remained on the yellow slipway notice… It had been seven days to the afternoon" (Scene 6)
- **Problem:** The deadline set up in Scene 1 never pressures a decision. They have days to spare and then, suddenly, hours. Scene 5 already summarises a safe homecoming, so Scene 6's near-miss tension arrives after the reader has been told everything is fine. Donald's impound threat then folds in a paragraph.
- **Reader needed:** Either let the clock force a choice at sea, or drop the "safe at last" summary so Scene 6 carries the return.

### 7. [nit] A convenient arrival after the storm
- **Where:** Scene 4 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** "the Shearwater drifted into the muddy gut of Low Water Creek, ruined, dismasted, and safe." (Scene 4); "steering the dismasted hull well clear of the broad granite breakwater that sheltered the commercial wharves of Tern Bay" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** A storm-crippled boat limping blind ends up right at the destination. It's plausible on a coastal passage, but the story doesn't show them choosing to push on.
- **Reader needed:** A beat where they decide to press on to Tern Bay rather than making for the nearest shelter.

## What works
- The hidden crack is a real, character-driven choice with a delayed payoff: "If they turned back, Donald would see the flaw, Helen would refuse to clear the bar a second time, and whatever Arthur had hidden at Tern Bay would stay drowned in his arithmetic."
- The Scene 3 fight is specific and earned by the lost tender: "Not the painter. Not Donald's seven days. Not him."
- The scattering carries the sisters' judgement in one image: "Not a grain touched the flagstones of the porch."

## Out of lane
Helen left "five winters back" (Scene 1), but Ruth says "You spent three years running railway timetables in the south" (Scene 3). Helen's post was also in Inverness, which is north, not south (consistency).
