# Prose Craft — The Apprentice's Bill

**Score: 2/5** — the prose is overwrought and circles the same few images (crypts, hollowing, weeping, frantic), and a verbatim seam between Scenes 2 and 3 pulls the reader out again and again.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Recycled funereal imagery: "crypt" three times, plus tomb, wake, cadaver
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 2, Scene 3
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "pressing close as an iron crypt while the chandelier flinched overhead"; Scene 2: "The pass of L'Hiver opened like a low-ceilinged crypt lined in zinc"; Scene 3: "took his tread with the muffled silence of an ancient crypt."
- **Problem:** Each scene reaches for the same simile. Around it are "heavy as a tomb slab", "felt like a wake" (Scene 1), "a condemned man's placard" and "a cadaver pinned to his apron" (Scene 3). By the third crypt the image is a verbal habit and no longer atmosphere.
- **Reader needed:** One strong death image, placed once, with the other scenes finding their own texture.

### 2. [major] Seam: Scene 3 opens by repeating Scene 2's closing line word for word
- **Where:** Scene 2 → Scene 3
- **Quote:** Scene 2 ends: "The great hand had failed." Scene 3 begins: "The great hand had failed, rattling like dry lath against the edge of the pass"
- **Problem:** The echo shows that each scene was written on its own. It also retells the tremor we just watched ("his knuckles seized mid-reach, shivering violently") as if the reader hadn't seen it.
- **Reader needed:** Scene 3 should pick up from where the reader already is, not restate the previous beat.

### 3. [minor] Same words in every scene: hollow, weeping, frantic, seeped
- **Where:** Scenes 1–3
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "time had hollowed the flesh beneath his cheekbones"; Scene 3: "his sallow face hollowed by the yellow bulb", "a hollowed vessel waiting to be shattered", "seemed to hollow out whatever remained of his stature"
- **Problem:** "Hollow" appears four times. "Weeping/weep" appears four times ("weeping moisture" S1; "beginning to weep", "weeping center", "weeping scullery drains" S3). "Frantic" appears three times ("frantic reek" S1; "frantic relief", "frantic, stuttering drumroll" S2). "Seeped" appears three times (twice in S1, once in S2). Readers notice the loop.
- **Reader needed:** A wider vocabulary, so repeated words don't draw more attention than what they describe.

### 4. [minor] Portentous one-liner at the end of every scene
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 2, Scene 3
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "He had built the fire that built this house. Now he would tend the hearth while it fell." Scene 2: "The great hand had failed." Scene 3: "leaving the heat and the name behind him forever in the dark."
- **Problem:** Every scene closes on an aphoristic sting. Scene 1's line is also a muddled metaphor, since tending a hearth doesn't make a house fall.
- **Reader needed:** At least one scene that ends on action or an image, so the final "forever in the dark" has something to stand out against.

### 5. [minor] Metaphors stacked in a single sentence, and redundant phrasing
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 2, Scene 3
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "holding the hay smoke suspended in velvet suspension"; Scene 2: "the cold iron biting through his coat like an iron brace"
- **Problem:** Both sentences repeat a word inside a figure of speech ("suspended … suspension", "iron … iron"). The opening sentence of Scene 1 piles gilded crest, dying amber and tomb slab together before the reader is oriented. Scene 3's "voice level and dead as cold slate" and "like an axe falling through timber" pile simile on simile.
- **Reader needed:** Fewer, cleaner figures of speech, with no word echoing inside a single image.

### 6. [minor] Narrator states conclusions and emotions
- **Where:** Scene 3 (also Scene 1)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "The vow was kept." / "his shoulders shaking with a slow, devastating gratitude … a silent, pleading apology that found no purchase."
- **Problem:** The burned name already shows the vow was kept, yet the narrator announces it. André's gratitude and apology are labelled rather than shown through a gesture. Scene 1's "effectively burying him beneath the cold weight of the industry's closed doors" flattens into summary in the same way.
- **Reader needed:** The act and a physical detail, trusted to do the work without a caption.

### 7. [minor] "It was not X. It was Y." construction
- **Where:** Scene 2
- **Quote:** "It was not fatigue. It was a ruinous nerve palsy, an uncontrollable shaking that would shatter fine sugar cages"
- **Problem:** A stock model construction. Here it also has the narrator diagnose the tremor, which the wedding-band image had already made vivid.
- **Reader needed:** To stay inside Julien's observation instead of getting a narrator's diagnosis.

### 8. [nit] The mentor's name is spelled differently between scenes
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 3 vs Scene 2
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "*by André Laurent*"; Scene 2: "Andre Laurent stood by the marble prep slab."
- **Problem:** The accent disappears for all of Scene 2 and comes back in Scene 3, which shows the story was written in separate pieces.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent spelling.

## What works
- The commis moment in Scene 2 has plain, specific dialogue that shows character without explaining it: "Leave it, Luc … The reduction was mine. I left it too thin to hold the spoon. Scrape the floor, scrub the edge, and draw another half-liter. Go."
- A precise, earned physical detail: "rattling his wedding band against the metal in a frantic, stuttering drumroll."
- The torch beat is clean and exact: "A needle of cold blue fire whistled into being, smelling of pressurized butane and ozone."

## Out of lane
Scene 1 seats Julien at "Table 4", and in Scene 3 he sends the dessert to "Table nine". Someone should check whether that is intended.
