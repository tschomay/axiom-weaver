# Logical Consistency — The Apprentice's Bill

**Score: 4/5** — no major contradictions; one minor slip about what dish Julien made, plus some drift in the kitchen's physical details that only a careful reader would catch.

## Findings

### 1. [minor] The stolen dish is called a "braise" but it is a tart
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "Not a single mention of the three winters Julien had spent perfecting the braise" (Scene 1) / "the quince tart destined for the critic's table … upon the crisp pastry shell" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** The dish Julien says was stolen is a smoked quince dessert with a crust and a spun-sugar dome. Calling it "the braise" suggests a different, savoury dish, so the reader briefly wonders whether two recipes are involved.
- **Reader needed:** The same dish named consistently, as a dessert or tart, each time it comes up.

### 2. [minor] The critic's ticket moves from the pastry station to the pass
- **Where:** Scene 2 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "Pinned dead-center over the pastry station, catching the harsh white glare of the heat lamps, hung the critic's VIP service ticket" (Scene 2) / "Above the pass, pinned to the iron wire like a condemned man's placard, the critic's VIP service ticket" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** The one object the vow depends on is placed in two different spots. The lighting also changes, from "harsh white glare" to "copper heat lamps" and a "yellow bulb".
- **Reader needed:** The ticket hanging in one place both times, so the burning lands where the reader last saw it.

### 3. [nit] André's worktop keeps changing material
- **Where:** Scene 2 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "planted both hands flat upon the stainless steel ledge" (Scene 2) / "shivering violently against the marble slab" … "falling slack upon the cold zinc counter" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** The surface André leans on is steel, then marble, then zinc, even though he seems to stay at the same spot on the pass.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent surface, or a sentence showing he moves.

### 4. [nit] The kitchen floor changes
- **Where:** Scene 2 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "the sour moisture of wet duckboards" … "splatter the floor tiles" (Scene 2) / "the wet flagstones beneath the rubber runners" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** The floor is described as duckboards, then tiles, then flagstones with rubber runners.
- **Reader needed:** One floor.

### 5. [nit] Julien's hiding place is named two ways
- **Where:** Scene 2 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "the dim recess between the dry-stores racking and the reach-in cooler" (Scene 2) / "Julien stepped out from the cold shadow of the dry-goods pantry" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** A recess between racking and a cooler becomes a "pantry". The two are close, but the second sounds like a separate room.
- **Reader needed:** The same place named the same way.

### 6. [nit] The chef's name is spelled two ways
- **Where:** Scene 2 (vs Scenes 1 and 3)
- **Quote:** "Andre Laurent stood by the marble prep slab." (Scene 2) / "André Laurent's name vanished" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** The accent is dropped throughout Scene 2 only.
- **Reader needed:** One spelling.

### 7. [nit] Table numbers: Julien is at 4, and the dessert goes to "nine"
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "The cardstock at Table 4" (Scene 1) / "\"Table nine,\" Julien said" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** The critic's table number is never given, so the only table number the reader knows is Julien's own. "Table nine" reads for a moment like a mistake before the reader works out it must be the critic's table.
- **Reader needed:** The critic's table number established earlier, or visible on the ticket Julien burns.

## What works
- The smoker's failure is set up and paid off consistently: "The kitchen's cold-smoking apparatus was running far too hot … the fragile pectin matrix of the fruit would fail to set" (Scene 1) matches "the scorched smoke infusion broke the delicate pectin matrix" (Scene 3).
- André's tremor is set up before Julien's steady hand pays it off: "an uncontrollable shaking that would shatter fine sugar cages" (Scene 2) → "lowered it over the quince without the tremble of a single filament" (Scene 3).
- The vow is stated in Scene 1 and kept exactly as stated in Scene 3: "he would see André's name burned off the ticket before the final course ever cleared the pass" → "André Laurent's name vanished in a sharp hiss of blackening ash … The vow was kept."

## Out of lane
Nobody in the brigade reacts to a stranger in a street coat walking in, lighting a torch and plating the critic's dessert (a plausibility and engagement issue, not a contradiction).
