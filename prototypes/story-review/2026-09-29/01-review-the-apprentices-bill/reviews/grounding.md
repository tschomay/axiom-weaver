# Grounding and Detail — The Apprentice’s Bill

**Score: 3/5** — Every scene is placed well, but the climax depends on an object the reader never learned the meaning of: André's name on the kitchen ticket. The rescue of the dessert is also stated rather than shown, so the payoff reads as a claim.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The name on the ticket is never set up, so burning it means nothing concrete
- **Where:** Scene 3 (set up in Scene 1 and Scene 2)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "he swore he would see André's name burned off the ticket before the final course ever cleared the pass." Scene 2: "hung the critic's VIP service ticket—its margins scored with red ink to mark the eight-course sequence toward dessert." Scene 3: "André Laurent’s name vanished in a sharp hiss of blackening ash … The vow was kept."
- **Problem:** The only place André's name is shown attached to the dish is the diner's menu card in Scene 1. The kitchen ticket is described in Scene 2 without any name on it. So when Scene 3 burns "André Laurent’s name" off the ticket and says the vow is kept, the reader can't tell whether this changes anything. Does the critic learn who made the dish? Does the credit move? Or is it only a private gesture? The whole climax hangs on that question.
- **Reader needed:** When the ticket first appears, a line saying it carries André's name as the credited chef, and what that name on the ticket does (for example, it's what the critic is told).

### 2. [major] The rescue technique is asserted rather than shown
- **Where:** Scene 3
- **Quote:** "poured it in a thin, merciless ribbon directly into the weeping center … The cold bound the separated oils; the glossy curd snapped back into cohesive tautness, holding the hay smoke suspended in velvet suspension."
- **Problem:** Scene 1 carefully explains why the dessert is failing: too much heat breaks the pectin. The fix gets one line of chemistry. Nothing earlier sets up the fix, or Julien knowing it, as something only the recipe's real author would know. The rescue proves Julien is the true chef, but the reader has to take the narrator's word that it works.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier hint that ties this cold-cream fix to Julien's own work on the recipe (the confiscated notebooks, his three winters), so the rescue feels earned.

### 3. [minor] How the blacklisted chef got a seat, and walked into the kitchen, is skipped
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 2
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "The cardstock at Table 4 bore a gilded crest…" Scene 2: "Julien slipped through the service vestibule, leaving the muffled velvet of the dining room…"
- **Problem:** The story says Julien can't even get work scrubbing pots, yet he's seated in André's dining room on review night. Nothing explains how (an alias, a booking, a favour). Then a diner in a black wool coat walks through the service doors and hides in the kitchen with no staff or doorway obstacle mentioned.
- **Reader needed:** One clause on how he got the reservation, and one beat on how he got past the front of house and the brigade unseen.

### 4. [minor] The brigade disappears from the blocking once Julien takes over
- **Where:** Scene 3
- **Quote:** "When his hand descended over the brass casing of the station blowtorch, the steel line went utterly still."
- **Problem:** Scene 2 fills the kitchen with an expeditor, Luc and "the brigade". In Scene 3 an outsider takes the torch, sets fire to the ticket and plates the critic's course, and the only reaction is that the line "went utterly still". No one is placed in the room or reacts, apart from a runner at the end.
- **Reader needed:** A glance at where the cooks and expeditor are and what they do while a stranger takes over the pass.

### 5. [minor] The spun-sugar dome appears from nowhere
- **Where:** Scene 3
- **Quote:** "From the sugar cage he lifted the dome of spun caramel—threads so thin they seemed woven from amber cobwebs—and lowered it over the quince"
- **Problem:** Scene 2 mentions sugar cages only as something André's tremor "would shatter". A finished dome ready at the station was never placed, so the reader has to accept a prop that's just there when it's needed.
- **Reader needed:** A mention in Scene 2 or early in Scene 3 of the finished caramel dome waiting at the pastry station.

### 6. [nit] "Dining syndicates" is used as if it were a known institution
- **Where:** Scene 1 (again in Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "set his whispering campaign through every dining syndicate in the capital"; Scene 3: "an emperor of the syndicates"
- **Problem:** The term suggests some formal guild with real power over hiring, but the story never says what a syndicate is. The reader has to guess.
- **Reader needed:** A few words on what the syndicates are (restaurant owners' guilds, critics' circles).

## What works
- Scene 1 anchors place, POV and cast quickly: "In the vaulted quiet of the L'Hiver dining room … Two banquettes away sat Giles Lambert, the chief critic".
- André's decline is shown physically instead of told: "forced two round white pills into his mouth, dry-swallowing them … rattling his wedding band against the metal in a frantic, stuttering drumroll."
- The handoffs between scenes are clean and continuous: Julien rises "his eyes locked on the swinging doors", then "slipped through the service vestibule".

## Out of lane
Table 4 in Scene 1 becomes "Table nine" in Scene 3, and the "braise" becomes a tart (consistency).
