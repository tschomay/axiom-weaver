# Setup and Payoff — The Apprentice's Bill

**Score: 3/5** — the tremor, smoker and mercy threads are well planted and pay off, but the critic, the story's weighted clock, is dropped, and the vow gets its payoff by the narrator saying so.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The critic is built up and then never used
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "Two banquettes away sat Giles Lambert, the chief critic whose ink-stained ledger had consecrated and ruined dining houses across three provinces, his napkin already tucked, his palate waiting for the legendary dessert." (Scene 1); "bearing the flawless dish out toward the critic seated under the dining room chandeliers." (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** The review night is the premise and the critic gets a full-weight introduction, but the story ends as the plate leaves the pass. The reader never learns what the critic tastes, judges or credits, so the "reckoning" has no public result.
- **Reader needed:** One beat that shows what the review does with the dish (or makes it plain that Julien no longer cares), so the critic's build-up leads somewhere.

### 2. [major] The vow is kept on a technicality, and the narrator declares it kept
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "he swore he would see André's name burned off the ticket before the final course ever cleared the pass." (Scene 1); "André Laurent's name vanished in a sharp hiss of blackening ash … The vow was kept." (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** In Scene 1 the vow reads as a promise to strip André of public credit, which is the wrong the menu line sets up. In Scene 3 it is met literally, by burning an internal kitchen ticket the critic never sees, and "The vow was kept" tells the reader to accept that as the payoff instead of letting them feel it.
- **Reader needed:** Something that shows the burning means what the vow meant (a transfer of credit, or a conscious choice to settle for a private act), rather than the narrator stating it.

### 3. [minor] The stolen notebooks and the blacklisting are raised as grievances and never come back
- **Where:** Scene 1
- **Quote:** "nor of the cellar notebooks André had confiscated on the morning he locked the pastry walk-in and cast Julien out." / "set his whispering campaign through every dining syndicate in the capital"
- **Problem:** These are the specific wrongs the reckoning should answer. Neither the notebooks nor the blacklist is mentioned again. André's "silent, pleading apology" (Scene 3) doesn't reach them, and Julien leaves still shut out.
- **Reader needed:** Some acknowledgement, even a glance or a single line, that the old theft and the career ruin were answered or deliberately left alone.

### 4. [minor] The rescue technique appears only when it is needed
- **Where:** Scene 3
- **Quote:** "He reached into the ice well, seized a measure of unpasteurized heavy cream chilled to near-freezing, and poured it in a thin, merciless ribbon directly into the weeping center."
- **Problem:** Scene 1 plants the failure carefully ("the fragile pectin matrix of the fruit would fail to set") but plants no way to fix it. The cold-cream rescue appears out of nowhere. Julien's expertise is established, so it is plausible, but it is not fair play.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier hint (from his three winters on the recipe) that a collapse can be saved, so the rescue reads as recognition and not invention.

### 5. [nit] The tremor reveal is restated at the scene break
- **Where:** Scene 2 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "The great hand had failed." (Scene 2 end); "The great hand had failed, rattling like dry lath against the edge of the pass" (Scene 3 opening)
- **Problem:** Scene 3 opens with the reveal Scene 2 has just closed on, so the reader is given the same revelation twice back to back.
- **Reader needed:** Scene 3 should start from the consequence and not restate the reveal.

### 6. [nit] "Before the glass clouds" has no referent
- **Where:** Scene 3
- **Quote:** "\"Table nine,\" Julien said, his voice level and dead as cold slate. \"Before the glass clouds.\""
- **Problem:** The line is delivered like a callback, but no glass (sugar or otherwise) that could cloud was set up earlier, so it falls flat.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier mention of what "the glass" is, or a plainer line.

## What works
- The tremor is planted with a clear consequence and paid off exactly: "an uncontrollable shaking that would shatter fine sugar cages" (Scene 2) → "lowered it over the quince without the tremble of a single filament" (Scene 3).
- The overheating smoker is a fair plant for the collapse: "the fragile pectin matrix of the fruit would fail to set" (Scene 1) → "the scorched smoke infusion broke the delicate pectin matrix" (Scene 3).
- André's mercy to Luc sets up Julien's turn without the story explaining it: "He had come expecting to look upon an emperor gorging himself on stolen grain. He had not prepared for mercy." (Scene 2). The "emperor" image returns in Scene 3, and the reversal of "tend the hearth while it fell" (Scene 1) is left for the reader to see.

## Out of lane
Julien sits at "Table 4" and the critic is "two banquettes away", yet the dish goes to "Table nine" (consistency).
