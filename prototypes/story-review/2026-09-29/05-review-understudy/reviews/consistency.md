# Logical Consistency — Understudy

**Score: 2/5** — several major contradictions: the story's central secret (the forged cue) changes form every time it is told, and key objects and tickets reset between scenes, so the reader stops trusting details.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The forged cue is a different forgery every time it is described
- **Where:** Scene 6, Scene 8, Scene 9, Scene 12 (also Scene 4)
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "the faded ruled grid of Act Two, the smeared aniline ink, and the altered cue that pushed Maren Gyles's entrance twelve bars past her music."
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "the graphite scrubbed away until the fibres frayed, beneath which Hester's narrow, slanted hand had penciled the false cue twenty bars early."
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "The ink you lifted with a knife to delay Maren's entrance." / Scene 12: "It was the third act ... I drew the nib through the margin and set your cue two speeches late"
- **Problem:** The same act of sabotage is late by twelve bars, early by twenty bars, and late by two speeches. It is done in ink, in pencil over an erasure, with a knife, and with a nib, and it falls in Act Two in one telling and the third act in another. Maren's own account also moves her. In Scene 4 she "stood petrified behind the second flat", and in Scene 8 she "sat in the dressing cubicle ... listening for a call boy". The ledger is the "1913 Lyceum" book, but the incident happened "at the Prince's" / "in Manchester".
- **Reader needed:** One fixed account of the forgery (direction, amount, tool, act, venue), so that each re-telling confirms it instead of contradicting it.

### 2. [major] The 1913 ledger and the recast rider reset to their Scene 1 positions
- **Where:** Scene 3, Scene 5, Scene 6, Scene 8, Scene 17 (also Scene 1)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "Hester slides the book into her black lacquered deed box, turns the small brass key with a sharp click, and slips the key into the bosom of her shift."
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "I spent two days in the damp vaults beneath the Lyceum before our coach departed. The 1913 archive ledger, Hester."
- **Quote:** Scene 17: "beside the West End transfer recast rider that Kemper had signed ... and the dog-eared 1913 Lyceum production cue ledger ... lies the cast-brass dagger"
- **Problem:** Hester locked the ledger away in her own box, yet Kemper studies it "in the vaults". Maren later holds a page "torn long ago from the prompt book", and at the end the whole ledger is back on the vanity as if nothing happened. The rider has the same problem. It was "unexecuted" on the vanity (Scenes 1 and 3), then Kemper slipped it "back into his leather portfolio" (Scene 5), and now it is on the vanity again, signed.
- **Reader needed:** A consistent chain of custody for the ledger (and the torn leaf) and for the rider.

### 3. [major] Arthur's train ticket changes: origin, time, and who bought it
- **Where:** Scene 5 and Scene 15
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "That green ticket is a non-refundable seat on the eight-fifteen morning express out of Leeds the morning after our final provincial curtain."
- **Quote:** Scene 15: "It is the single third-class passage from Oakhaven Junction down to the Devon coast ... 'The morning express departs at seven, Arthur.'"
- **Problem:** Arthur already showed Hester his own ticket for 8:15 from Leeds. Now Hester hands him a different ticket from a different town at a different hour, as though he had none. She took it "before Kemper's solicitors arrived", but no solicitors appear anywhere in the story.
- **Reader needed:** Either the same ticket (Leeds, 8:15) or a stated reason why Arthur's plans changed.

### 4. [minor] The tour route goes somewhere the story said it would not
- **Where:** Scene 5, Scenes 10–12, Scenes 16–18
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "We finish Harrowgate, we finish Leeds, and Clara assumes the role for the capital."
- **Quote:** Scene 16: "The company sleeper coach rolls through the night toward Blackwood"
- **Problem:** The itinerary was fixed as Harrowgate, then Leeds, then London. Instead the company plays an unmentioned Oakhaven, never reaches Leeds, and ends with Clara's debut back at the Blackwood Lyceum where the tour started.
- **Reader needed:** The itinerary in the prose should match the stops the story actually visits.

### 5. [minor] The escrow sum doubles and then doubles again
- **Where:** Scene 5 and Scene 12
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "You lose five thousand pounds before the curtain even lifts in St. Martin's Lane."
- **Quote:** Scene 12: "You will not risk twenty thousand pounds simply to punish an old quarrel."
- **Problem:** Hester quotes the same escrow penalty at two different amounts.
- **Reader needed:** One figure.

### 6. [minor] Elapsed time and season don't add up
- **Where:** Scene 3, Scene 8, Scene 9 (also Scene 14)
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "the faint gray scorch-mark of the iron she had pressed into the velvet hem three nights ago."
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "a draft that smells of scorched dust and the sulk of late August thunder" vs. Scene 9: "A cold November rain beats against the high clerestory windows"
- **Problem:** The scorching happened the night after Scene 3 (Scene 4). The coach left that night (Scene 5), Harrowgate is "the morning" after (Scene 6), and Scenes 7–8 are that same afternoon. That makes it one night ago, not three. Across those same one or two days the weather jumps from late August to November. Also, the scorch was on the "shoulder-cape" (Scene 4), not the hem.
- **Reader needed:** Durations and season that follow from the scene sequence.

### 7. [minor] Clara's London contract is revealed twice as news
- **Where:** Scene 5 and Scene 12
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "'Kemper has signed Clara for St. Martin's.'"
- **Quote:** Scene 12: "'Miss Byrne executed her solo agreement three hours before this evening's rehearsal commenced ... The contract is executed, Hester.'"
- **Problem:** Hester already knew and told Arthur that Clara was signed. In Scene 12 the signing is delivered as a fresh blow, and it is dated to the day of Scene 12.
- **Reader needed:** Either Scene 5 as intent only (not yet signed), or Scene 12 framed as something other than a reveal.

### 8. [nit] Small drift in fixed details
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 14; Scene 9, Scene 13, Scene 15
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "stays wedged till the municipal inspector stamps the clearance book at nine tomorrow morning" vs. Scene 14: "we're shuttered through tomorrow night's curtain. The council inspector doesn't take his tea until ten."
- **Problem:** The safety-curtain rule shifts between a morning reset (at nine, or at ten) and a full day's closure. Arthur's role is "Lord Malory" (Scenes 2, 15) but "the duke" / "the Duke's velvet mantle" (Scenes 13, 15). He is Hester's stage husband "through twelve long winters" (Scene 9) against eleven years everywhere else.
- **Reader needed:** One version of the rule, one title for the role, and one span of years.

## What works
- The prop dagger's lead plug is set up precisely and paid off exactly: Scene 1 "the concealed lead counterweight sat plugged inside the brass hilt", and Scene 17 "the gray cylinder of lead slides out".
- The scorch carries forward as physical evidence: Scene 4 "the pile flattens, hisses, and begins to scorch", then Scene 8 "the velvet mantle that still bears the faint, singed ghost of Maren's flatiron".
- The safety-curtain threat set up in Scene 2 ("No hauling it back up by hand") is respected in Scene 14 and closed off in Scene 18: "the safety curtain did not fall".

## Out of lane
Clara goes back to forcing Hester's growl in Scene 11 right after her triumph in her own voice in Scene 9. That is a motivation question for the character reviewer.
