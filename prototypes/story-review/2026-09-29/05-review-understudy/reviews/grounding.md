# Grounding and Detail — Understudy

**Score: 3/5** — Most scenes open with a clear room and a clear point of view, but several load-bearing things are never shown: where the incriminating ledger went, whether Hester is still in the company after she is fired, why the tour is in Oakhaven and then back in Blackwood, and where Arthur's ticket came from. The reader has to piece these together while the emotional arc carries on.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The ledger moves between hands without being shown
- **Where:** Scene 3, Scene 6, Scene 8 (also recurs in Scene 17)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "Hester slides the book into her black lacquered deed box, turns the small brass key with a sharp click, and slips the key into the bosom of her shift." Scene 6: "I spent two days in the damp vaults beneath the Lyceum before our coach departed. The 1913 archive ledger, Hester."
- **Problem:** The reader last saw the ledger locked in Hester's own box, with the key on her body. Kemper then says he found it in the Lyceum vaults. In Scene 8 Maren holds "a single leaf of foolscap, torn long ago from the prompt book" that "Kemper left … upon the prompt desk", although in Scene 6 Kemper showed a "photostat negative" and "slips the paper back into his breast pocket." In Scene 17 the ledger is back "upon the scarred vanity table." Each time the ledger or page turns up, it reads as a claim, not as something that happened.
- **Reader needed:** One line showing how Kemper got the ledger out of Hester's locked box (or saying there are two ledgers), and how the torn leaf reached Maren.

### 2. [major] Hester's status after she is fired is never established
- **Where:** Scene 6 → Scene 9 (recurs in Scenes 12 and 18)
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "I am not asking for your blessing. I am serving your termination for cause." Scene 9: "Hester clings to the iron stay-wire of the prompt wing … watching Clara Byrne die under the harsh white spill of the lime."
- **Problem:** Scene 9 opens with Clara already playing the role in front of an audience, and Hester in a "cardigan" in the wings. Nothing says whether the firing took effect, how Clara came to go on, or which town or night this is. In Scene 12 Hester is again arguing "You cannot touch me, Donald," and in Scene 18 she is still dressing Clara in the wings. The reader has to guess whether Hester is fired, suspended, or still under contract.
- **Reader needed:** A sentence at the top of Scene 9 saying that Clara has been put on in Hester's place, and on what terms Hester is still with the company.

### 3. [major] Moves to new towns are unexplained: Oakhaven, then back to Blackwood
- **Where:** Scene 10 (recurs in Scenes 16–17)
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "We finish Harrowgate, we finish Leeds, and Clara assumes the role for the capital." Scene 10: "The silence in the Oakhaven Theatre Royal had none of the hollow, resonant peace of London houses"
- **Problem:** The only itinerary the reader was given is Harrowgate, then Leeds, then London. Scene 10 jumps to Oakhaven, a town never mentioned before, with no time cue and a sudden switch to past tense. Scene 16 then puts the coach "toward Blackwood," and Scenes 17–18 play out at the Lyceum where the story began. Nothing says why the tour has gone back there rather than on to Leeds or St. Martin's Lane.
- **Reader needed:** A short cue at each move saying where the company is on its route and how much time has passed.

### 4. [major] Arthur's ticket comes from an offstage event
- **Where:** Scene 15 (set up in Scene 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 15: "the small rectangle of pasteboard she had taken from the green room desk before Kemper's solicitors arrived. It is the single third-class passage from Oakhaven Junction down to the Devon coast"
- **Problem:** "Kemper's solicitors" arriving is never shown or mentioned anywhere else. Arthur already showed his own ticket in Scene 5 ("a non-refundable seat on the eight-fifteen morning express out of Leeds"). The reader cannot tell whose ticket this is, why it was lying on a desk, or why Hester is the one handing it over, so the gesture has nothing to stand on.
- **Reader needed:** Where this ticket came from, and how it relates to the Leeds ticket Arthur already has.

### 5. [minor] The past sabotage is described too loosely to picture
- **Where:** Scene 4, Scene 6, Scene 8, Scene 12
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "where the entrance of the sister once stood ruled in red ink … Hester's narrow, slanted hand had penciled the false cue twenty bars early." Scene 12: "I drew the nib through the margin and set your cue two speeches late"
- **Problem:** This is the core of the reveal: how a changed cue in the prompt book made Maren miss her entrance. It is described as "twelve bars past her music" in Scene 6, "twenty bars early" in pencil over an erasure in Scene 8, and "two speeches late" in ink in Scene 12. The "sister" role Maren was playing is never introduced. The reader cannot build a single picture of how the trick worked.
- **Reader needed:** One concrete version of the trick: which part Maren was playing, what the cue said, and how the call boy passed the wrong time on to her.

### 6. [minor] Scene 1 names documents and people as if the reader already knows them
- **Where:** Scene 1 (similar in Scene 6)
- **Quote:** "Clara, whose lyric soprano had made Donald Kemper dream of West End transfers, hovered near the vanity where the West End transfer recast rider lay unexecuted beside the dog-eared 1913 Lyceum production cue ledger."
- **Problem:** Kemper has no job attached until Scene 2. The "recast rider" and "cue ledger" are dropped in like props from a list, with nothing to say why they matter. The rider's meaning only becomes clear in Scene 5. Similarly, in Scene 6 Hester scorns "Kemper … his train tickets," but the only ticket the reader has seen is Arthur's.
- **Reader needed:** A few words saying who Kemper is, and a hint of what the rider is, when each first appears.

### 7. [minor] The same props and character labels are explained again and again
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 3, 17 (dagger); Scenes 2, 7, 11 (Toby); Scenes 1, 3, 17 (rider and ledger)
- **Quote:** Scene 17: "fashioned years ago with a hollow screw-cap pommel designed to bear an illicit slug of poured lead—a hidden ballast that had lent Hester's climactic stage death an artificial, thunderous rattle"
- **Problem:** The dagger's lead plug is fully explained in Scene 1, mentioned again in Scene 3, and explained a third time in Scene 17. Toby is introduced as "the company stage manager" three separate times, and Kemper is given a new label ("the tour manager") in Scene 12. Each repeat pauses the scene to re-explain something the reader already knows.
- **Reader needed:** Nothing more. Later mentions can rely on the earlier setup.

## What works
- Scene 2 plants the safety-curtain rules clearly ("it stays wedged till the municipal inspector stamps the clearance book at nine tomorrow morning"). That makes Hester's hand on the lever in Scene 14 immediately readable, and "the safety curtain did not fall" in Scene 18 lands.
- Scene 1's opening sentences settle place, point of view and who is present straight away: "The gas jets in Lyceum Dressing Room 1, Blackwood, hissed … Hester Vance stood rigid upon the mounting block."
- Scene 5 gives the coach a real layout the reader can move through: "She steps out of the lounge cubicle and into the narrow, darkened corridor between the sleeper berths. Near the rear axle … Arthur Phelan sits wedged upon a bench."

## Out of lane
Details that don't agree with each other (ledger dated 1913 versus "eleven years ago"; Clara's voice ruined in Scene 7 but perfect in Scene 10; a lead plug described as "hollow" yet landing with a "deadened thud") belong to the consistency reviewer.
