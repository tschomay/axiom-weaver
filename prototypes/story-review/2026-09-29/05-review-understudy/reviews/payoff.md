# Setup and Payoff — Understudy

**Score: 3/5** — Hester's arc and the dagger, voice and safety-curtain threads all pay off, but the story's main secret (the forged 1913 cue) is revealed four times, and two heavily weighted setups (the locked deed box and Hester's failing breath) never pay off.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The forged-cue secret is revealed four times
- **Where:** Scene 6, Scene 8, Scene 9, Scene 12
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "the altered cue that pushed Maren Gyles's entrance twelve bars past her music. 'Your handwriting is unmistakable'" / Scene 8: "beneath which Hester's narrow, slanted hand had penciled the false cue twenty bars early" / Scene 9: "he showed me the old ledger from eleven years ago. The altered entry, Hester." / Scene 12: "I drew the nib through the margin and set your cue two speeches late"
- **Problem:** Kemper's reveal in Scene 6 lands hard. Then Maren (Scene 8), Arthur (Scene 9) and Hester's own confession (Scene 12) each present the same forgery as if it were news, with a different piece of evidence each time. Scene 12 even admits nothing new has happened: "She had known it for days, perhaps for years, and having the truth spoken aloud alters nothing." After the first reveal, each repeat has less effect.
- **Reader needed:** One reveal, with the later scenes carrying new information (what it cost Maren, Arthur's part in it, Hester's motive) instead of fresh proof of the same fact.

### 2. [major] The locked deed box and key are set up with weight, then ignored
- **Where:** Scene 3, then Scene 6 and Scene 17
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "Hester slides the book into her black lacquered deed box, turns the small brass key with a sharp click, and slips the key into the bosom of her shift." / Scene 6: "I spent two days in the damp vaults beneath the Lyceum before our coach departed. The 1913 archive ledger, Hester."
- **Problem:** Scene 3 closes on Hester hiding the ledger and keeping the key on her body, which reads as a promise that someone will have to get past that lock. Instead, Kemper finds the evidence "in the vaults", and the box and key never come up again. In Scene 17 the ledger is simply lying on the vanity. It isn't clear whether the ledger was ever taken, or how, so the hiding scene goes nowhere.
- **Reader needed:** Either the lock matters to how the forgery comes out, or the hiding moment shouldn't be given a scene-ending beat.

### 3. [major] Hester's failing breath is made to look like the reason for her replacement, then dropped
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 3 (never picked up later)
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "Kemper says nothing... while his cold eyes measure every hitch in Hester's chest against the ledger sheets clasped tightly against his ribs." / Scene 3: "her breath catches in her throat—short, sharp hitches that she strangles behind clenched teeth"
- **Problem:** Arthur's warning ("You won't make the third-act curse"), the lozenge, and the producer silently watching her breathing all suggest her health will be the cause of her fall. Kemper's actual reasons are ticket sales (Scene 5) and fraud (Scene 6), and the breathing is never mentioned again. Her failed voice in Scene 13 is framed as emotional emptiness, not the illness hinted at earlier.
- **Reader needed:** The breathing hitches should feed into the replacement or the collapse, or they shouldn't be staged as something Kemper is recording.

### 4. [minor] Hester's train-ticket gift repeats a ticket Arthur already had
- **Where:** Scene 5, then Scene 15
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "That green ticket is a non-refundable seat on the eight-fifteen morning express out of Leeds the morning after our final provincial curtain." / Scene 15: "It is the single third-class passage from Oakhaven Junction down to the Devon coast... 'The morning express departs at seven, Arthur.'"
- **Problem:** The gesture that is supposed to resolve Arthur's thread gives him something he already owned, with a different station and time. It feels unconnected to the setup, when it should feel earned by it.
- **Reader needed:** The goodbye should build on the Exmouth ticket and cottage from Scene 5 (for example, releasing him before Leeds), not replace them with a new ticket.

### 5. [minor] Clara's triumph in Scene 9 comes before the lesson it depends on
- **Where:** Scene 9, then Scene 11 and Scene 16
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "She has crumpled across the painted stone steps not as an unyielding tyrant defying heaven, but as a mother, broken-hearted and frail" / Scene 16: "Let Lady Malory sing with tragic softness... Sing her grief, not my rage."
- **Problem:** In Scene 9 Clara has already found the soft, grieving Malory and brought the house down. In Scene 11 she is back to forcing Hester's growl, and Hester's teaching in Scene 16 is presented as a discovery. The later lesson and the Scene 18 ovation feel like a repeat of Scene 9, not its resolution. It also isn't clear why Clara went on in Scene 9 at all.
- **Reader needed:** Either Clara's first success is partial or accidental, or her slide back into the growl is motivated, so that Scene 16 feels like progress.

### 6. [minor] The dagger's lead is recast as "illicit" only when it pays off
- **Where:** Scene 1, then Scene 17
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "the heavy hollow crash calibrated to carry straight to the farthest gallery row. The lead plug held tight. The sound was correct." / Scene 17: "designed to bear an illicit slug of poured lead—a hidden ballast that had lent Hester's climactic stage death an artificial, thunderous rattle"
- **Problem:** The dagger is planted fairly and paid off well, but Scene 1 presents the counterweight as legitimate stagecraft. The idea that it is a cheat (illicit, artificial) is stated by the narrator at the payoff, not planted earlier.
- **Reader needed:** One early hint that the plug was Hester's own secret embellishment, not standard props work.

### 7. [nit] The hollow-self realisation is told twice
- **Where:** Scene 13 and Scene 18
- **Quote:** Scene 13: "there is no core underneath, no secret person who had been waiting for the curtain to fall" / Scene 18: "there was only a vast and hollow silence where Hester Vance ought to have been"
- **Problem:** Scene 18 has the narrator restate the Scene 13 epiphany almost word for word. Hester smiling and staying in the wings already shows it.
- **Reader needed:** Let the final image carry the change without explaining it again.

## What works
- The safety-curtain rule set up in Scene 2 ("No hauling it back up by hand, no second chances before dawn") makes the temptation in Scene 14 believable, and Scene 18 closes it quietly with "the safety curtain did not fall."
- Hester's early creed becomes her ending without being pointed out: Scene 3 has "You must be the ghost who never walks," and Scene 18 has "content to remain unseen in the peaceful quiet of the wings."
- Clara's voice thread is planted and paid off step by step: "lyric soprano" (Scene 1), then strain (Scene 7), then the ballad (Scene 10), then aphonia (Scene 11), then the breath lesson (Scene 16).

## Out of lane
Consistency: the escrow sum is five thousand pounds in Scene 5 and twenty thousand in Scene 12; the forged delay is "twelve bars", "twenty bars early" and "two speeches".
