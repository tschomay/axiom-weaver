# Grounding and Detail: Toll

**Score: 3/5**: Most scenes are set precisely in space, with elevations, galleries and sheds. But the aftermath of the climax happens offstage, and several people and facts arrive as if already known, so the reader has to reconstruct them.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The aftermath of the gallery fight is skipped
- **Where:** Scene 12 (set against Scene 11)
- **Quote:** "tumbled backward over the edge, crashing onto the sump grating twelve feet below with a sickening thud that ended in silence." (Scene 11); "We took the six-foot cheater pipe to the dog-latch, Vance. It won't dog back." (Scene 12)
- **Problem:** Scene 11 ends with Pike possibly dead, Kroll's shoulder "shattered", and Teresa opening the scour gate in front of him. Scene 12 has all three survivors standing together on the parapet the next morning. Callow briefs Teresa like a colleague, and nobody mentions Pike, the dynamite, or the fact that she opened the gate. Whether she was caught, protected, or simply ignored overnight is left for the reader to guess.
- **Reader needed:** A few lines on what happened between the gallery and dawn: Pike's fate, and why Teresa is standing free on the crest.

### 2. [minor] Kroll gets to the crest before Teresa
- **Where:** Scene 9 (set against Scene 8)
- **Quote:** "Teresa dipped her head in silent acknowledgment ... and walked past him into the echoing vault." (Scene 8); "Arthur Kroll stood braced against the brass railing of the spillway overlook ... He had not fallen in the gallery; the spasm had passed" (Scene 9)
- **Problem:** Teresa leaves Kroll collapsed against a penstock deep in the dam. Her next stop is the crest, and Kroll is already there waiting. The story never says how much time passed or how a man who could barely stand got there first.
- **Reader needed:** A time cue, or a line showing Kroll going up ahead of her.

### 3. [minor] Kroll's position keeps changing
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 3, Scene 5 (also Scene 12)
- **Quote:** "the thin, miserable guilt of a junior director" (Scene 1); "The Board had not made him an officer; they had left him here to mind the leakages" (Scene 3); "Arthur Kroll is sitting up in the glass tower trimming five percent off the maintenance ledger every Tuesday" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Kroll is a surveyor, then a ditch-raker, then a budget-cutter in the glass tower, and then he has a district stamp on the key locker. His rank decides why he patrols the galleries and why he has the karst surveys, and the reader can't place him.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent statement of Kroll's current job after the ten-year gap.

### 4. [minor] Backstory arrives as if already known
- **Where:** Scene 9 and Scene 10 (also Scene 11)
- **Quote:** "You walk like Caleb" (Scene 9); "Nomi was under the lean-to behind their uncle's packing shed" ... "the grief that had marked their first nights in the tents" ... "He forged the diversion notices." (Scene 10); "Julian Pike, the diver technician who had unbolted the inspection hatch" (Scene 11)
- **Problem:** The father's name, the uncle, the tents, the forged notices and the inspection hatch all appear with the definite article or as settled fact, and none of them was introduced. The forgery charge in particular is a new accusation with nothing on the page behind it.
- **Reader needed:** Each of these introduced once before it's leaned on, or dropped.

### 5. [minor] Pike's turn to dynamite comes from nowhere
- **Where:** Scene 11 (set against Scene 5)
- **Quote:** "Get the brass keys, open the bypass, and let Kroll swallow the water himself." (Scene 5); "Bundles of commercial dynamite lay open on an oilcloth ... 'They took my river,' Julian snarled" (Scene 11)
- **Problem:** Pike's own grievance ("my river") and his switch from helper to bomber both happen offstage. The reader learns his motive at the moment he acts on it.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier hint of what the dam took from Pike.

### 6. [minor] Scene 3 starts at an event the reader never sees
- **Where:** Scene 3
- **Quote:** "Teresa had slipped away while the speeches were thickest, trailing the solitary figure down the switchbacks until the noise of the municipal band thinned"
- **Problem:** The ten-year jump is signalled clearly, but the ceremony (what is being celebrated?) and "the solitary figure" (whom she is following) arrive unexplained. Nothing covers the decade in which Teresa became a certified hydraulic fitter, and Scene 4 depends on that.
- **Reader needed:** A clause naming the occasion and a sentence on what Teresa did with those ten years.

### 7. [minor] Scene 11 opens with a recap
- **Where:** Scene 11
- **Quote:** "his cracked voice cutting through the rising squall to lay bare the truth he had hidden for years: the deep gypsum cavern yawning in the northern canyon wall, an unstable karst pocket that would have swallowed the cliffs"
- **Problem:** The opening paragraph retells the karst revelation from Scene 9, which Scene 10 has also just gone over. Three tellings in a row stall the run into the climax.
- **Reader needed:** One short reminder at most, then straight into the gallery.

### 8. [nit] Kroll re-identified with a clumsy appositive
- **Where:** Scene 8
- **Quote:** "\"Who is down here without a chit?\" Arthur Kroll asked, the engineer who had driven the stakes through the Vance orchard in his patched canvas waders"
- **Problem:** The reader has known Kroll since Scene 1. Re-introducing him mid-line interrupts the dialogue tag.
- **Reader needed:** Just his name.

## What works
- The heist is carefully set up before it happens. Callow explains the interlock in Scene 4, Pike places the key locker in Scene 5, and Scene 7 pays both off: "That emergency gate will not budge an inch unless the secondary pressure release cylinder is engaged first with the master brass interlock key." (Scene 4)
- Precise elevations keep the geography readable, anchored to the carved 740: "four numerals were bitten into the shoulder of the gorge: 740." (Scene 2)
- The aquifer stakes are planted in Scene 6 and pay off in Scene 13: "It's the reservoir's pressure that keeps these apples sweet on the bough. The whole settlement drinks from the seepage." (Scene 6)

## Out of lane
For the consistency reviewer: the time since the flood is "ten years" (Scene 3), "three springs" and "weeks" (Scene 10), "twenty years" (Scenes 9 and 11), and "three summers" (Scene 12).
