# Character Coherence — Toll

**Score: 2/5** — Several key characters act as the plot needs. Teresa's climactic choice goes against what she has just learned, Pike exists to supply access and then a villain turn, and Kroll and Callow shrug off the sabotage. Nomi and Teresa's early arc hold up well.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Teresa opens the gate she has learned will collapse the mountain
- **Where:** Scene 11 (against Scene 9)
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "To vent the scour gate… meant unseating that ballast. It meant tearing the plug from a hollow mountain." Scene 11: "'The town will endure silt,' Teresa said, the decision falling like a portcullis. 'They will not drown in a tidal wave.'"
- **Problem:** Teresa backed off in Scene 9 because draining the basin removes the ballast over the karst. In Scene 11 she explains the karst danger to Pike herself. She then drains the basin anyway, and her justification treats draining as the safe option, which is the reverse of what she understood. The narration, "yet the vow of the dry hearth pulled at her like an open wound", states the pull but doesn't show why it beats everything she now knows.
- **Reader needed:** Either a reason Teresa believes a slow scour avoids the collapse, or an on-page moment where she knowingly chooses the vow over the valley.

### 2. [major] Nobody reacts to Julian's fall or to Teresa's crime
- **Where:** Scene 11, Scene 12 (and Scene 13)
- **Quote:** Scene 11: "crashing onto the sump grating twelve feet below with a sickening thud that ended in silence." Scene 12: "'We took the six-foot cheater pipe to the dog-latch, Vance. It won't dog back.'"
- **Problem:** Teresa may have killed a man, and she never thinks about him again. The next morning Callow reports to her like a colleague. Kroll, whose shoulder she has watched being shattered and whom she defied to open the gate, stands beside her and only murmurs. The scale of the reactions is far too small for the events.
- **Reader needed:** Some acknowledgement of what happened to Pike, and of Teresa's standing as the saboteur, from her and from the men she wronged.

### 3. [major] Pike is a plot device with a late grievance
- **Where:** Scene 5 and Scene 11
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "'You want the bypass line for the bottom scour gate,' Pike said softly. It was not a question." Scene 11: "'They took my river,' Julian snarled… 'I am putting an end to Crestview.'"
- **Problem:** Pike guesses a stranger's secret plan, tells her where the keys are and volunteers to create a diversion, all on first meeting and with no reason given. His only visible complaint in Scene 5 is Kroll's cost-cutting. The loss of "my river" that drives his dynamite plan first appears in the line where he announces it. He supplies access, then supplies an obstacle.
- **Reader needed:** An early glimpse of Pike's own loss, so that both his help and his turn to dynamite come from the same person.

### 4. [minor] Kroll recognises Teresa and still lets her keep the key
- **Where:** Scene 9 (against Scene 8)
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "He waved a frail, dismissive hand toward the northern corridor, not even glancing at the cabinet behind her." Scene 9: "'I knew the name the morning you handed your papers to Callow. Teresa Vance.'"
- **Problem:** Kroll has known from the start that the evicted daughter is working his dam. He found her at the scour-key locker as it was being robbed and then followed her to the crest, yet he never asks for the key back, even after persuading her. He is careful everywhere else, but not about the one thing that matters.
- **Reader needed:** A reason Kroll leaves the key with her, such as trust, exhaustion or a test, or a failed attempt to take it back.

### 5. [minor] Kroll's rank and bearing keep changing
- **Where:** Scene 3, Scene 5, Scene 11 (and Scene 1)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "The Board had not made him an officer; they had left him here to mind the leakages, a spent clerk ankle-deep in the silt." Scene 5: "Arthur Kroll is sitting up in the glass tower trimming five percent off the maintenance ledger every Tuesday."
- **Problem:** Kroll is described in turn as a guilty junior director, a broken ditch-raker, a cost-cutting director in the tower, and "an old keeper" with "desperate authority". Each version suits its scene, but together they don't add up to one man with one position.
- **Reader needed:** One settled picture of Kroll's standing, so that Teresa's view of him can change without the man himself changing.

### 6. [minor] Everyone talks like a hydraulic engineer
- **Where:** Scene 6 (and Scenes 9, 10, 13)
- **Quote:** Scene 6 (Nomi): "The weight behind that wall forces five thousand miners' inches of water through the deep river silt every hour of the day, feeding the gravel strata right under our boots."
- **Problem:** Nomi the fruit-sorter, Kroll the engineer, Pike the diver and Teresa all explain the dam in the same technical register. When Nomi is lecturing on head pressure, her lines could belong to Kroll. Her strongest lines are the plain ones.
- **Reader needed:** Nomi's case made in the terms of her own life, as it is in "Leave the masonry alone… Let the dead stone drown."

### 7. [nit] Teresa's final line has no clear referent
- **Where:** Scene 13
- **Quote:** "'They were going to take it anyway,' Teresa whispered."
- **Problem:** It's unclear who "they" are and what "it" is. The line is meant to show Teresa's self-justification breaking down, but nothing earlier sets it up.
- **Reader needed:** A defence the reader has heard Teresa make before, so that it lands as that defence failing.

## What works
- Teresa's refusal to pity Kroll in Scene 3 is dramatised and in character: "To forgive a ruined man was merely an easier way to abandon the dead. His misery was not restitution; it was only an evasion."
- Nomi is consistent and has her own stake from start to finish. Her closing line is the story's best character beat: "We have our hearth back, Teresa. You gave us back our hearth."
- The flat aftermath of the vow fits the reckoning the story builds toward: "She stood on the stone. The vow was kept. It felt like nothing at all."

## Out of lane
The eviction is served on the sisters in Scene 1, but Scenes 9 and 10 say Kroll gave "Father" the seventy-two hours (consistency).
