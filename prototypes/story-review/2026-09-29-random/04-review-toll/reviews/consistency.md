# Logical Consistency — Toll

**Score: 2/5** — several major contradictions: the time since the flood, how early the flood came, Kroll's rank, and the karst rule that the climax quietly drops. Numbers drift from scene to scene, and the reader learns to distrust every figure.

## Findings

### 1. [major] How long ago the flood was changes from weeks to three years to ten to twenty
- **Where:** Scene 3, Scene 4, Scene 5, Scene 9, Scene 10, Scene 12
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "Ten years of impounded water lay over the valley floor"; "the datum line carved into the granite ten winters ago"; Scene 4: "sweating since the tenth-year surcharge"
- **Quote (later):** Scene 5: "Kroll flooded your ditch three weeks early"; "before the ribbon-cutting"; Scene 10: "the grief that had marked their first nights in the tents" / "weeks of salvage and smoke"; Scene 12: "where the water had rested for three summers" / "three years of dark submersion"; Scene 9: "weighed twenty years of bitter smoke"
- **Problem:** The gap between the eviction and the heist is ten years in Scenes 3–4. Scene 10 frames it as weeks of tent life, Scene 12 as three summers, and Scene 9 as twenty years of grievance. A dam still awaiting its ribbon-cutting can't also have a tenth-year surcharge.
- **Reader needed:** One fixed span between Scene 2 and Scene 3, used everywhere after.

### 2. [major] The karst rule is dropped at the climax
- **Where:** Scene 9 and Scene 11, against Scenes 12–13
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "The only thing that could hold the roof of that karst cavern was the brute hydrostatic weight of sixty billion gallons of water"; "To vent the scour gate ... meant unseating that ballast. It meant tearing the plug from a hollow mountain."
- **Quote (later):** Scene 11: "The town will endure silt ... They will not drown in a tidal wave." Scenes 12–13: the lake drains below 740 feet overnight and the only consequence is dry wells.
- **Problem:** The story states firmly that draining the reservoir brings down the ridge and kills everyone to the delta within forty minutes, and Teresa accepts this in Scene 9. In Scene 11 she reasons that the scour gate only means silt. The lake then empties and the cavern does nothing, except for Kroll's passing "the northern caverns will answer for it". The rule that turned her in Scene 9 disappears without comment.
- **Reader needed:** Either the collapse threat stays in play once the lake drains, or the story explains why a slow scour drain doesn't trigger it.

### 3. [major] How early the flood came: 48 hours, three weeks, "seventy hours"
- **Where:** Scene 1, against Scene 5 and Scene 9
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "you gave us seventy-two hours. We have two days before the diversion closes." / "Forty-eight hours early."
- **Quote (later):** Scene 5: "Kroll flooded your ditch three weeks early to beat the autumn rain"; Scene 9: "You stole seventy hours from him ... You cut the river twenty hours after the ink dried."
- **Problem:** Scene 1 sets the theft at exactly 48 hours, with the flooding that same morning. Scene 5 makes it three weeks. Teresa, who was there, makes it seventy hours in Scene 9, with a twenty-hour gap after the notice. At most one of these can be true, and Teresa's own version contradicts what the reader watched.
- **Reader needed:** One figure for the stolen time, matching Scene 1, especially from Teresa.

### 4. [major] Kroll is a broken ditch clerk and also the Director in the glass tower
- **Where:** Scene 3, against Scene 5 and Scene 7
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "The Board had not made him an officer; they had left him here to mind the leakages, a spent clerk ankle-deep in the silt"
- **Quote (later):** Scene 5: "Arthur Kroll is sitting up in the glass tower trimming five percent off the maintenance ledger every Tuesday ... the Director wants his balance sheets pristine"; "Kroll keeps the duplicate in his wall safe"; Scene 7: "Arthur Kroll's district stamp was pressed into the soft metal"
- **Problem:** The same man is shown raking a culvert at the settlement and then described as the dam's budget-cutting director, with a wall safe and his own seal. Callow also shifts from "the shift supervisor" (Scene 4) to "The chief operator" (Scene 12).
- **Reader needed:** One rank for Kroll, and one for Callow.

### 5. [minor] Flood depths, speeds and places don't add up
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 2, Scene 3, Scene 4/5, Scene 11
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "the bottom lands take six feet of water before dusk"; Scene 2: "The water had taken forty years in forty minutes" / "beneath forty fathoms"; Scene 3: "six hundred feet of black silt and drowned loam pressing down upon ... her family's farmstead"
- **Problem:** A rise of six feet by dusk becomes a chimney swallowed within minutes. The crest is at 1,020 feet and the hearth at 740, so there can't be 600 feet of anything above the farm. Other figures drift too. The turbine gallery is "six hundred and eighty feet" (Scene 5) but also "the six-hundred level" (Scenes 4, 9). The settlement huddles "beneath the spillway's shadow" (Scene 3) but also "sits four miles down the gorge" (Scene 11).
- **Reader needed:** Elevations, depths and distances that agree with the crest and hearth figures the story gives.

### 6. [minor] Father's chisel is found twice and changes shape
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 2 (drift recurs in Scenes 6, 7, 9, 12)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "Teresa Vance worked the forged-carbon steel chisel between the flags"; Scene 2: "Set deep in the lime ... rested his heavy stone chisel ... She wrenched it free of the joint"
- **Problem:** She is already using a forged-carbon chisel at the hearth in Scene 1, then pulls the same kind of tool out of the chimney in Scene 2 as a discovery. After that, the "octagonal" all-steel chisel gains a "squared steel shank" (Scene 6) and a "hickory stock/handle/haft" (Scenes 7, 9, 12). The hearth mortar is her grandfather's in Scene 1, yet her father "set that stone ... before she was born" in Scene 13.
- **Reader needed:** One chisel, introduced once, with one physical description.

### 7. [minor] The heist timing and sequence stutter
- **Where:** Scene 5, Scene 6, Scene 7, Scene 8
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "But tomorrow, half an hour before sixteen hundred, I can pull the drain plugs"; Scene 6: "the dam hung above the tree line ... ticking down toward sixteen hundred"; Scene 7: "the black minute hand stood three ticks before sixteen hundred ... the drain plugs pulled"
- **Problem:** Pike sets the diversion for tomorrow, but the story runs it that same afternoon, at three minutes to rather than half an hour before. Scene 7 ends with Teresa walking "into the dark" toward the stairwell, and Scene 8 opens with her back at the locker closing its door. She also calls herself "Second-shift hydraulics" after being "logged until sixteen hundred" on a dawn start.
- **Reader needed:** One day and time for the diversion, and a Scene 8 that picks up where Scene 7 left her.

### 8. [minor] The karst and reservoir figures change in every retelling
- **Where:** Scene 9, Scene 10, Scene 12
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "a rotten throat of soft gypsum, miles across" / "three million tons of scree" / "core drills from twenty years back"; Scene 10: "an unmapped cavern sixty rods long" / "Ten million tons of limestone" / "the collapse logs from three springs ago" / "rotten chalk"
- **Problem:** Within a single scene of hearing it, Teresa gives Kroll's evidence a different size, rock type, tonnage and date. The reservoir also shrinks from "sixty billion gallons" (Scene 9) to "twenty million tons" (Scene 12) and "fifty feet of standing water" (Scene 13).
- **Reader needed:** The same survey facts each time they are cited.

## What works
- The 740-foot datum is carried exactly from the carving ("four numerals were bitten into the shoulder of the gorge: 740") through the painted benchmark in Scene 12 to the hearthstone in Scene 13.
- The brass key's path is tracked cleanly: stolen and strapped under her coat in Scene 7, hidden from Kroll in Scene 8, dropped and retaken in Scene 11, "bent three inches off true" in Scene 12.
- Nomi's seepage-well rule in Scene 6 ("The whole settlement drinks from the seepage") pays off consistently in Scene 13 ("The wells in town are sucking air").

## Out of lane
Teresa faces no consequence in Scene 12 for sabotage and a man's fall, which is a plausibility question for another lens.
