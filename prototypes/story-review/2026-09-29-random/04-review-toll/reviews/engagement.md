# Engagement and Believability — Toll

**Score: 2/5**. The flooding at the start and the hollow ending are strong, but the middle runs on convenient allies and obstacles that collapse, and the climax contradicts the story's own central revelation, so the major turns don't feel earned.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The climax ignores the karst threat the story just set up
- **Where:** Scene 11 (against Scene 9)
- **Quote:** "The only thing that could hold the roof of that karst cavern was the brute hydrostatic weight of sixty billion gallons of water sitting directly over the fault." (Scene 9) / "\"The town will endure silt,\" Teresa said... \"They will not drown in a tidal wave.\"" (Scene 11)
- **Problem:** Scene 9 establishes that removing the water risks a collapse that would kill "every settlement down to the delta," and Teresa accepts it ("Show me the bore logs"). Then she drains the reservoir anyway, reasoning as if only the dynamite could cause a flood. The karst never collapses and is never mentioned again after Kroll's single line in Scene 12. The revelation that turned the story ends up changing nothing.
- **Reader needed:** Either Teresa learning why the drain won't trigger the collapse, or the collapse threat being present and real as the gate opens.

### 2. [major] Teresa's decision to open the gate is left unexplained
- **Where:** Scene 11 (after Scene 9 and Scene 10)
- **Quote:** "She could not blow the dam to drown the valley, but she could not leave her family's past locked beneath the lake either."
- **Problem:** She has taken her hand from her pocket "empty" on the parapet, heard Nomi out twice, and seen Pike's madness. Then she turns the key in a single sentence. The reader has no sense of what finally tips her, so the climax feels like the plot needing an outcome rather than her choosing one.
- **Reader needed:** A visible internal pressure in the moment (grief, the sight of the key, something Kroll says) that makes the reversal feel inevitable rather than imposed.

### 3. [major] Julian's sabotage plot arrives out of nowhere
- **Where:** Scene 11 (against Scene 5 and Scene 7)
- **Quote:** "Bundles of commercial dynamite lay open on an oilcloth across the concrete floor" / "\"They took my river,\" Julian snarled"
- **Problem:** Until now Pike was a cynical helper grumbling about maintenance budgets. His own grievance, his plan to blow up the dam, and his willingness to kill are revealed and resolved within a page. His fall into the pit is never mentioned again, as though no one died.
- **Reader needed:** Some earlier hint of Pike's personal loss or extremism, and at least a glance back at what happened to him.

### 4. [major] Obstacles dissolve through convenient allies and collapses
- **Where:** Scene 5, Scene 8 (and Scene 9)
- **Quote:** "\"You want the bypass line for the bottom scour gate,\" Pike said softly. It was not a question." / "too spent to ask for her work slip or look past the brim of her cap."
- **Problem:** A stranger works out her secret at a glance, tells her where the key is, and volunteers a diversion. When she's caught at the forced locker, her enemy has a seizure and waves her on. Scene 9 then reveals Kroll "knew the name the morning you handed your papers to Callow", which makes his letting her go inexplicable. The heist never tests her.
- **Reader needed:** At least one obstacle Teresa overcomes by her own nerve or skill, and a reason why Kroll, knowing who she is, doesn't stop her.

### 5. [minor] Scene 3 builds a confrontation and then drops it
- **Where:** Scene 3 to Scene 4
- **Quote:** "\"The culvert's choked, Kroll,\" she said, her voice dropping into the ditch like a dropped stone."
- **Problem:** Ten years pass, she tracks him down and refuses to pity him, speaks one line, and the scene cuts. Scene 4 opens with her already hired. The premise ("takes a job on the dam") happens offstage, and the first reunion goes nowhere.
- **Reader needed:** The confrontation playing out, or cutting it and showing how and why she got onto the dam crew.

### 6. [minor] Revelations and warnings are recapped rather than advanced
- **Where:** Scene 10 and Scene 11 (repeating Scene 9 and Scene 6)
- **Quote:** "That revelation had stripped the ground from beneath her hatred, reducing her seventy-two stolen hours to the brutal calculus of a subsidence survey."
- **Problem:** Scene 10 retells the karst reveal to Nomi, and Nomi repeats her Scene 6 warning about the wells. Scene 11 then opens by summarising Scene 9 a third time. The long middle stalls just as the pressure should be building.
- **Reader needed:** Nomi's second confrontation raising the stakes or changing something, not repeating her first.

### 7. [minor] The reservoir empties to the exact datum overnight
- **Where:** Scene 12
- **Quote:** "All through the cold night, the reservoir had fallen. Hour by hour the dark line had plunged past the eight-hundred-foot contour marks"
- **Problem:** A basin said to hold "sixty billion gallons" drains through one scour gate in a single night, down to the hearth. It's convenient to the vow and stretches belief, especially with no alarm, crew response or authorities arriving.
- **Reader needed:** Some sense of the scale of time and of the institutional scramble, so the draining reads as a disaster happening in the world rather than a stage cue.

## What works
- The early eviction makes the stakes clear, and Kroll's guilt is planted and later explained: "There was a tremor in the line of his mouth—the thin, miserable guilt of a junior director who understood the ruin he authored."
- The wells consequence is set up in Scene 6 and pays off in a bleak, earned reckoning: "Every bore in the lower terrace ran dry within an hour of your gate opening."
- The ending refuses triumph and lands: "She stood on the stone. The vow was kept. / It felt like nothing at all." Nomi's closing line, "You gave us back our hearth," is bitterly specific to this story.

## Out of lane
The time since the flood is inconsistent ("Ten years" in Scene 3, "three summers" and "three years" in Scene 12, "three weeks early" in Scene 5), and Kroll is both a ditch-minder (Scene 3) and in the "glass tower" (Scene 5).
