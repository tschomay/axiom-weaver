# Prose Craft — Toll

**Score: 2/5** — There are strong set pieces, but across 13 scenes the story repeats itself: the chisel is "against her ribs" in nearly every scene, one sentence is copied almost verbatim, scenes restate facts with different numbers, and the typos and stock phrases pull a reader out regularly.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The chisel and key sit "against her ribs" in scene after scene
- **Where:** Scene 4, Scene 5, Scene 6, Scene 7, Scene 8, Scene 9, Scene 10, Scene 12
- **Quote:** "the cold steel heavy against her ribs" (Scene 4) / "her father's chisel lay hard against her ribs" (Scene 5) / "The metal bit hard against her ribs." (Scene 9) / "the scarred hickory haft pressed against her ribs" (Scene 12)
- **Problem:** About ten uses in eight scenes. Scene 9 uses it twice ("just as the steel rested against her ribs"), and it also covers the brass key ("The dead brass hung firm against her ribs", Scene 7; "an ingot of cold ballast against her ribs", Scene 8; "concealed against her ribs", Scene 10). Each scene also re-describes the chisel from scratch ("octagonal carbon-steel chisel", Scenes 9 and 12), and the solid forged tool gains a "hickory stock/handle/haft" from Scene 7 on. Scene 1 already has Teresa working with "the forged-carbon steel chisel", yet Scene 2 presents her father's chisel as newly found in the mortar. The talisman turns into a verbal tic.
- **Reader needed:** The chisel established once and then simply used, with its physical presence varied or left unsaid.

### 2. [major] A sentence copied almost verbatim between scenes
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 11
- **Quote:** "The smell of damp stone and rot was immediate, an ancient riverbed damp rising out of the very masonry" (Scene 2) / "the smell of damp stone and rot was thick as silt, the ancient riverbed damp rising out of the very masonry" (Scene 11)
- **Problem:** The farmhouse kitchen and the turbine gallery get the same sentence. An attentive reader notices this, and it breaks the sense of one author.
- **Reader needed:** Each place given its own sensory signature.

### 3. [major] Scenes restate facts with different numbers and descriptions
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 2, 3, 7, 9, 12
- **Quote:** "Ten years of impounded water lay over the valley floor" (Scene 3) / "weighed twenty years of bitter smoke" (Scene 9) / "lime mortar that had resisted three years of dark submersion" (Scene 12)
- **Problem:** Time since the flood is ten, twenty or three years depending on the scene, and Scene 12 also says "three summers". The early closure is "Forty-eight hours early" (Scene 1), "three weeks early" (Scene 5) and "twenty hours after the ink dried" (Scene 9). The chisel was driven into the mortar "twenty years before" (Scene 2) but carries "forty years of limestone dressing" (Scene 7). Kroll is a "junior director" (Scene 1), "sitting up in the glass tower" (Scene 5), and wearing "patched canvas hip waders" with "broad shoulders" (Scenes 8, 11, 12). Each scene seems to have rebuilt the story's facts from its own brief.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent set of figures and one consistent Kroll, carried across scenes.

### 4. [major] Recaps and re-introductions at the scene seams
- **Where:** Scene 11 (also Scenes 8, 10, 13)
- **Quote:** "The memory of Kroll upon the rain-swept parapet clung to Teresa like wet wool—his cracked voice cutting through the rising squall to lay bare the truth he had hidden for years: the deep gypsum cavern yawning in the northern canyon wall" (Scene 11)
- **Problem:** Scene 11 opens by summarising Scene 9, which Scene 10 had already retold in dialogue. Characters are reintroduced with appositives as if the reader were new: "Arthur Kroll asked, the engineer who had driven the stakes through the Vance orchard in his patched canvas waders" (Scene 8); "Julian Pike, the diver technician who had unbolted the inspection hatch" (Scene 11). The aquifer is explained three times (Scenes 6, 10, 13), and many scenes open with a travel-and-reset sentence ("By dawn, Teresa had climbed… to stand once more upon the parapet", Scene 4; "Leaving the windy crest behind, Teresa picked her way down", Scene 10).
- **Reader needed:** Trust in the reader's memory, with scenes that start inside the action.

### 5. [minor] Recycled stock phrases
- **Where:** Throughout
- **Quote:** "the cold weight of the valley fog" (Scene 1) / "The cold weight of the impounded basin pressed against her mind" (Scene 9) / "The cold weight of the drained gulf rose to meet her" (Scene 12) / "The cold weight of the empty valley pressed down upon her shoulders" (Scene 13)
- **Problem:** "Cold weight" appears seven times (also Scenes 3 and 4). There are also "voice dropping" (Scenes 1, 3, 6), "level and cold" (Scenes 2, 9), "did not flinch" (Scenes 9, 10), "voice flat" / "falling flat" (Scenes 4, 5, 9, 12), "like an iron ingot" / "like an ingot" (Scenes 1, 8) and "guttering" (Scenes 3, 10, 12, the last one applied to sunlight). Kroll's wheeze is described fresh at every appearance.
- **Reader needed:** Fewer default phrases, so the ones that remain carry weight.

### 6. [minor] Telling, "not X but Y", and rhetorical self-questions
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 3, Scene 9, Scene 11, Scene 12
- **Quote:** "To forgive a ruined man was merely an easier way to abandon the dead. His misery was not restitution; it was only an evasion." (Scene 3) / "Why not leave the old man to his mud?" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** The narrator hands down Teresa's conclusions. The "not X but Y" frame recurs: "no longer a home, but a filling basin" (Scene 2); "not an act of bureaucratic malice, but a desperate, crushing ballast" (Scene 9). Scene 11 explains her state ("The dynamic of vengeance was gone, murdered by Julian's madness and Nomi's bitter warning"). Scene endings lean on summary one-liners: "The water had taken forty years in forty minutes." (Scene 2); "The vow had reached its shore." (Scene 12); "The vow was kept." (Scene 13).
- **Reader needed:** Teresa's shifts shown through her actions, as in "Slowly, she pulled her hand from her pocket, empty" (Scene 9), which works.

### 7. [minor] Expository dialogue and melodramatic tags
- **Where:** Scene 4, Scene 9, Scene 11
- **Quote:** "District safety rules are strict: it's a two-stage hydraulic interlocking safety protocol down there. That emergency gate will not budge an inch unless the secondary pressure release cylinder is engaged first" (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Callow briefs the reader in manual language, and Kroll delivers a geology lecture in a gale (Scene 9). Julian's "They took everything we had and called it municipal equity. I am putting an end to Crestview." (Scene 11) comes with "Julian snarled" and "With a feral cry", and the confrontation slides into villain register.
- **Reader needed:** Technical stakes carried by what Teresa sees and does, and dialogue with some subtext.

### 8. [nit] Typos, doubled words and garbled syntax
- **Where:** Scenes 3, 7, 9, 11, 12
- **Quote:** "like an trapped wave" (Scene 9); "One charge against the collar collar-seat" (Scene 11); "split into two braided braids" (Scene 12); "the braided seal snapped with a brittle snap" (Scene 7)
- **Problem:** There are also "her voice dropping into the ditch like a dropped stone" (Scene 3), "nothing but mist and grey drop down" (Scene 9), the fragment "Its guttering light shivering across the ripples cast long, trembling yellow shadows" (Scene 12), and "dark water… through the dark" (Scene 11). Together they signal an unedited draft.
- **Reader needed:** A copy-edit pass.

## What works
- A crisp, confident opening beat: "Then the gravel took the bite of boots that were not farm leather." (Scene 1)
- The flood as a paced sequence of concrete losses: "The porch roof held for a few minutes, a raft pinned against the current, before the joists buckled with a subterranean snap and slipped under. Then the shingles vanished." (Scene 2)
- A bitter, understated close in dialogue: "We have our hearth back, Teresa. You gave us back our hearth." (Scene 13)

## Out of lane
The hearth stands at 740 feet, yet the settlement at 620 feet is dry beside a lake over the farm. That elevation logic belongs to another lens.
