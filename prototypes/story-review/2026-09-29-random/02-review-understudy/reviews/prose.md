# Prose Craft — Understudy

**Score: 3/5** — The period voice and present tense hold throughout and there are fine moments, but the scenes contradict each other's numbers, reveal the same ring mark twice, recycle Roland's speech and the same gestures, and repeatedly spell out Hester's choices and conclusions.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Hester's tenure changes from scene to scene
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 2, Scene 3, Scene 4, Scene 5
- **Quote:** "For eleven unbroken years she has spoken Duke Malcor's closing soliloquy" (Scene 1) / "for the first time in thirty years, the stage beneath her feet feels entirely unmoored" (Scene 2) / "For thirty years, she played Malcor as a monument" (Scene 3) / "Eleven years. Eleven years of barricading dressing rooms" (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Eleven years becomes thirty and then eleven again. Scene 3 even gives Maisie "thirty years of theatrical precedence", although she has six years of training ("For six years, Hester", Scene 2), which Scene 5 then calls "half a decade". Scene 5 adds "four thousand nights" and "since she was twenty-four". Each scene has its own arithmetic, and a reader stops to reconcile them.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent figure for Hester's years in the role and for Maisie's apprenticeship.

### 2. [major] The ring and its groove are "revealed" twice
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 5 (also Scene 2)
- **Quote:** "her right ring finger bears the mark she knows as intimately as her own name: a permanent, bloodless depression pressed deep into the skin and cartilage" (Scene 1) / "Beneath it lies the flesh she hasn't seen bare since she was twenty-four: a deep, bloodless furrow" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Scene 5 treats taking off the ring as a first unveiling, with the same image ("bloodless", "cartilage", the resisting joint), but Scene 1 already staged it nightly. In between, the ring sits in her coat pocket (Scene 2, "the cold iron signet ring biting sharp into the flesh of her thumb"), and Scene 4 has "Maisie waits with the heavy iron signet". The story's central image loses its force because it is used twice, and its whereabouts wander.
- **Reader needed:** The groove kept back for the ending, or Scene 5 written knowing the reader has already seen it.

### 3. [minor] Roland's rebuke is delivered again in each scene
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 3, Scene 4 (and Scene 5)
- **Quote:** "Maisie signed the replacement contract this morning at half-salary. Half. She did it so the company could pay the carters and the stagehands who have gone without wages for three weeks" (Scene 2) / "She signed for twenty-two shillings, Hester… Half your wage… forty souls who haven't seen silver in three weeks" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** Scene 3 opens by rewinding Roland to the aisle so he can make the Scene 2 speech again. Scene 4 repeats "forty souls" and "three weeks", and Scene 4 and Scene 5 both repeat "forty people". This is the clearest seam between separately written scenes.
- **Reader needed:** The point made once, with later scenes building on it.

### 4. [minor] Card-like exposition in dialogue and narration
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "The regional tour operates under a crippling financial deficit caused by dwindling provincial ticket sales." (Scene 1) / "Like every provincial touring house, the Dunmere theatre has neglected its machinery; the stage trapdoor counterweight in provincial touring theatres requires an actor's full bodily momentum" (Scene 1)
- **Problem:** Roland's line reads like a summary, not speech, and the trapdoor sentence repeats "provincial touring" within one clause, like a pasted fact. Scene 3 then explains the same mechanism again at length ("It is designed to be tripped by an actor's decisive momentum").
- **Reader needed:** The mechanism set up once, woven into what Hester is doing, and a manager who talks like a person.

### 5. [minor] Recycled gestures and sounds
- **Where:** Scenes 1–4
- **Quote:** "Hester's breath catches, cold and bitter in her chest" (Scene 1); "Hester's breath catches." (Scene 3); "her breath catching in her throat" (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Hester's breath catches in three scenes. "Gravel" appears three times ("cutting gravel", Scene 1; "scraping like gravel in her throat" and "ground my register into gravel", Scene 2), and Scene 3 adds "scraping like dry shale". Maisie "flinches" in Scenes 1, 2 and 3, and Roland "does not flinch" in Scene 4. The "flat" voice appears for Roland in Scene 1 ("says flatly") and Scene 3, and for Hester in Scene 3.
- **Reader needed:** Fresh physical detail each time, or fewer beats of this kind.

### 6. [minor] Choices and revelations are announced, not dramatised
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 3, Scene 4, Scene 5
- **Quote:** "A choice hangs between them, razor-thin… Hester hardens her jaw. She chooses the knife." (Scene 2) / "She closes her eyes, weighs the terror of destitution against the death of her name, and chooses." (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** The same "and she chooses" device is used twice. Elsewhere the narrator explains the meaning: "It strikes Hester like cold water. The tragedy of Malcor is not that an empire falls; it is that a gentle soul is ground beneath the wheels of it." (Scene 3). Scene 4 also has a paragraph of self-summary ("She had severed friendships, poisoned company after company, convinced herself that cruelty was merely vigilance"), and Scene 1 tells us Maisie is "utterly paralyzed by guilt and anxiety".
- **Reader needed:** Hester's turns shown through her action and speech, which the story already has (the lever, the ledger).

### 7. [minor] Every scene ends on a portentous line
- **Where:** Scenes 1–4
- **Quote:** "take back what is hers." (Scene 1); "the stage beneath her feet feels entirely unmoored." (Scene 2); "And Roland Ghyll will answer for every penny of this." (Scene 3); "understands that the play has gone on without her." (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Each scene ends on a declarative summary beat. Together with the isolated "No." and "It was not a usurpation. It was a salvage." (Scene 3), they make the rhythm feel formulaic.
- **Reader needed:** Some scenes that end on an image or action and let the reader draw the conclusion.

### 8. [nit] Typos and overloaded similes
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 3, Scene 5
- **Quote:** "he corridor seems to contract." (Scene 1); "settles into straight, unyielding line" (Scene 5); "like an October gale off the high fells, bitter and damp with the promise of rain" (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** There is a dropped letter and a dropped article. The gale simile is a triple-stacked comparison for a draught in a room.
- **Reader needed:** A copy-edit pass and one comparison where three are used.

## What works
- Maisie's notebook is characterisation in specifics: "*Dunmere, October: voice pitched like a kitchen wench, dropped the fourth vowel; … Crossbeck, dress: lower the larynx, bite the consonants until the gum bleeds.*" (Scene 2)
- Plain, earned dialogue at the turn: "It was wrong. It was my voice, not Malcor's." (Scene 5)
- A fresh, physical close to the ring moment: "there is only the startling sensation of air moving across skin that has been covered for half a lifetime." (Scene 5)

## Out of lane
The trap rig is "rusted iron and lead, suspended on stiff friction dogs", yet it then "drops smoothly on its greased rails" (Scene 3). That logic question belongs to another lens.
