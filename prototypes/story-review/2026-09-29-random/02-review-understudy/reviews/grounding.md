# Grounding and Detail: Understudy

**Score: 3/5**: The rooms and atmosphere are richly drawn, but a reader has to work out for themselves why Hester is at Crossbeck after being dismissed, and the climactic rescue at the trapdoor is hard to follow in space and in mechanics.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Hester is at Crossbeck after being dismissed, with no explanation
- **Where:** Scene 2 (set against Scene 1)
- **Quote:** "Crossbeck will not see you. This is your formal notice of termination." ... "You are done after tonight." (Scene 1); "Crossbeck Civic Theatre smells of river damp ... From the darkness of the pit, Hester watches the platform." (Scene 2)
- **Problem:** Scene 1 ends with Hester's dismissal, effective that night, and Scene 2 opens with her inside the theatre she was barred from. The story never says how much time has passed, how she got there, or in what role (still on the payroll? a hanger-on? plotting?). The whole plan to sabotage Maisie depends on her being there.
- **Reader needed:** A sentence on why Hester is still travelling with the company, and when this is relative to Dunmere.

### 2. [major] The trapdoor rescue can't be pictured
- **Where:** Scene 3 (and the start of Scene 4)
- **Quote:** "Hester watches from the dark of the tormentor." ... "Across the wings, on the elevated backstage platform, the manual release lever stands upright" ... "Hester throws herself across the timber platform."
- **Problem:** Hester is at stage level in the wing. The lever appears only at the moment it's needed, on a raised platform "across the wings", and Hester is on that platform one line later. Scene 4 then has her coming down "from the flies". Stagehands also appear under the stage ("caught safely in the arms of the stagehands waiting below") without ever being placed there. It's also unclear why a manual lever would stop a light step from jamming the trap, so the rescue reads as asserted.
- **Reader needed:** The lever, and Hester's route to it, set up before the cue, plus one line on why pulling the lever makes the drop safe.

### 3. [minor] Where Hester is in Scene 2 keeps shifting
- **Where:** Scene 2
- **Quote:** "From the darkness of the pit, Hester watches the platform." ... "On the edge of the prompter's trestle ... Hester reaches out" ... "Hester steps out from behind the wing curtain into the gaslight."
- **Problem:** Within a few paragraphs Hester is in the pit, then at the prompter's trestle, then behind the wing curtain, with no movement between them.
- **Reader needed:** One fixed position for Hester, or a line where she moves.

### 4. [minor] The timing of the performance is unclear
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 4
- **Quote:** "ensure the girl cannot take the stage tomorrow night" (Scene 2); "setting the ducal tomb for the evening's performance" ... "Give me closing night." (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Scene 2 places Maisie's debut tomorrow. Scene 4 seems to happen the same afternoon, before tonight's performance, and calls it "closing night". A reader can't tell whether a day has passed, or whether tonight is the opening or the last night of the tour.
- **Reader needed:** A time cue at the start of Scene 4, such as "the next afternoon".

### 5. [minor] Roland's reasons arrive piecemeal, and one is repeated
- **Where:** Scene 3 (repeating Scene 2) and Scene 4
- **Quote:** "Maisie signed the replacement contract this morning at half-salary. Half." (Scene 2); "She signed for twenty-two shillings, Hester ... Half your wage." (Scene 3); "The subsidy is contingent on Maisie Callow taking the lead." (Scene 4)
- **Problem:** Scene 3 repeats the half-salary and forty-souls explanation from a few paragraphs earlier. Meanwhile the council subsidy, which is the real reason Hester can't buy her way back, first appears at the moment it's used.
- **Reader needed:** The repeat cut, and the subsidy hinted at earlier so that Scene 4 confirms something rather than introducing it.

### 6. [minor] The trapdoor setup reads like a textbook entry
- **Where:** Scene 1
- **Quote:** "the stage trapdoor counterweight in provincial touring theatres requires an actor's full bodily momentum to release without jamming in its guides."
- **Problem:** A general statement about all provincial theatres interrupts Hester's scene. Scene 3 then explains the same mechanism again at length, just before the climax.
- **Reader needed:** One setup, carried by what Hester notices, instead of two lectures.

### 7. [nit] Maisie's role is withheld at her first appearance
- **Where:** Scene 1
- **Quote:** "Across the narrow table, Maisie Callow's hand creeps forward."
- **Problem:** Maisie arrives with a full name and no role. The reader learns she is the understudy only at the end of the scene.
- **Reader needed:** "the understudy" or "her apprentice" when Maisie first appears.

## What works
- Each venue gets its own concrete smell and texture: "Crossbeck Civic Theatre smells of river damp and wet horsehair plaster, a cavernous, drafty barn of a house" (Scene 2)
- The ring is set up physically in Scene 1 and pays off in Scene 5: "a permanent, bloodless depression pressed deep into the skin and cartilage from eleven unbroken years of wearing the ring." (Scene 1)
- Hester's money is made concrete before she stakes it: "two hundred and forty pounds—eleven years of scrimping in cold lodgings, of sour tea and third-class carriages" (Scene 4)

## Out of lane
For the consistency reviewer: "eleven years" and "thirty years" both describe Hester's tenure; the trap machinery is "rusted" and then runs on "greased rails"; and Scene 4 says Maisie waits "with the heavy iron signet" while Hester is still wearing it.
