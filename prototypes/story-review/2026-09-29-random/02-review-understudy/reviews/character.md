# Character Coherence — Understudy

**Score: 3/5** — Hester's arc swings back and forth without cause. She lurches from "chooses the knife" to coaching Maisie, and from sparing Maisie to trying to buy the role off her, and the final change is narrated rather than dramatised. Roland is steady and the three voices are distinct.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Hester tries to buy the role back after her epiphany
- **Where:** Scene 4 (against Scene 3)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "The audience will not merely admire him; they will weep for him." … "She will not let the girl be broken." Scene 4: "Yet if she does not spend it now, she walks out an exile while another woman speaks Malcor's final verse… Tear up the notice, Roland. Give me closing night."
- **Problem:** Scene 3 has Hester see that Maisie's Malcor is better than hers and risk herself to protect the girl. Scene 4 then has her spend her life savings to take the part away from Maisie again, and never once thinks about Maisie while doing it. A reader can't tell whether the Scene 3 turn was real, and the Scene 4 bid doesn't mention it.
- **Reader needed:** Hester needs to weigh the Scene 3 revelation against her bid, for example by telling herself closing night is owed to her, or by asking to share it. Otherwise the bid reads as a reset.

### 2. [major] Hester goes from the knife to coaching with no beat between
- **Where:** Scene 3 (against Scene 2)
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "Hester hardens her jaw. She chooses the knife." Scene 3: "She could leave the girl to flounder… 'From the entrance of the emissaries,' Hester says, her voice low, flat. 'Take it from "Now breaks the iron girdle."'"
- **Problem:** Minutes after deliberately trying to break Maisie's nerve, and still planning sabotage, Hester starts directing her. The only link is that the stage "feels entirely unmoored", which describes a mood, not a reason. The coaching sets up the epiphany, but nothing in Hester's head gets us to it.
- **Reader needed:** One visible reason Hester picks up the rehearsal, whether it's a professional reflex she can't suppress, a plan to watch for weaknesses, or Roland's words sinking in.

### 3. [minor] The final transformation is summarised, not shown
- **Where:** Scene 4 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "She had severed friendships, poisoned company after company, convinced herself that cruelty was merely vigilance and that art demanded solitude… The fight drains out of her, leaving only a hollow, shuddering stillness."
- **Problem:** A backstory of poisoned companies appears for the first time as narration in the same paragraph that announces her change. Scene 3 does the same with "a fury that is no longer turned outward at the world, but inward against her own cruelty". The narrator names the self-reckoning instead of Hester living through it.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier concrete sign of the damage Hester has done to others, so this reckoning pays off something the reader has already seen.

### 4. [minor] Hester's notes contradict her own discovery
- **Where:** Scene 5 (against Scenes 2 and 3)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "Her voice… rises in its natural register—pale, frayed at the rim, carrying a raw, unbearable ache." Scene 5: "I had always insisted you pinch the vowels, clip the cadence short… You have the lower chest resonance to let the lines roll out."
- **Problem:** In Scene 2 Maisie said Hester forced her to "grind my register into gravel", and Scene 3 shows her real gift is a light, frayed voice. Hester's parting correction then describes both the old drilling and Maisie's strength the other way round, so it undoes the insight the gift is supposed to honour.
- **Reader needed:** Hester's note should release exactly what Scene 2 said she suppressed.

### 5. [minor] Hester's last thought after the rescue points at Roland
- **Where:** Scene 3
- **Quote:** "She will not let the girl be broken. And Roland Ghyll will answer for every penny of this."
- **Problem:** After an act of self-sacrifice, Hester's parting thought is resentment at Roland. What he is meant to answer for is unclear, and the next scene she approaches him as a supplicant, not an accuser.
- **Reader needed:** What Hester now blames Roland for, or that thread dropped.

### 6. [nit] Maisie's feelings are named rather than shown
- **Where:** Scene 1 (and Scene 3)
- **Quote:** "The understudy does not speak… utterly paralyzed by guilt and anxiety beneath the glare."
- **Problem:** Outside her Scene 2 outburst, Maisie is mostly flinching and having her emotions labelled for her. Scene 2 shows she has a real inner life, but the rest of the story mostly leaves it unused.
- **Reader needed:** Nothing more than trusting the gestures that are already there, without the label.

## What works
- Maisie's outburst in Scene 2 gives her a history and a grievance of her own: "every time I sang the cadence the way it came to me—you struck the prompter's table with your ring."
- Roland is consistent and believable: tired, money-minded and fair. His refusal of the draft has a concrete reason: "The subsidy is contingent on Maisie Callow taking the lead. Your money cannot buy out a town council contract."
- The ring-groove image gives Hester's release a physical form and doesn't need explaining: "there is only the startling sensation of air moving across skin that has been covered for half a lifetime."

## Out of lane
Hester's career is "eleven unbroken years" in Scenes 1 and 4 but "thirty years" in Scenes 2 and 3 (consistency).
