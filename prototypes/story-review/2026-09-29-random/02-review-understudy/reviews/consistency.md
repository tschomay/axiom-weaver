# Logical Consistency — Understudy

**Score: 2/5** — Hester's career length and the signet ring, the story's two load-bearing facts, both contradict themselves across scenes, and several smaller slips pile up around them, so a reader starts to distrust the details.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Eleven years as Malcor becomes thirty years
- **Where:** Scene 1, against Scene 2 and Scene 3
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "For eleven unbroken years she has spoken Duke Malcor's closing soliloquy"; "from eleven unbroken years of wearing the ring"
- **Quote (later):** Scene 2: "for the first time in thirty years, the stage beneath her feet feels entirely unmoored"; Scene 3: "For thirty years, she played Malcor as a monument"
- **Problem:** The premise and Scene 1 hammer "eleven years" twice. Scene 3 then says she played the role for thirty. Scene 4 ("Eleven years. Eleven years of barricading dressing rooms") and Scene 5 ("eleven years of thumbing") return to eleven. Scene 3 also has Maisie expecting blows from "thirty years of theatrical precedence", though Maisie has known Hester for six.
- **Reader needed:** One span for Hester's tenure in the role, kept apart from the length of her whole career if the two differ.

### 2. [major] The ring's whereabouts and "never removed" status contradict each other
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 2, Scene 4, Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "prises Duke Malcor's heavy iron signet ring from her hand ... When it finally comes away"; Scene 2: "Hester's fingers tighten inside her coat pocket, the cold iron signet ring biting sharp"
- **Quote (later):** Scene 4: "the dressing room where Maisie waits with the heavy iron signet"; Scene 5: "Beneath it lies the flesh she hasn't seen bare since she was twenty-four ... skin that has been covered for half a lifetime"
- **Problem:** The ring comes off after the Scene 1 performance and is in her pocket in Scene 2. Scene 4 says Maisie has it. In Scene 5 it is back on Hester's finger, and taking it off is treated as the first time in decades. The climax depends on the finger never having been bare, and the reader has already watched it bared.
- **Reader needed:** A single track for the ring: on her finger the whole time, or taken off nightly with Scene 5 framed differently. It should not pass through Maisie's hands.

### 3. [minor] "Tomorrow night" becomes "tonight"
- **Where:** Scene 2, against Scenes 3–5
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "ensure the girl cannot take the stage tomorrow night"; Scene 3: "beg her to take Malcor into the fire tonight"; Scene 4: "setting the ducal tomb for the evening's performance"
- **Problem:** Scenes 2 and 3 are one continuous rehearsal, yet the opening shifts from tomorrow to tonight. It then plays that same evening, with "beginners for act one".
- **Reader needed:** One performance date.

### 4. [minor] The Crossbeck trap is rusted and stiff, then glides on greased rails
- **Where:** Scene 3 (echoing Scene 1)
- **Quote:** "the counterweight sled in the undercroft is rusted iron and lead, suspended on stiff friction dogs" ... "the counterweight sled drops smoothly on its greased rails"
- **Problem:** A few lines apart, the same mechanism is neglected and rusted, then freshly greased. In Scene 1 greasing the runners was the job left undone.
- **Reader needed:** One state for the machinery.

### 5. [minor] Hester's Scene 5 note reverses the drilling Maisie described
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 3, against Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "You made me crush it. I ground my register into gravel until I could roar the way you roared"; Scene 3: her natural register is "pale, frayed at the rim"
- **Quote (later):** Scene 5: "I had always insisted you pinch the vowels, clip the cadence short ... You have the lower chest resonance to let the lines roll out."
- **Problem:** Earlier, Hester forced Maisie into a low gravel roar and Maisie's own voice was light. Scene 5 says Hester had made her pinch and clip, and that her true gift is low chest resonance. The two are opposites.
- **Reader needed:** Hester's correction should undo the specific fault Scene 2 described.

### 6. [minor] Hester's position in the rehearsal hall jumps
- **Where:** Scene 2
- **Quote:** "From the darkness of the pit, Hester watches the platform" ... "On the edge of the prompter's trestle ... Hester reaches out" ... "Hester steps out from behind the wing curtain into the gaslight."
- **Problem:** In one short passage she is in the pit, then at the prompter's trestle, then behind the wing curtain, with no movement between.
- **Reader needed:** One vantage point, or a line moving her between them.

### 7. [nit] Costume, seating and office drift
- **Where:** Scenes 2–5
- **Quote:** Scene 2: Maisie "drowning in the heavy velvet mantle of Duke Malcor"; Scene 3: "shivering in her thin rehearsal petticoats"; Scene 3: "the empty stalls where Roland sat"; Scene 5: "the silence in Roland's office"
- **Problem:** Maisie's mantle vanishes between two continuous scenes. Roland never sat in the stalls; he strode down the aisle and left. The Scene 4 exchange happened at a trestle in the wings, not in an office.
- **Reader needed:** Details that match what was shown.

## What works
- The money holds together: "She signed for twenty-two shillings, Hester ... Half your wage" agrees with the half-salary deal, and "forty souls" and "three weeks" of back wages repeat exactly in Scenes 2, 3 and 4.
- Scene 1's trapdoor rule ("A tentative step leaves the mechanism stranded halfway") sets up the danger in Scene 3 fairly.
- The prompt ledger stays in Hester's hands from Scene 1 ("This book stays with me") until she deliberately gives it away in Scene 5.
