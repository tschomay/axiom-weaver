# Setup and Payoff — Understudy

**Score: 4/5** — The three big setups (the trapdoor mechanism, the guarded prompt ledger, the ring and its groove) all pay off and are fairly planted, but the ring's payoff forgets the ring was already removed, and several smaller turns rest on assertion.

## Findings

### 1. [minor] The groove is revealed twice, and the second time forgets the first
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "When it finally comes away, her right ring finger bears the mark she knows as intimately as her own name: a permanent, bloodless depression". Scene 5: "Beneath it lies the flesh she hasn't seen bare since she was twenty-four: a deep, bloodless furrow"
- **Problem:** The closing image depends on the ring coming off for the first time in decades. But Scene 1 already took it off and described the same groove in almost the same words, so the payoff is a repeat of the setup, not the moment it builds to.
- **Reader needed:** Scene 1 to leave the ring on (or only hint at the mark), so that uncovering the groove in Scene 5 is new.

### 2. [minor] Maisie's lost voice comes back by assertion
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 3
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "now my natural voice is gone, and you stand there and tell me I am ruined." Scene 3: "Her voice, freed from Hester's tyrannical drill, rises in its natural register—pale, frayed at the rim"
- **Problem:** Scene 2 raises the question of whether Maisie's natural voice can come back. Scene 3 answers it within minutes, with nothing having happened except Hester giving a cue line. The narrator declares the voice "freed" and never shows what freed it.
- **Reader needed:** a visible cause for the release, such as Hester holding back her correction, or the "caesura" note being the opposite of her old drill.

### 3. [minor] Hester changes twice
- **Where:** Scene 3 and Scene 4
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "The tragedy of Malcor is not that an empire falls ... The audience will not merely admire him; they will weep for him." Scene 4: "She closes her eyes, weighs the terror of destitution against the death of her name, and chooses."
- **Problem:** Scene 3 gives Hester her realisation and her rescue, and ends with her deciding "She will not let the girl be broken". Scene 4 then has her try to buy the role back, and she has to give up a second time ("The fight drains out of her"). With the turning point shown twice, the first one reads as if the story forgot it had happened.
- **Reader needed:** Scene 4's attempt at the role framed as the last reflex of pride after the Scene 3 realisation, or the Scene 3 realisation kept partial.

### 4. [minor] The council subsidy that decides the outcome is barely planted
- **Where:** Scene 4 (planted only in Scene 3)
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "The subsidy is contingent on Maisie Callow taking the lead. Your money cannot buy out a town council contract." Scene 3: "leave the girl to flounder before the town committee"
- **Problem:** Hester's final defeat depends on a civic grant the reader first hears of at the moment it rules her out. The one earlier mention of a "town committee" is too faint to count as a setup. Up to here Roland's reasons were all about money (Scene 1: "crippling financial deficit"), and that is exactly what Hester's draft solves.
- **Reader needed:** an earlier mention that Crossbeck's backing is tied to Maisie, so Roland's refusal confirms something the reader already knew.

### 5. [nit] Scene 3's closing threat against Roland goes nowhere
- **Where:** Scene 3 and Scene 4
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "And Roland Ghyll will answer for every penny of this."
- **Problem:** The line promises a confrontation, but in the next scene Hester comes to Roland with a peace offering, not an accusation, and the threat is never raised again.
- **Reader needed:** either a Scene 4 that starts from this anger, or no threat at the end of Scene 3.

## What works
- The trapdoor is planted exactly and pays off exactly: Scene 1's "only a deliberate, unyielding drop of dead weight will trip the catch" sets up Scene 3's "Maisie is creeping forward, tentative, her steps light and frightened."
- The prompt ledger, guarded in Scene 1 ("This book stays with me"), is handed over in Scene 5 with new annotations, which closes the arc of ownership.
- Scene 1's intent to sabotage ("Hester will find the lever, the cue, the weight") is fulfilled by reversal in Scene 3, where she finds the actual lever and uses it to save Maisie.

## Out of lane
Hester's tenure is "eleven unbroken years" in Scene 1 but "thirty years" in Scenes 2 and 3 (consistency).
