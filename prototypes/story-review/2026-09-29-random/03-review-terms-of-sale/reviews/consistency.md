# Logical Consistency — Terms of Sale

**Score: 2/5** — The afternoon timeline around the four o'clock sluice cannot be reconciled, and Scenes 7 and 9 each describe earlier events that did not happen as the reader saw them, so the reader stops trusting times and who-was-where.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Too much happens before four o'clock, and it gets dark first
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 5, Scene 6, Scene 7
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "It was already twenty past two." Scene 5: "The gaslamps inside Gallagher and Sons Estate Office hissed against the gathering dark" Scene 6: "For nearly an hour they had wandered through the cavernous upper suites" Scene 7: "The four o'clock harbour siren groaned across the spit"
- **Problem:** Between 2:20 and 4:00 the story fits in the Redoubt tour, Nicholas's retreat to the inn and a drink there, the night-time scene in the office, a return to the Redoubt, an hour in the upper suites, twilight on the balcony, the plumb-line check and the cellar descent. "Gathering dark" and "twilight" both come before the 4 p.m. siren, and Nicholas crosses the spit twice more even though the story has made access to it the main constraint.
- **Reader needed:** A timeline that fits in the window the story set, or a second day between the tour and the cellar.

### 2. [major] Jean is cut off until midnight, yet she is back in town an hour after four
- **Where:** Scene 7 and Scene 8 (set up in Scene 1)
- **Quote:** Scene 7: "Nicholas—we're cut off until midnight." Scene 8: "The four o'clock chime passed an hour ago, Jean," and "By the time the high tide receded enough for the causeway skiff to ferry her back"
- **Problem:** Scene 1 says anyone caught out "won't be setting foot on Arkwright's spit until tomorrow morning's ebb", Scene 7 says midnight, and Scene 8 has Jean back at the office around five, carried by a skiff that has never been mentioned. The three statements about the same tide all disagree.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent rule for when the spit reopens, and a crossing that follows it.

### 3. [major] Scene 9 contradicts the sluice, cellar and warrant scenes
- **Where:** Scene 9 (against Scene 7 and Scene 8)
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "You left the spit before the sluice came down." / "The repossession warrant arrived at quarter past three," / "She gave me until five to clear personal effects"
- **Problem:** Nicholas was standing beside Jean in the cellar when the four o'clock siren sounded (Scene 7), so he knows she did not leave before it. At 3:15 Jean was at the Redoubt, not the office. In Scene 8 Brenda serves the warrant after five and says "You have until noon tomorrow to remove personal effects."
- **Reader needed:** Scene 9's account of when things happened should match what the reader saw in Scenes 7 and 8.

### 4. [major] Scene 7 recalls meetings that never happened
- **Where:** Scene 7 (against Scenes 2–3)
- **Quote:** "emptied of whatever polite fiction he had carried into her office that morning." / "the hesitant gentility he had offered her across tea at the harbour inn."
- **Problem:** Nicholas and Jean first meet at the Redoubt's front door in the afternoon (Scene 3). He never came to her office that morning, and they never had tea together at the inn. The same passage also has Jean's point of view treat the pen as "hollowed out", which she has never been shown.
- **Reader needed:** References back that match the one afternoon meeting the reader actually saw.

### 5. [minor] "Warned him about the southern bay window" reverses Scene 3
- **Where:** Scene 9 (against Scene 3)
- **Quote:** Scene 9: "the woman who had warned him about the southern bay window when silence would have cleared her debt." Scene 3: "'Just the coastal damp,' Jean said with an airy, practiced laugh"
- **Problem:** In Scene 3 Jean explains the stuck sash away and steers him off the cellar. She never warns him about it, and Scene 4 treats that very moment as evidence that she was hiding something.
- **Reader needed:** Nicholas's memory of the sash to match what Jean actually did.

### 6. [minor] "Yesterday" for a same-day scene
- **Where:** Scene 8 (against Scene 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "Toby wanted me to burn it yesterday." Scene 5: "Vance has only just walked through the front door with his boots on."
- **Problem:** Scene 5 happens after the Thursday viewing, the same day as Scene 8, so it was not yesterday. Scene 5 also has Jean imagine the waiver vanishing "between a Tuesday viewing and a Thursday exchange", but the viewing happens on Thursday. (The Scene 1 deadline also slips: "Thirty days from today" becomes "Four weeks from Friday", and in Scene 4 Toby says "by Friday".)
- **Reader needed:** Consistent day labels for the one day the story covers.

### 7. [minor] The waiver's content, date and position keep changing
- **Where:** Scene 5, Scene 8, Scene 9
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "He instructed this firm to sell strictly under caveat emptor, without warranting the footings." Scene 9: "Gallagher & Sons declines to market without mandatory disclosure." / "dated two months prior"
- **Problem:** Scene 8 describes the document as the vendor's caveat-emptor instruction, dated by an admission "three years ago". Scene 9 presents it as the agency refusing to market without disclosure, which the agency was doing anyway. The paper also moves: "into the center of the listing ledger" (Scene 5), "From the ledger's back pocket" (Scene 8), "folded neatly into its flyleaf" (Scene 9).
- **Reader needed:** One fixed description of what the key document says and where it is kept.

### 8. [minor] Recurring drift in small facts
- **Where:** Scenes 2, 3, 6, 8, 10 (and Scene 1)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "his grey eyes" vs Scene 10: "his dark eyes"; Scene 2: the pen holds "a finely weighted miniature plumb bob" vs Scene 6: "he bypassed the deceptive fountain pen and drew out his heavy brass plumb line"
- **Problem:** Several details that are stated firmly later change. The creditor is "Keel Holdings" (Scene 1), then "the Keel Trust" (Scene 8). The £18,000 is Jean's commission in Scene 1 but "an eighteen-thousand-pound sale" in Scene 2. "Vance and Gallagher, if your family pride requires alphabetical precedence" (Scene 10) gets the alphabet backwards. "eighty-four years of family pride" does not fit a 1948 founding, given a setting with gaslamps and oil lamps alongside a phone with a "glowing display" (Scene 6).
- **Reader needed:** Each of these details to stay as first stated.

## What works
- The safe combination stays the same in all three scenes and grows more meaningful each time: "nineteen forty-eight, the agency's founding year" (Scene 5), repeated in Scene 8 and then "The year my grandfather hung the shingle" (Scene 9).
- The two-degree tilt appears in Scene 3 ("thrown stubbornly out of true by a two-degree seaward tilt of the chalk footing") and Nicholas measures exactly that in Scene 6 ("A two-degree seaward rake"), so the setup pays off.
- The debt stays at £18,000 throughout, and the price of striking the listing is stated as a rule the story keeps: "If you strike the listing, you default." (Scene 8)

## Out of lane
The innkeeper and the vendor are both called Arthur, which invites confusion (grounding).
