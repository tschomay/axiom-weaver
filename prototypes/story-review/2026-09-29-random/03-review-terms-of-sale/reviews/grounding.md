# Grounding and Detail — Terms of Sale

**Score: 3/5** — The first five scenes are well placed in space. From Scene 6 on, the reader has to fill in offstage moves: how the pair got back to the Redoubt and down into the cellar. The waiver reveal, which the plot turns on, is stated as a claim rather than explained.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The jump into the flooded cellar is never shown
- **Where:** Scene 7 (following Scene 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 6 ends: "Nicholas stared at the brass plumb bob swinging gently against the chalky dust of the sill". Scene 7 opens: "In the vaulted cellar of The Redoubt, Jean Gallagher froze on the bottom step of the Portland stone stairs, her boots already hemmed in by three inches of foul, frothing tide."
- **Problem:** When Scene 6 ends, Jean is outside on a phone call and Nicholas is alone in the salon. Jean had earlier made a point of keeping him out of the cellar ("the main breaker is isolated"). Scene 7 then puts them in the cellar together, with a lantern, while it floods. Who went down, why, and what happened to Jean's call are all left for the reader to supply, and this is the confrontation the story is built around.
- **Reader needed:** One or two beats showing how the two of them ended up in the cellar and who led the way.

### 2. [major] The return to the Redoubt, and when it happens, is not grounded
- **Where:** Scene 6 (also Scenes 4 and 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 6: "For nearly an hour they had wandered through the cavernous upper suites". Scene 5: "The gaslamps inside Gallagher and Sons Estate Office hissed against the gathering dark". Scene 7: "The four o'clock harbour siren groaned across the spit".
- **Problem:** Scene 3 ends with the two of them on the terrace. Scene 4 has Nicholas back at the inn, Scene 5 has Jean at the office at dusk, and Scene 6 has them both at the Redoubt again at twilight, with no reason or means given for the second visit. Scene 7 then fixes the time at four o'clock. At each cut the reader cannot say how much time has passed or how anyone crossed the spit.
- **Reader needed:** At the top of Scenes 4–6, a clear marker of time relative to the tour, plus a line explaining why they went back to the house.

### 3. [major] The waiver reveal is asserted, not explained
- **Where:** Scene 9 (depends on Scenes 5 and 8)
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "He instructed this firm to sell strictly under caveat emptor, without warranting the footings." Scene 9: "Gallagher & Sons declines to market without mandatory disclosure." and "knowing that the moment she produced it, Brenda would pull the agency's credit"
- **Problem:** The two scenes describe the paper differently: in one it is a vendor's caveat-emptor instruction, in the other a refusal to market without disclosure. The reason producing it would make Brenda pull credit is never given. The exchange "You could have handed this to Brenda three weeks ago" / "And let her dump the liability on whoever bought the note?" names a mechanism nobody has explained. The scene is meant to clear Jean, but the reader has to work out why the paper clears her.
- **Reader needed:** A plain statement of what the waiver commits the agency to, and why disclosing it costs Jean the agency.

### 4. [minor] The resignation mechanics in the final scene are hard to follow
- **Where:** Scene 10 (depends on Scene 7)
- **Quote:** "Except the mandate applies only to accredited inspectors of the Trust. As of six o'clock, I am merely an unemployed gentleman" / "You threw away twelve years of standing to keep my name out of the Gazette?"
- **Problem:** By this point Jean has already withdrawn the listing and lost the firm. The reader cannot tell what a condemnation notice would still do to her, or why resigning (rather than just filing a report that clears her) is what protects her. The romantic gesture depends on a rule the story set up in one line in Scene 7.
- **Reader needed:** One sentence on what the notice would still cost Jean after the foreclosure.

### 5. [minor] The period is never fixed, and a mobile phone breaks it
- **Where:** Scene 6 (setting built up in Scenes 1, 2 and 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "The gaslamps inside Gallagher and Sons Estate Office hissed" / "three seconds with a Lucifer". Scene 6: "She withdrew the handset, glanced at the glowing display".
- **Problem:** Gaslamps, vesta cases, oil lamps, pocket watches and a ferry gig all place the story in the past. A glowing handset with patchy signal then turns up with no warning. The reader cannot say when the story is set.
- **Reader needed:** An early cue that settles the era, so later technology fits it.

### 6. [minor] People named before they are introduced, or sharing a name
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 4, Scene 6
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "There it stood, in Toby's careful, looping hand". Scene 4: "the innkeeper, Arthur, inquired". Scene 6: "It's the district manager. Brenda's office."
- **Problem:** Toby appears by first name only until Scene 4 says who he is. The innkeeper shares a first name with Arthur Arkwright, which invites a moment of "the same Arthur?". "District manager" gives Brenda's office a role nothing else in the story gives it; elsewhere she is a creditor running Keel Holdings or the Keel Trust.
- **Reader needed:** A role tag the first time each name appears, and distinct names for different people.

### 7. [minor] Referenced events never happened on the page
- **Where:** Scene 7
- **Quote:** "stripped of the hesitant gentility he had offered her across tea at the harbour inn" / "whatever polite fiction he had carried into her office that morning"
- **Problem:** Jean and Nicholas never have tea at the inn, and he never visits her office. They first meet at the Redoubt in the afternoon. The reader goes looking for a scene they must have missed.
- **Reader needed:** References to the shared history the reader actually saw.

### 8. [minor] The hidden gauge is explained again and again
- **Where:** Scene 6 (also Scenes 3, 4 and 7)
- **Quote:** "It held no ink, no nib, only the slim brass leaves of a calibrated crack-width gauge—a covert instrument designed to diagnose ruin under the guise of casual scribbling."
- **Problem:** Scene 2 already sets up the gutted pen thoroughly. Scenes 4, 6 and 7 each re-explain it, and in the end it is never used: the plumb line does the measuring.
- **Reader needed:** After Scene 2, a brief reference is enough.

## What works
- Scene 1 sets place, weather, time and stakes in its first lines and plants the four o'clock deadline clearly: "the Portling tidal sluice gate automatically closes at four in the afternoon on storm warning days, cutting off road access to The Redoubt spit."
- The plumb-line measurement in Scene 6 is shown step by step, so the diagnosis lands as a finding rather than a claim: "At the top casing, zero. At the lower sill, a clear, unmistakable offset. A two-degree seaward rake."
- The combination lock is shown in Scene 5 and paid off in Scene 9, so the reader can follow the drawer: "aligned the four milled brass wheels of the tumbler mechanism: nineteen forty-eight, the agency's founding year."

## Out of lane
The clock times contradict each other: the warrant "arrived at quarter past three" while Jean was in the cellar at four; the island is "cut off until midnight" yet she is back within the hour; the burning was "yesterday". This is a consistency issue.
