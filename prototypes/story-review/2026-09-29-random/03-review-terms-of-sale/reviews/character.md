# Character Coherence — Terms of Sale

**Score: 3/5** — The two leads have distinct wants and readable fears, but the story's two biggest choices (Nicholas's resignation and Jean's move from hiding the cellar to giving up the agency) are not motivated on the page. The ending also calls Jean scrupulously honest, which the prose contradicts.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Jean's integrity is announced, but her behaviour contradicts it and nobody reckons with that
- **Where:** Scene 3, then Scene 7 and Scene 9 (and Scene 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "The cellar is entirely dark, I'm afraid—the main breaker is isolated for the winter draughts," and "The softwood sashes swell like dried peas in a kitchen pot" / Scene 7: "I never lied to you about the foundation, Nicholas!" / Scene 9: "the woman who had warned him about the southern bay window when silence would have cleared her debt"
- **Problem:** On the page Jean lies about the sash, invents a breaker fault to keep him out of the cellar, and in Scene 7 lies again ("The drainage trap sticks"). Yet she protests that she never lied, Nicholas remembers her *warning* him, and the ending ("my grandfather never sold a wet cellar as dry ground") treats her as spotless. Her own waiver even says the firm "declines to market without mandatory disclosure", while she was doing just that.
- **Reader needed:** Either a moment where Jean owns the deflections as the pressure she gave in to, or earlier behaviour that actually matches the honest agent the ending celebrates.

### 2. [major] Nicholas resigns to bury a condemnation he believes in
- **Where:** Scene 10 (against Scene 7)
- **Quote:** Scene 7: "People could have moved their children into these rooms. A family could have been sleeping above that bay when the cliff face broke off!" / Scene 10: "As of six o'clock, I am merely an unemployed gentleman with … an unfiled draft in his pocket."
- **Problem:** Nicholas's core value is that a ruined building must be declared ruined ("a building was either sound or it was not", Scene 2). Yet his big romantic gesture is to suppress the public condemnation of a house that really is sliding into the sea. By Scene 10 the listing is withdrawn and the agency already repossessed, so the sacrifice protects almost nothing. It also clashes with the "absolute disclosure" partnership he proposes a page later.
- **Reader needed:** A visible reason why *not* filing serves something Nicholas cares about, or a gesture that fits his devotion to the truth of the stone.

### 3. [major] Jean takes Nicholas into the cellar she fought to keep him out of
- **Where:** Scene 7 (against Scene 3 and Scene 6)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "The cellar was the very last precinct on the English coastline she could permit this perceptive man to inspect." / Scene 7: "In the vaulted cellar of The Redoubt, Jean Gallagher froze on the bottom step of the Portland stone stairs"
- **Problem:** The last we saw, Jean had stepped outside to take Brenda's call and Nicholas was alone upstairs. Next she is standing in the cellar with him. We are never shown why the woman who invented a fault to block the cellar would go down there, or agree to it.
- **Reader needed:** The moment she chooses (or is forced) to open the cellar, which is her real turning point.

### 4. [minor] Jean's decisive turn happens off-page
- **Where:** Scene 8 (against Scene 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 5: "We sell the property on its merits, or we negotiate down the indemnity." / Scene 8: "The listing is struck. Permanent withdrawal on grounds of severe coastal cliff subsidence."
- **Problem:** In Scene 5 Jean still means to sell. In Scene 8 she has already withdrawn the listing and accepted ruin. The choice that costs her the agency is reported, not shown, so her "entirely, utterly free" feeling lands as an announcement rather than a payoff.
- **Reader needed:** A beat between the cellar and the office where Jean actually decides to strike the listing and what tips her.

### 5. [minor] Nicholas swings between resolve and tenderness without cause
- **Where:** Scene 4 to Scene 6
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "when the hammer fell, he would make certain she bore the full weight of it." / Scene 6: "there was an uncalculated warmth between them, an impulse that made Nicholas want to take the pen casing from his pocket and cast it down into the foam below"
- **Problem:** Scene 4 ends with cold, vengeful certainty. Nothing between that and Scene 6 softens him, apart from the father anecdote he has every reason to distrust as a swindler's patter. He then snaps back to fury in Scene 7. The swings follow what each scene needs rather than anything that happens to him.
- **Reader needed:** Some sign of what in Jean's balcony talk gets past the suspicion he had just settled on.

### 6. [minor] Brenda and Toby exist to push the plot
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 4, Scene 5, Scene 8
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "Then Toby had more commercial sense in his little finger than your father ever passed down to you," / Scene 4: "If Jean asks—well, don't let her ask."
- **Problem:** Brenda is pure obstruction: gloves, purring, warrants. Her one flicker of "cold calculation" (Scene 8) goes nowhere. Toby only proposes the fraud and, conveniently, announces the agency's debts aloud where Nicholas can hear. Neither has a want beyond their plot job. Toby, notably, gets no reaction to Jean refusing him or to the agency's fall.
- **Reader needed:** One beat each of what the person wants for themselves, e.g. what the closure costs Toby.

### 7. [nit] Leads and antagonist share one arch register
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 7, Scene 10
- **Quote:** Scene 10: "Let us tally the bill honestly, Mr. Vance." / Scene 7: "Do not insult us both by lying about a trap"
- **Problem:** Jean, Nicholas and Brenda all speak in the same polished, ledger-metaphor wit. Toby's panic is the only clearly distinct voice.
- **Reader needed:** Some difference between the surveyor's precise idiom and the agent's salesmanship.

## What works
- Nicholas's opening unease is concrete and fits him: "Stepping on the fingers of someone already hanging by their fingernails was not what he had signed up for" (Scene 2).
- Jean's refusal to burn the waiver is dramatised through fear as well as principle, which makes it credible: "There was an ocean of difference between letting a buyer fall in love with an awkward house and committing outright criminal fraud" (Scene 5).
- Scene 9's reversal is well staged from Nicholas's side: "To take the key was to step off the clean, orderly platform of professional outrage and risk discovering he had mismeasured the drop entirely."

## Out of lane
Timeline and name clashes (warrant "quarter past three" vs Brenda arriving after four; two Arthurs; "tea at the harbour inn" that never happened) belong to the consistency reviewer.
