# Prose Craft — Terms of Sale

**Score: 2/5** — There is real wit in places, but the prose keeps pulling the reader out. Nearly every scene opens with the same comic simile of weather as an aggrieved clerk. Adjectives come stacked in twos and threes. The same stock descriptors return scene after scene. And a mobile phone rings out of nowhere in a gaslit, Lucifer-match world.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Period voice breaks with a mobile phone
- **Where:** Scene 6 (against the texture of Scenes 1, 2, 5, 9)
- **Quote:** "Then a metallic ring shattered the silence. In her skirt pocket, Jean's telephone buzzed with insistent vibration. / She withdrew the handset, glanced at the glowing display … the signal is terrible inside the salon."
- **Problem:** Up to this point the story has built an interwar or mid-century world: "The gaslamps inside Gallagher and Sons Estate Office hissed" (Scene 5), "a brass vesta case" and "a Lucifer" (Scene 5), buyers ferried "by gig" (Scene 1), a gutted Conway Stewart pen (Scene 2). A glowing handset with bad signal breaks that world in one sentence. The sentence also contradicts itself: the phone gives "a metallic ring" and then "buzzed with insistent vibration".
- **Reader needed:** The call to Jean must come by a means that belongs to the world already built, such as a boy sent with a message or a telephone in the house.

### 2. [major] The same comic simile opens scene after scene
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 2, Scene 6, Scene 8 (the same device is also used on a person in Scene 1 and misfires in Scene 10)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "with the indignant petulance of a landlord whose tea had arrived cold"; Scene 2: "with the severe, unblinking disapproval of a parish auditor examining a butcher's receipts"; Scene 6: "with the shrill, persistent grievance of an elderly clerk denied his half-day"; Scene 8: "with all the sulky petulance of an unpaid apprentice made to scrub flags in November"
- **Problem:** Four scenes open on weather or a gull behaving "with the [adjective] [noun] of a [minor functionary with a grievance]". "Petulance" appears twice. What charms the first time is a formula by the third. In Scene 10 the same machinery produces a simile that works against the moment: Nicholas's proposal comes out "with sudden, eager precision—a cat stalking a particularly plump field mouse across a sunny parlor rug", which makes Jean the prey.
- **Reader needed:** Each scene should open with its own image, and the joke should get one or two outings, not a standing slot at the top of every scene.

### 3. [minor] Stock descriptors recycled across scenes
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 2, 5, 8, 9, 10 ("scarred"); Scenes 5, 8, 10 ("three generations"); Scenes 8 and 10 ("lightness")
- **Quote:** "scarred mahogany counter" (Scene 1, Scene 5, Scene 8, Scene 9), "scarred mahogany desk" (Scene 9), "scarred pine table" (Scene 2), "the scarred table" (Scene 10); "absorbed three generations of spilled ink" (Scene 5) / "absorbed three generations of maritime grievances" (Scene 10)
- **Problem:** "Scarred" appears seven times and "three generations" three times, twice with the same verb "absorbed". Jean's release is described as "an extraordinary lightness" (Scene 8) and then again as "an unexpected, giddy lightness" (Scene 10). Each writer reached for the same small set of words, and a reader hears the repetition.
- **Reader needed:** Each named object should be described once and then trusted to stay furnished.

### 4. [minor] Scenes re-introduce people, places and props as if the story had just begun
- **Where:** Scenes 2, 3, 10 (age); Scenes 2, 6 (pen); Scenes 2, 4, 10 (inn); Scene 8 (Brenda); Scenes 5, 8, 9 (combination)
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "He was thirty-two"; Scene 3: "Nicholas Vance, thirty-two and carrying himself…"; Scene 6: "It held no ink, no nib, only the slim brass leaves of a calibrated crack-width gauge—a covert instrument designed to diagnose ruin"; Scene 8: "The town creditor—who had held Portling's commercial debts in an iron grip for twenty years"
- **Problem:** Nicholas's age is given three times, and the gutted pen is explained a second time. The Anchorage is re-described each time a scene lands there. Brenda gets a fresh introduction in Scene 8. The combination is explained as "nineteen forty-eight, the agency's founding year" in both Scene 5 and Scene 8. Together these are the seams of scenes written separately.
- **Reader needed:** Later scenes should build on what the reader already holds rather than re-briefing them.

### 5. [minor] The surveying instrument changes form from scene to scene
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 4, Scene 6, Scene 9
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "a calibrated brass crack width gauge … alongside a finely weighted miniature plumb bob"; Scene 6: "he bypassed the deceptive fountain pen and drew out his heavy brass plumb line, its braided cord wrapped taut around a turned rosewood spool"; Scene 9: "The plumb line in his coat pocket felt less like evidence and more like dead iron."
- **Problem:** The key prop is concealed in a pen in one scene and a separate heavy spool in the next. It is brass in one place and iron in another. It sits in the "inner breast pocket" (Scene 2) and then "in his waistcoat pocket" (Scene 4). The story's central image never settles.
- **Reader needed:** The props need one consistent description so the reader can track them.

### 6. [minor] Model tics: "not merely X; it was Y", stated emotions in threes, portentous scene endings
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 4, 6, 7 (construction); Scenes 4, 6, 7, 8 (endings); Scenes 3, 8, 9 (triplets)
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "does not merely blow against an estate agent's bay window; it lodges an official complaint"; Scene 6: "It was not mere neglect or old mortar."; Scene 8: "feeling ruined, bankrupt, and entirely, utterly free"; Scene 9: "extraordinarily clumsy, petty, and small"
- **Problem:** The "not X; Y" reversal appears at least six times (also Scene 4 "Toby was not merely drinking"; Scene 6 "The wood had not merely swollen"; Scene 7 "Arkwright had not panelled…; he had slapped…"). Scenes 4, 6, 7 and 8 all end on a one-line verdict, and the narrator announces emotions ("tears of shock and fury", "utterly stripped of his neat accusations") instead of dramatising them. Stacked adjectives such as "a sharp, assessing gaze that was startlingly quick" (Scene 3) thicken almost every paragraph.
- **Reader needed:** Fewer announced verdicts, and more room for a gesture or a line of dialogue to carry the feeling.

### 7. [minor] Expository and stilted dialogue
- **Where:** Scene 7, Scene 10 (also Scene 4)
- **Quote:** Scene 7: "\"My name is Nicholas Vance,\" he said … \"Senior Building Surveyor for the maritime board. I was sent here on an undercover assignment…\""; Scene 10: "\"Under section four, a surveyor retained by the Trust is legally bound to publish a public condemnation notice…\""
- **Problem:** Nicholas tells Jean a name she has used all afternoon and recites his job title and the terms of his contract like a charge sheet. In Scene 10 he repeats the mandate from Scene 7 as a statute reading. In Scene 7 both characters shout in exclamation marks ("Look at it!", "I did not!"), which flattens the confrontation into melodrama.
- **Reader needed:** The reveal and the resignation should come through what the characters want from each other, not through recited credentials.

### 8. [nit] Copy-edit slips and spelling drift
- **Where:** Scene 7, Scene 9, passim
- **Quote:** Scene 7: "arkwright had not panelled the basement"; Scene 9: "Arkwright didn't just pane over active shear lines"; Scene 9: "hiding it would have let her pocket a buyer's deposit and walked away clean"
- **Problem:** A lowercase name, "pane" for "panel", and a broken verb form. British and American spellings are mixed ("harbour"/"harbor", "colour"/"color", "parlour"/"parlor", "Mr"/"Mr."). In Scene 9 one sentence says "demand note, demanding immediate settlement on pain of immediate foreclosure", and another pair of sentences goes from "irritated him enormously" to "irritatingly tranquil".
- **Reader needed:** One proofreading pass and a single house spelling.

## What works
- Brenda's introduction in Scene 1 is dry, precise and funny: "Brenda Keel did not remove her pigskin gloves when indoors. To remove them would suggest a willingness to linger, or worse, sympathy."
- Jean's small superstitions give her a voice early on: "One did not touch formal demand notes until absolutely necessary; doing so seemed to invite the ink into one's bloodstream." (Scene 1)
- The closing banter in Scene 10 is light and earns its ending: "\"A catastrophic balance sheet,\" she murmured … \"Disastrous,\" Nicholas agreed" and "\"Gallagher comes first. And we buy our own chalk.\""

## Out of lane
The timeline and backstory contradict each other in places (Scene 9's warrant arrives "at quarter past three" after Scene 8 puts it past four; Scene 7 refers to "tea at the harbour inn" and Scene 9 to Jean having "warned him about the southern bay window", and the story shows neither), which is for the logic reviewer.
