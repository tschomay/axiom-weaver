# Setup and Payoff — Terms of Sale

**Score: 3/5** — The waiver, the 1948 combination, the whistleblower mandate and the "total disclosure" ending are all planted and paid off fairly. But one major setup is dropped: Jean really does hide the cellar and lie about the sash. The reconciliation leaves that out and even says the opposite happened, and the waiver reveal itself is staged twice.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Jean's on-page concealment is never answered, and the reconciliation says the opposite happened
- **Where:** Scene 3 and Scene 7, against Scene 9
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "The cellar was the very last precinct on the English coastline she could permit this perceptive man to inspect." / "'Just the coastal damp,' Jean said with an airy, practiced laugh" — Scene 9: "the woman who had warned him about the southern bay window when silence would have cleared her debt."
- **Problem:** The reader watched Jean lie about the sash, fake a reason to keep him out of the cellar ("the main breaker is isolated"), and in Scene 7 lie again ("The drainage trap sticks"). Then she says "I never lied to you about the foundation." Scene 9 clears her by claiming she warned him, which never happened. Nobody on the page acknowledges her deceptions, so the romance turns on an innocence that the earlier scenes don't support.
- **Reader needed:** Either an honest reckoning with the tour-day evasions (Jean owning them, or Nicholas seeing why the waiver outweighs them), or earlier scenes that actually show her warning him.

### 2. [major] The waiver is revealed twice, and the second time adds a clause the reader never saw
- **Where:** Scene 5, Scene 8, Scene 9
- **Quote:** Scene 8: "She knelt before the low pedestal of her oak desk and spun the brass dial to nineteen forty-eight … She slid open the drawer and drew out the green-bound listing ledger" — Scene 9: "Jean had possessed the legal shield all along."
- **Problem:** The reader has seen the waiver in Scene 5. Scene 8 goes through the dial, drawer and ledger ritual and explains the waiver in full to Brenda. Scene 9 repeats the same ritual a scene later and presents it as a revelation, with the narrator explaining its meaning ("She had kept the waiver in the ledger, knowing…"). Nicholas's key discovery is new information to him but a rerun for the reader. The one thing that is new to the reader, Jean's handwritten clause "declines to market without mandatory disclosure", appears for the first time here. Earlier scenes described a "caveat emptor" waiver, so the proof that clears her is partly unplanted.
- **Reader needed:** Nicholas's discovery should be the first time the reader sees how the waiver clears Jean (so Scene 8 should hold that back), and her disclosure clause should be shown before it matters.

### 3. [minor] The hollow-pen crack gauge gets a full reveal and is never used
- **Where:** Scene 2 (recurs Scene 3, 4, 6, 7, 10)
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "a calibrated brass crack width gauge, engineered down to tenths of a millimetre … this little brass tongue would measure the yawning gap without ever betraying its purpose." — Scene 6: "he bypassed the deceptive fountain pen and drew out his heavy brass plumb line"
- **Problem:** The story gives the pen a lingering reveal and keeps mentioning it: it clinks, it weighs on his chest, he wants to throw it into the sea. But it never measures anything, never gives him away, and is never discarded. Scene 7's "chased vulcanite cap … jutted plainly from his lapel" hints at a payoff that never comes, and Scene 10 swaps it for "an expensive pair of calipers."
- **Reader needed:** The gauge measuring the cellar crack or exposing his cover, or at least a closing gesture with it, such as handing it over or putting it away.

### 4. [minor] The four-o'clock sluice is built up as a trap, then the stranding is skipped
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 7, against Scene 8
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "the Portling tidal sluice gate automatically closes at four in the afternoon on storm warning days, cutting off road access" — Scene 7: "'Nicholas—we're cut off until midnight.'" — Scene 8: "By the time the high tide receded enough for the causeway skiff to ferry her back"
- **Problem:** Scene 1 sets up the gate as a clock ("He will be through the gate by three"), and Scene 7 springs it on the pair, stranding them together. The next scene opens after the stranding is over. The forced night together, the obvious payoff in a courtship story, is dropped, so the gate ends up only as scenery.
- **Reader needed:** Some consequence of being cut off together, whether a scene, a beat or a shift between them, or else no promise of stranding in the first place.

### 5. [minor] The tilt is stated before Nicholas measures it
- **Where:** Scene 3, against Scene 6
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "thrown stubbornly out of true by a two-degree seaward tilt of the chalk footing" — Scene 6: "A two-degree seaward rake. The entire southern bay was tilting toward the drop"
- **Problem:** Scene 3 hands the reader the exact figure and cause, so Nicholas's careful plumb-line work in Scene 6 only confirms what we already know. It is written as a discovery ("the cold realization") but lands as repetition.
- **Reader needed:** Jean's dread without the exact figure, so the measurement in Scene 6 can be the reveal.

### 6. [minor] The payoff of the resignation depends on stakes the story has already wiped out
- **Where:** Scene 7 and Scene 10
- **Quote:** Scene 10: "the Trust would have had the board papered over by noon, and the Ministry would have pulled the firm's trading charter by Friday."
- **Problem:** The mandate is fairly planted in Scene 7. But by Scene 10 the firm has already been repossessed and the villa withdrawn, so the charter he "saves" is already gone. The sacrifice gets its weight from Nicholas saying it matters ("to keep my name out of the Gazette"), not from anything still at risk.
- **Reader needed:** A concrete harm to Jean that the unfiled notice actually prevents after the repossession.

### 7. [nit] Brenda's flicker of temptation goes nowhere
- **Where:** Scene 8
- **Quote:** "something like cold calculation flickered behind her spectacles—a creditor measuring the ease of an illicit escape against the friction of repossession"
- **Problem:** This hints that Brenda might collude or have a hidden angle, and it is never followed up.
- **Reader needed:** Either a follow-through or no hint at all.

## What works
- The father's maxim pays off in the ending's terms. It is set up in Scene 6 ("an agent who cuts a corner is really only digging a pit to fall into later") and returns as the partnership's founding term: "Gallagher first. On total disclosure."
- Jean refusing to burn the waiver in Scene 5 ("We do not burn files. We never have") is a clean, character-driven setup for her ruin and for Scene 9.
- The whistleblower mandate is stated plainly in Scene 7 ("legally bound by a whistleblower mandate to file a statutory condemnation notice"), so Nicholas's resignation in Scene 10 feels earned, not invented.

## Out of lane
Scene 9 says Jean "left the spit before the sluice came down" and the warrant "arrived at quarter past three", which contradicts Scene 7 and Scene 8 (consistency).
