# Prose Craft — Second Pressing

**Score: 3/5** — The sensory cellar detail is competent and often precise, but a reader will notice the recycled gestures (jaws, flat voices), the dialogue that explains itself, and a final scene that re-describes and re-explains things the reader already has.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Scene 5 re-explains the story as if it had just begun
- **Where:** Scene 5 (echoing Scene 4)
- **Quote:** "The Highline Crest Resorts contract. Section four laid it out in plain, unsparing prose: full debt assumption and cash settlement for the Callow estate, contingent upon the immediate, permanent cessation of all commercial viticulture." / "Bruno hadn't blundered. He had taken Clara's unpasteurized dessert pressings and deliberately dosed the topping carboy with live vinegar wash to force the collapse."
- **Problem:** Scene 4 already gave the contract clause ("contingent upon the immediate and permanent cessation of all commercial winemaking on the parcel before spring") and Bruno's confession. Scene 5 restates both, and Bruno repeats the hail backstory ("The vintage was dead in November… The hail took half the hillside"). It reads like a separately written scene recapping for a reader who has not seen the earlier ones, and it slows the climax.
- **Reader needed:** For the last scene to rely on what the reader already knows, and to spend its words on Tess's choice and Clara's discovery.

### 2. [minor] Recycled physical tells: jaws and flat voices
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
- **Quote:** "his voice flat, dropping like lead" (Scene 1); "her jaw rigid" (Scene 2); "his jaw tightening" (Scene 3); "his voice flat, exhausted" … "his jaw working" (Scene 4); "his voice flat, pitch-dry" … "his jaw tight" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Five jaw beats and three "voice flat" tags across five scenes, plus "voice dropping" in Scenes 2 and 4 and cold settling in Tess's chest in Scenes 1 and 4 ("The cold in Tess's chest hardened" / "a cold certainty settling in her chest"). Every scene reaches for the same stock gestures to signal tension.
- **Reader needed:** Gestures specific to each character and moment, so the tension is not always signalled by the same body parts.

### 3. [minor] Props change and labels collide across scene seams
- **Where:** Scene 4 → Scene 5; Scene 1 / Scene 3 / Scene 5
- **Quote:** "He reached into his coat for his fountain pen." (Scene 4) / "a cashier's voucher for twelve hundred dollars, endorsed on the back, and a typed recommendation on estate stationery" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** The handwritten note Tess asked for "In ink. Signed by your hand" becomes a typed letter and a cashier's voucher. The "manila folder" of "stiff pages" becomes "stiff parchment". "Section four" names a clause of Tess's employment agreement in Scene 3 and then a clause of the resort contract in Scene 5. Scene 5 also calls the same object a "draft slip" and a "voucher". These are the marks of separately written scenes, and "Section four" in particular makes a reader stop and reread.
- **Reader needed:** The same objects, described the same way, carried from one scene to the next.

### 4. [minor] Expository dialogue
- **Where:** Scene 1 (also Scene 2, Scene 5)
- **Quote:** "Topping-up—replacing the wine lost to evaporation so air couldn't pocket in the headspace—is work a first-year cellar hand manages without souring an entire vintage. My signature is on your seasonal employment agreement, Vance."
- **Problem:** Bruno defines a term his cellar hand already knows, and tells her about a contract she signed. It is dialogue aimed at the reader, not at Tess. The same happens in Clara's "The potassium metabisulphite dispenser has been locked in my cabinet all week" (Scene 2) and in Clara's two-line summary of the whole plot in Scene 5: "You poisoned our wine… You poisoned my harvest to break the estate."
- **Reader needed:** Technical context carried by narration or action, so the characters talk to each other.

### 5. [minor] POV drifts between and within scenes
- **Where:** Scene 2, Scene 3
- **Quote:** "To Clara, the shape of the disaster was clear: her brother had grown desperate, cut a reckless corner to puff up their low gallonage, and ruined everything through sheer, arrogant incompetence." (Scene 3)
- **Problem:** Scene 1 is close on Tess. Scene 2 moves into Clara's head without signalling it ("her stomach tightening"). Scene 3 opens on Tess ("Tess had to hurry to keep pace") and closes in Clara's mind with a summary line that tells us her conclusion. The narrative distance shifts from scene to scene.
- **Reader needed:** A consistent anchoring point of view, or clear handoffs when it moves.

### 6. [minor] Telling, plus the "not X, but Y" construction
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 3, Scene 5
- **Quote:** "The clause was real, and it meant walking out of the county blacklisted and penniless." (Scene 1); "The cruelty of it sat between them, ugly and deliberate." … "Keeping silent wasn't survival anymore; it was an endorsement." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** The narrator states the stakes and the moral turn instead of dramatising them. The "It wasn't X; it was Y" pattern also opens the story ("It wasn't the clean, earthy sting of toasted oak…; it was the raw, prickly vapor of vinegar") and recurs in Scene 3 ("not the shock of an innocent man…, but the strange, sudden relief").
- **Reader needed:** Tess's decision shown through what she does, which Scene 5 then does anyway with the contract on the table.

### 7. [nit] Heavy dialogue tags
- **Where:** Scenes 2, 3, 5
- **Quote:** "Clara spat" (Scene 2); "pointing violently down at the thirty-litre carboy" (Scene 3); "Bruno said coldly" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Adverbs and strong verbs in the tags do work the lines already do.
- **Reader needed:** Plain tags, so the dialogue carries the tone.

### 8. [nit] Loose sentences and inconsistent spelling
- **Where:** Scenes 1, 2, 4, 5
- **Quote:** "Bruno rubbed his jaw, where dark stubble shadowed his neck." (Scene 4); "In his pocket, the matter was closed." (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** The jaw/neck image misfires, and the pocket line is an odd figure of speech. Spelling also wanders: "sulfur" (Scene 1), "sulphite" (Scene 2), "sulphur" (Scene 4). Units mix too: "thirty-litre" carboys against "twenty gallons" and "fifty milliliters".
- **Reader needed:** One spelling convention and images that land on the body part they name.

## What works
- Precise sensory work in the cellar: "He didn't even lean in; the sharp, vinegar vapor was already blooming out of the bunghole." (Scene 1)
- Understated reveals through objects: "The nut was off. It lay resting on the rim of an empty bucket." (Scene 2)
- A strong image of futility: "testing cask after cask as though sheer frantic labor could pull the vinegar back out of the wood." (Scene 5)

## Out of lane
In Scene 5 Clara asks "How could a cellar contamination spread this fast?" after she diagnosed exactly that in Scene 3. That is a continuity/logic question for another lens.
