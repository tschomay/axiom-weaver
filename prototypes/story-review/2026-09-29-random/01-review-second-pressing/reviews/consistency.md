# Logical Consistency — Second Pressing

**Score: 3/5** — the central clue (what was in the carboy) is described two incompatible ways, and several smaller facts (yield loss, "Section four", the payment instrument, the hail's timing) drift between scenes.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The carboy smells sweet and fresh, but it was dosed with sour vinegar mother
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 2, against Scene 4 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "a soft, cloying sweetness drifted from the glass mouth—the unpreserved fragrance of dessert pressings left exposed, sweet and flat"; Scene 2: "The carboy in the cellar smelled like fresh grape pulp."
- **Quote (later):** Scene 4: "The pressings had already turned sour on the skins. Full of active mother. I poured five gallons straight into the topping carboy"; Scene 5: "deliberately dosed the topping carboy with live vinegar wash"
- **Problem:** The story's key physical clue says the carboy held sweet, unturned juice. The confession says it held pressings already turned to vinegar. A carboy of active mother, sitting open since Tuesday, would smell of vinegar, not "fresh grape pulp". The reveal overturns the clue the reader was given.
- **Reader needed:** The carboy's smell in Scene 1 needs to fit what Bruno later says he put in it, or the story needs to say why the sourness was masked.

### 2. [minor] "Section four" moves from Tess's contract to the resort contract
- **Where:** Scene 3 and Scene 4, against Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "Section four of your agreement covers basic diligence"; Scene 4: "You cited Section Four."
- **Quote (later):** Scene 5: "The Highline Crest Resorts contract. Section four laid it out in plain, unsparing prose: full debt assumption"
- **Problem:** Section four was the negligence clause in Tess's employment agreement. In Scene 5 it becomes the resort contract's debt clause, which Scene 4 had placed "on the second page".
- **Reader needed:** One label for one document, so the reader doesn't wonder whether the two contracts are linked.

### 3. [minor] The harvest shortfall changes from thirty percent to half
- **Where:** Scene 3 and Scene 4 (repeated in Scene 5)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "We were thirty percent under on the vintage yield"; Scene 4: "It took fifty percent of the fruit off the hill ... Half the crop gone"
- **Problem:** Bruno states two different figures for the same loss, and the story never marks either one as a lie.
- **Reader needed:** One consistent figure, or a clear sign that the first one was part of his cover story.

### 4. [minor] The handwritten draft becomes a cashier's voucher and a typed letter, then a draft again
- **Where:** Scene 4 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "a clean letter of discharge ... In ink. Signed by your hand." / "He reached into his coat for his fountain pen." Scene 5: "a cashier's voucher for twelve hundred dollars, endorsed on the back, and a typed recommendation on estate stationery"
- **Problem:** A few minutes and a flight of stairs separate the pen from the handover. That leaves no time to get a cashier's voucher or type a letter, and Scene 5 opens and closes by calling the same paper a "draft slip".
- **Reader needed:** The payment and letter should be the handwritten items Scene 4 set up.

### 5. [minor] Bruno "left the dispenser locked", but Clara held the only key
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 4
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "The potassium metabisulphite dispenser has been locked in my cabinet all week. The key has been on my belt loop since Monday." Scene 4: "And you left the sulphur dispenser locked inside her cabinet ... You made sure I couldn't dose the barrels"
- **Problem:** Tess credits Bruno with a lock that Clara controlled all week. Nothing shows Bruno arranging for it to stay locked.
- **Reader needed:** Some way Bruno made sure the preservative stayed out of reach, or Tess's accusation should fit the facts from Scene 2.

### 6. [minor] Clara forgets the explanation she gave two scenes earlier
- **Where:** Scene 3 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "You know what unheated dessert must does without a sulphite dose! It turned every stave into an incubator for acetobacter." Scene 5: "How could a cellar contamination spread this fast? It makes no sense. The pressings were clean when they came off the slats."
- **Problem:** Clara worked out and shouted the mechanism in Scene 3. In Scene 5 she is puzzled by it as if she never had.
- **Reader needed:** Her Scene 5 confusion should be about something she doesn't already know, such as how the must could sour this fast.

### 7. [minor] Hail in November, before picking, doesn't fit a vintage whose secondary fermentation ended "weeks ago"
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 4, Scene 5
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "The secondary fermentation finished clean weeks ago." Scene 5: "The vintage was dead in November ... The hail took half the hillside before we ever picked a cluster."
- **Problem:** If the fruit was still unpicked in November, the harvest, pressing, primary fermentation and completed secondary fermentation all have to fit into the time since then. The deadline "before spring" gives no room for that.
- **Reader needed:** A hail date that comes before harvest in a normal calendar.

### 8. [nit] Small physical drift in the carboy, the valve and the light
- **Where:** Scenes 1–3, 5
- **Quote:** Scene 1: carboy "Resting on a low wooden dolly ... toward the alcove near the drainage trench"; Scene 3: "the thirty-litre carboy nested in its woven wicker jacket beside cask four"; Scene 2: hose "still damp at the nozzle" vs Scene 3: "The valve was dry."
- **Problem:** The carboy changes place with no one moving it. The damp hose becomes a "dry" valve. Clara's butterfly-nut clamp becomes "unlocked my line". The cellar's lantern light (Scene 3) becomes "bare hanging bulbs" (Scene 5).
- **Reader needed:** Each object and light source should stay where and as it was left.

## What works
- The count of twelve casks holds in every scene: "All twelve oak barrels. Across every separate lot, both tiers."
- The Monday/Tuesday timeline lines up: "Those casks were stable on Monday" / "You performed the topping-up on Tuesday afternoon" / "I poured five gallons straight into the topping carboy on Monday night."
- The $1,200 figure and the contract as Tess's leverage carry through cleanly from Scene 4 to the reveal in Scene 5.

## Out of lane
Clara's unexplained return from "toward the road" to the cellar in Scene 5 is a grounding question, not a contradiction.
