# Engagement and Believability — Second Pressing

**Score: 3/5**. The opening sets clear, personal stakes and Scene 3 has a good turn, but the mystery solves itself: the key evidence is left lying out and the culprit confesses as soon as he is asked, so the story is readable but loses its pull halfway through.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The case-breaking clue is left out in the open, where the victim works
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "Beside the logbook lay an unstamped manila folder, half-tucked beneath an iron scale weight. A blue seal showed beneath the flap. Tess reached it before he could turn."
- **Problem:** A man who has just poisoned the vintage and hidden his motive from his sister leaves the sale contract on the worktable in Clara's own press shed (Scene 2: "her small stainless-steel experimental tank"). Tess doesn't find it by investigating. She stumbles on it. The mystery's central reveal is luck plus the culprit being careless.
- **Reader needed:** Tess working out or digging for the motive through something she notices, reasons about or goes after herself, not a document handed to her by coincidence.

### 2. [major] The culprit confesses in full at the first push
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "\"The pressings had already turned sour on the skins. Full of active mother. I poured five gallons straight into the topping carboy on Monday night. All it took was fifty milliliters in each bung-hole to inoculate the lot.\""
- **Problem:** One scene earlier Bruno was sharp enough to cover a crime with a smaller admission of negligence. Here Tess says "So you killed it" and he explains the method, the dose and the timing without any resistance. The antagonist folds on contact, so there's no confrontation at the scene that should be the peak of the investigation.
- **Reader needed:** Bruno fighting back, denying, bargaining or threatening, so that Tess has to corner him before he gives anything up.

### 3. [minor] Tess's moral climax costs her nothing
- **Where:** Scene 5 (set up in Scene 4)
- **Quote:** "She could walk away. The money was in her pocket." / "leaving the Callow heirs standing together in the sour quiet of their ruined vintage."
- **Problem:** The story frames revealing the contract as a choice between survival and conscience. But she keeps the endorsed voucher and the letter, and walks out with "the draft slip secure against her chest." Breaking the deal carries no visible risk to her wages or her reputation, so the choice that is supposed to be brave is a free one.
- **Reader needed:** Some real price or danger attached to Tess breaking the deal, or an explicit reason why Bruno can't touch her now.

### 4. [minor] Bruno's scapegoat plan invites the scrutiny he needs to avoid
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 3
- **Quote:** "Spoilage ruled as worker negligence forfeits your back wages and kills any industry reference you ever ask me for." / "Section four of your agreement covers basic diligence"
- **Problem:** A man hiding deliberate sabotage picks a public negligence fight with the one person who handled the carboy, which pushes her to examine it. The escalation works dramatically, but it makes him look careless in a way that doesn't fit how carefully he planned everything else.
- **Reader needed:** A reason why Bruno needs someone to blame (for insurance, for the bank, for Clara) that makes accusing Tess worth the risk to him.

### 5. [minor] Clara reaches the right-but-wrong answer instantly
- **Where:** Scene 2
- **Quote:** "\"Bruno. He couldn't stomach the short harvest numbers on the ledger. He took my experimental run and emptied it straight into the carboys to stretch the cellar volume.\""
- **Problem:** From an empty tank and a loose nut, Clara jumps straight to a full theory about who did it and why. The investigation moves forward through her guess rather than through anything Tess works out, which thins out the mystery's middle.
- **Reader needed:** One piece of concrete evidence (the hose, a log entry, a time) that points to Bruno before anyone names him.

### 6. [minor] The ending re-explains what the reader already knows
- **Where:** Scene 5 (repeats Scene 4)
- **Quote:** "Bruno hadn't blundered. He had taken Clara's unpasteurized dessert pressings and deliberately dosed the topping carboy with live vinegar wash to force the collapse."
- **Problem:** The contract clause and the plot are described again just before the climax. Momentum stalls right where it should be rising.
- **Reader needed:** The final scene spending its time on Tess's decision, not a recap of the scheme.

## What works
- The stakes are clear and personal from the first scene: "The clause was real, and it meant walking out of the county blacklisted and penniless."
- Bruno's reaction in Scene 3 is a well-planted tell that pulls the reader in: "not the shock of an innocent man caught unawares, but the strange, sudden relief of someone handed a lifeline."
- The sensory clue in Scene 1 is specific to this world and this crime: "No sulfur flared. Instead, a soft, cloying sweetness drifted from the glass mouth."

## Out of lane
Clara "stormed out toward the road" in Scene 4 but is back in the cellar testing casks in Scene 5 without being shown returning.
