# Character Coherence — Second Pressing

**Score: 3/5** — Bruno's full confession, which the plot turns on, has no visible cause, and Clara forgets in Scene 5 what she worked out in Scenes 2–3. The three voices are distinct, though, and Tess's final choice is mostly earned.

## Findings

### 1. [major] Bruno confesses the moment he is asked
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "\"Put that down, Tess,\" Bruno said, his voice flat, exhausted." … "\"I poured five gallons straight into the topping carboy on Monday night. All it took was fifty milliliters in each bung-hole to inoculate the lot.\""
- **Problem:** A man who has spent three scenes deflecting blame, first onto Tess and then onto his own "operational gamble", gives the saboteur's method in full, with quantities, to the cellar hand he is scapegoating. Nobody presses him and there is no evidence he cannot explain away. The word "exhausted" is the only bridge between the two versions of him.
- **Reader needed:** A visible reason Bruno stops defending himself now, such as proof he cannot talk around, or a need to recruit Tess, or relief at finally telling someone.

### 2. [major] Clara forgets what she already worked out
- **Where:** Scene 5 (against Scenes 2 and 3)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "\"You know what unheated dessert must does without a sulphite dose! It turned every stave into an incubator for acetobacter.\"" Scene 5: "\"How could a cellar contamination spread this fast? It makes no sense. The pressings were clean when they came off the slats.\""
- **Problem:** Clara diagnosed the cause herself and accused Bruno of it to his face. Now she is puzzled by the spoilage as if she never did. Her bewilderment is there to set up the reveal, not because it follows from what she knows.
- **Reader needed:** Clara's Scene 5 confusion should grow out of her Scene 3 theory, for example doubt that one careless fill could do this much, rather than wipe it out.

### 3. [minor] Bruno pays Tess in front of Clara and lets her keep the contract
- **Where:** Scene 5
- **Quote:** "Instead, he pulled two slips of paper from his breast pocket and held them out toward Tess… \"Take it,\" Bruno said… \"Get your coat and clear out of the valley.\""
- **Problem:** Bruno has been calculating up to now. Here he makes the payoff in front of the one person it would make suspicious, and he never asks Tess to give back the contract she used to blackmail him. That careless streak serves the ending, not the character.
- **Reader needed:** Some reason Bruno does the handover in front of Clara, or tries and fails to get the contract back.

### 4. [minor] Bruno forgets the threat he has been using
- **Where:** Scene 4 (against Scenes 1 and 3)
- **Quote:** Scene 3: "\"Section four of your agreement covers basic diligence,\" Bruno said, tapping the clipboard with a thumb." Scene 4: "his mouth parting as if the thought of her wage had genuinely slipped his mind amid the collapse of the estate."
- **Problem:** The wage forfeit was Bruno's main weapon in both earlier scenes, used deliberately. Showing him as "genuinely" forgetting it softens him in a way his earlier behaviour contradicts.
- **Reader needed:** Either keep him calculating here, or show that the scapegoating was a reflex he no longer cares about.

### 5. [minor] Tess's change of heart is stated more than shown
- **Where:** Scene 5 (against Scene 4)
- **Quote:** Scene 4: "\"I don't care about your resort, Bruno. But you will not balance your ruin on my back.\"" Scene 5: "Keeping silent wasn't survival anymore; it was an endorsement."
- **Problem:** Seeing Clara testing the casks is a real trigger. But Tess has already threatened to tell Clara, so it was always a tactic for her, and the narrator names the moral shift rather than letting a beat show it. She also keeps the money from the deal she is breaking, and never weighs that.
- **Reader needed:** One beat of Tess weighing the money she has just been paid against Clara, so the reversal costs her something.

## What works
- Bruno's reaction when Clara accuses him is shown, not named, and it is characterful: "his shoulders dropped an inch… not the shock of an innocent man caught unawares, but the strange, sudden relief of someone handed a lifeline."
- The three voices are distinct. Bruno hides behind contract language ("Section four of your agreement covers basic diligence"). Clara is volatile ("you killed it with schoolboy negligence!"). Tess is clipped and bargains hard ("In ink. Signed by your hand.").
- Tess's self-interest is set up in Scene 1 and paid off consistently: "The clause was real, and it meant walking out of the county blacklisted and penniless."

## Out of lane
Clara "stormed out toward the road" in Scene 4 yet is already in the cellar at the start of Scene 5 (consistency).
