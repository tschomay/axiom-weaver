# Setup and Payoff — Second Pressing

**Score: 3/5** — Bruno's guilt is fairly planted and the wage threat pays off cleanly, but the mystery's main physical clue (a sweet, unsulphited carboy) does not support the sabotage the story reveals, and the locked-dispenser clue only pays off because Tess says it does.

## Findings

### 1. [major] The planted clue points to a different crime than the one revealed
- **Where:** Scene 1, Scene 2, Scene 4
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "a soft, cloying sweetness drifted from the glass mouth—the unpreserved fragrance of dessert pressings left exposed, sweet and flat". Scene 4: "The pressings had already turned sour on the skins. Full of active mother. I poured five gallons straight into the topping carboy"
- **Problem:** Both sniff tests (the carboy in Scene 1, the tank residue in Scene 2: "the sweet, heavy perfume of unprotected juice") set up the carboy as fresh, sweet must, which fits Clara's "reckless stretching" theory. The reveal then says it was deliberately soured vinegar-mother wash. The evidence the reader was given supports the false solution and undercuts the true one, so the reveal is not fairly planted.
- **Reader needed:** at least one sensory detail in the carboy or tank (a sour edge under the sweetness, a vinegar film) that only the sabotage theory explains.

### 2. [major] The locked-dispenser clue pays off by assertion
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 4
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "The potassium metabisulphite dispenser has been locked in my cabinet all week. The key has been on my belt loop since Monday." Scene 4: "You made sure I couldn't dose the barrels with metabisulphite even if I caught the turn in time."
- **Problem:** The story spends time on the padlock ("seated clean in the staple without a scuff"), but the lock and key are Clara's and Bruno never touches them. Tess's line turns this into part of Bruno's plan without showing anything he did, so it reads as the narrator linking the clues, not a real deduction.
- **Reader needed:** some action of Bruno's involving the dispenser or its key (moving it, taking the cellar's own supply) for Tess's claim to rest on.

### 3. [minor] Twenty gallons drained, five gallons used
- **Where:** Scene 2 and Scene 4
- **Quote:** Scene 2: "Twenty gallons of raw dessert pressings," Scene 4: "I poured five gallons straight into the topping carboy on Monday night."
- **Problem:** The empty tank is the key discovery of Scene 2, and the confession accounts for only a quarter of it. Where the other fifteen gallons went is a question the story raises and never answers.
- **Reader needed:** one line saying where the rest of the tank went, or a confession that matches the amount.

### 4. [minor] The decisive evidence is found lying on a table
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "Beside the logbook lay an unstamped manila folder, half-tucked beneath an iron scale weight. A blue seal showed beneath the flap. Tess reached it before he could turn."
- **Problem:** The mystery turns on this document, and Tess finds it by luck, not by following a clue. The motive also depends on a hail loss ("It took fifty percent of the fruit off the hill") that is first mentioned here. Bruno's relief in Scene 3 hints that there is more to find, but the thing itself turns up out of nowhere.
- **Reader needed:** an earlier mention of the bank debt, the hail or a buyer (a visitor, a phone call, Bruno's wish to sell) so the contract confirms a suspicion instead of creating one.

### 5. [minor] The sabotage is explained a second time
- **Where:** Scene 5 (after Scene 4)
- **Quote:** "Bruno hadn't blundered. He had taken Clara's unpasteurized dessert pressings and deliberately dosed the topping carboy with live vinegar wash to force the collapse."
- **Problem:** Tess got this whole explanation in Scene 4, and Bruno confirmed it. Repeating it in her thoughts just before Clara's discovery weakens Clara's realisation, which is the part that should land.
- **Reader needed:** only Tess's decision here, since the reader already knows the facts.

### 6. [nit] Scene 1's aim is to "prove it", but the story resolves it by bargaining
- **Where:** Scene 1 and Scene 4
- **Quote:** Scene 1: "Someone had substituted or doctored it, and she would have to prove it before Bruno closed the ledger on her name."
- **Problem:** Tess never proves anything to anyone. She clears her name with blackmail and a signed letter. This works, but the stated promise and the actual resolution don't quite line up.
- **Reader needed:** nothing more than an acknowledgement that the leverage replaces the proof.

## What works
- Bruno's relief when Clara accuses him plants the deeper secret fairly without giving it away: "not the shock of an innocent man caught unawares, but the strange, sudden relief of someone handed a lifeline."
- The wage threat set up in Scene 1 ("forfeits your back wages and kills any industry reference") pays off in the extortion and the signed discharge, and Tess's choice to reveal the contract anyway gives it a second turn.
- Clara's reckless-stretching theory is a good false solution that the story sets up fully before overturning it: "her brother had grown desperate, cut a reckless corner to puff up their low gallonage".

## Out of lane
The yield shortfall is "thirty percent" in Scene 3 and "fifty percent" in Scene 4 (consistency).
