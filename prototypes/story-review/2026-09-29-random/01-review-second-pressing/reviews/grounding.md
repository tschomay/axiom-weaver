# Grounding and Detail: Second Pressing

**Score: 4/5**: Every scene is well placed physically. The gaps are in the reveal: its backstory (the hail) and its mechanics (the carboy dose, the locked dispenser) are stated as known or asserted rather than set up. A reader can recover them, but it takes a pause.

## Findings

### 1. [minor] The sabotage method is stated but hard to follow
- **Where:** Scene 4 (set against Scene 1)
- **Quote:** "I poured five gallons straight into the topping carboy on Monday night. All it took was fifty milliliters in each bung-hole to inoculate the lot." (Scene 4); "You performed the topping-up on Tuesday afternoon." (Scene 1)
- **Problem:** This confession is how the mystery is solved, but it doesn't say whether Bruno dosed each barrel himself or whether Tess's Tuesday topping-up carried the dose in. "Each bung-hole" reads like a separate act by Bruno.
- **Reader needed:** One clear line linking the Monday pour to Tess's Tuesday topping-up as the way the dose reached every barrel.

### 2. [minor] "You made sure I couldn't dose" has no groundwork
- **Where:** Scene 4 (set against Scene 2)
- **Quote:** "And you left the sulphur dispenser locked inside her cabinet ... You made sure I couldn't dose the barrels with metabisulphite even if I caught the turn in time." (Scene 4); "The key has been on my belt loop since Monday." (Scene 2)
- **Problem:** In Scene 2 the lock is Clara's own routine and the key is on her belt. Nothing shows Bruno arranging it, and Tess never tried to dose the barrels, so the deduction reads as a claim.
- **Reader needed:** Some sign that Bruno had a hand in the dispenser being locked away, or that Tess needed it and couldn't get it.

### 3. [minor] "The hail" appears as if the reader already knows about it
- **Where:** Scene 4
- **Quote:** "\"The hail,\" Tess said, reading the acreage addendum. \"In November. It didn't just clip the tops.\""
- **Problem:** No hail has been mentioned before this line. Tess uses the definite article as if it's shared knowledge, and this is the backstory that drives Bruno's motive. The reader only finds out what it was from Bruno's next line.
- **Reader needed:** An earlier mention of the hail and the short harvest, even in passing, so that Tess's line lands as recognition.

### 4. [minor] Clara leaves and comes back offstage
- **Where:** Scene 4 and Scene 5
- **Quote:** "When Clara stormed out toward the road, Bruno did not follow her." (Scene 4); "Clara was already there among the double row of casks" (Scene 5)
- **Problem:** Scene 3 ends with Clara laughing in the cellar. Scene 4 then reports after the fact that she has left, and Scene 5 has her back in the cellar with no mention of her return. Tess's threat, "before she reaches the highway", depends on her being gone.
- **Reader needed:** A beat showing Clara leaving at the end of Scene 3, and a hint of why she came back.

### 5. [minor] Exposition put into Bruno's mouth
- **Where:** Scene 1
- **Quote:** "Topping-up—replacing the wine lost to evaporation so air couldn't pocket in the headspace—is work a first-year cellar hand manages"
- **Problem:** Bruno explains the term to Tess, a cellar hand who already knows it. The gloss is for the reader, and it breaks up an angry speech.
- **Reader needed:** The definition carried by narration or Tess's thoughts rather than by Bruno.

### 6. [nit] Where the payment came from
- **Where:** Scene 5
- **Quote:** "a cashier's voucher for twelve hundred dollars, endorsed on the back, and a typed recommendation on estate stationery"
- **Problem:** Scene 4 ends with Bruno reaching for a fountain pen in the press shed. A cashier's voucher and a typed letter moments later imply an errand the story doesn't show.
- **Reader needed:** A handwritten cheque and letter, or a short time jump.

### 7. [nit] "Her" before anyone is named
- **Where:** Scene 1
- **Quote:** "Bruno Callow worked down the first rack without waiting for her."
- **Problem:** The pronoun comes before Tess is named, four lines later.
- **Reader needed:** Tess's name in or near the first line of the scene.

## What works
- Each scene opens with a strong sense of place: "The corrugated roof of the upper press shed shuddered under the mountain draft, its galvanized seams whistling against the timber rafters." (Scene 2)
- Physical clues are shown before anyone reasons from them: "The nut was off. It lay resting on the rim of an empty bucket." (Scene 2)
- Tess's stakes are grounded in something concrete: "Spoilage ruled as worker negligence forfeits your back wages and kills any industry reference you ever ask me for." (Scene 1)

## Out of lane
The yield shortfall is "thirty percent" in Scene 3 but "fifty percent" in Scene 4, and the carboy smells sweet in Scene 1 but was "already turned sour" in Scene 4. Both are for the consistency reviewer.
