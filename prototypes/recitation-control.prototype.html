Prototype — throwaway — answers issue #17 only

Recitation control on the famous-prose fixture

Question this demo answers: when the Story Package is a story the model has memorised (A Christmas Carol), does the engine perform it or recite it — and how would the compiler even tell? Built against scene_01_counting_house — the single highest recitation-risk scene in either fixture, since its own required_beats already quote three of the book's most famous lines.

A note on method, up front: this session has no Gemini API credentials and no network path to Google's endpoints, so no real model call was made. What follows is (a) an architectural finding that holds regardless of which model renders the scene, established by reading the fixture's own JSON rather than by running anything, and (b) one short hand-written performance of the scene, used the same way issue #16's prototype used a hand-written scene — as a concrete artifact to react to, not as a substitute for a real measurement. The real baseline (does gemini-3.7-flash actually trip finishReason: RECITATION on this fixture) is called out below as an execution step for whoever builds the read-time run loop, not something this planning session can produce a number for.

1. The finding that decided this ticket: the Scene Card already quotes Dickens

scene_01_counting_house.required_beats, verbatim from fixtures/a-christmas-carol/package.json:

#required_beats entry
1Fred visits, wishes his uncle a merry Christmas, and invites him to dinner; Scrooge answers 'Bah! Humbug!' and refusesauthor-quoted
2two portly gentlemen ask Scrooge for a subscription for the poor; he asks whether there are no prisons, no workhousesauthor-paraphrased, close
3told that many would rather die than go there, Scrooge answers that they had better do it, and decrease the surplus populationauthor-quoted
4Scrooge grudgingly allows Bob Cratchit the whole of Christmas Day off, calling it a poor excuse for picking a man's pocket every twenty-fifth of Decemberauthor-quoted
5Scrooge walks home alone through the fog to his gloomy chambersoriginal paraphrase
Three of five required beats already embed Dickens' own wording. The fixture author (issue #18) chose to quote rather than paraphrase the book's three most recognisable lines. Any writer model instructed to satisfy these beats will reproduce those phrases on purpose — that is not the writer recalling training data, it is the writer following its brief. This single fact reshapes the rest of the ticket: the question is never "did any Dickens wording appear in the output," it's "did wording appear that the Scene Card never asked for."

2. Why a client-side text-similarity detector can't be built here

The obvious instinct — diff the generated prose against the source novel, n-gram overlap or longest-common-substring, flag anything over a threshold — runs into an architectural wall specific to this project, not a tooling limitation:

The compiler's data model never holds canonical source prose. The Story Package schema (docs/schema/story-package.md, ADR 0001) is Scene Cards and World Model rows — Fabula and Syuzhet, author-owned. The engine's read-time inputs (ADR 0008's zoom-level assembler, ADR 0012's volatile tail) are built entirely from that schema plus the engine's own Scene/Chapter/Part digests. There is no field anywhere in the pipeline that carries "here is Dickens' actual paragraph for this scene, diff your output against it." Building one would mean embedding a full copy of the source novel into every famous-prose Story Package for the sole purpose of self-plagiarism-checking — a new schema surface this ticket has no mandate to add, and one that would only ever fire for the minority of Story Packages built from a memorised work in the first place.
Diffing against the Scene Card's own text detects the wrong thing. The only text the compiler does hold at read time is the Scene Card itself — and per finding 1, that text already contains the phrases most likely to trip a naive detector, by design. A local LCS check against required_beats would just confirm the writer did its job, every time, on every scene in every fixture — pure noise, not a recitation signal.
The one detector that actually exists lives on the far side of the API call, not in the compiler. Gemini's own training-corpus matcher reports finishReason: RECITATION (confirmed in the discovery document by issue #5's research, docs/research/gemini-capabilities.md §1) — Google's classifier compares against everything it was trained on, which is exactly the comparison this ticket wants and exactly the comparison the compiler has no way to run locally. Issue #16's writer prompt contract (ADR 0012 item 6) already gave this ticket a retry-on-RECITATION hook to inherit rather than re-derive.

3. Worked example — one hand-written performance

Original prose in the fixture's Voice Card register (Dickensian comic-gothic, intrusive narrator, per fixtures/a-christmas-carol/package.json), written to satisfy all five required beats. The three author-mandated quotations are highlighted; everything else is this prototype's own paraphrase, not Dickens' actual sentences.

Scrooge's office kept a chill of its own, colder than the fog outside could account for, and he liked it that way. So when his nephew Fred came in stamping the cold off his boots and glowing with more Christmas than any one room could reasonably hold, Scrooge received him the way he received most things that cost him nothing and asked for something back. "A merry Christmas, uncle! Dine with us tomorrow." Scrooge's answer arrived before the invitation had properly landed: "Bah! Humbug!" Fred took the refusal the way he took everything from his uncle, cheerfully, and let himself back out into the weather no worse for it. He had scarcely gone before two portly gentlemen arrived instead, subscription-book in hand, wanting a little something for the Poor at this festive season. Scrooge wanted to know whether the prisons were still standing. They were. The workhouses too? Also standing, though the gentlemen allowed that many of the poor would rather die than go into one. "If they would rather die," Scrooge said, entirely satisfied with the logic of it, "they had better do it, and decrease the surplus population." The gentlemen withdrew with their book unsigned. By the time the light failed, only Bob Cratchit remained, hunched at his desk in a coat too thin for the cold he wasn't allowed a fire against, and screwing up the courage to ask for the whole of Christmas Day off. Scrooge granted it the way he granted everything — as an injury he'd remember. "A poor excuse for picking a man's pocket every twenty-fifth of December," he said, buttoning his coat to the chin, "but I suppose you must have it." Then he went out alone into the fog, which had no trouble finding his gloomy chambers, since it went everywhere else that evening too.

The three mandated phrases sit inside otherwise-original sentences without reading as seams — a human editor skimming this would flag them as quotation, not as the surrounding prose secretly being Dickens'. That's the qualitative version of the diagnosis this ticket asks for: whole-passage reproduction did not happen here; isolated-line reproduction did, exactly where the Scene Card asked for it. This is one hand-written sample, not a statistical claim about gemini-3.7-flash's actual behaviour — see the baseline gap in the box below.

4. Decision: detection, diagnosis, mitigation

QuestionDecision
DetectionfinishReason: RECITATION from the Gemini API is the only detector. No client-side n-gram/LCS text-similarity check is built (§2) — there is nothing in the compiler's data model to diff against.
Authored quotation vs. unbidden recallNot distinguished, because it doesn't need to be. A phrase quoted in required_beats/dialogue is the writer satisfying its brief, identical in kind to any other mandated line — not a defect. This ticket's concern is scoped to reproduction the Scene Card never asked for.
BaselineNot measurable in this session — no API access. Becomes an execution step for issue #19's first real run of the A Christmas Carol fixture: log finishReason per scene, watch specifically for RECITATION on this fixture vs. its near-absence on Cinderella (the control).
MitigationReuse ADR 0012 item 6 as-is: one retry with a paraphrase instruction on RECITATION; a second occurrence falls through to the existing scene_generation_failed path, logged via the already-reserved recitation_flagged diagnostic (ADR 0012 item 7). No new mechanism. No preemptive Voice Card detuning — that trade against prose quality is only worth making if the baseline (above) shows a persistent problem.
Public-domain reality checkLegally moot — both fixtures are public domain. The line this ticket draws: an engine-initiated, multi-sentence verbatim reproduction the Scene Card never requested would make the POC dishonest about its own capability; an author-instructed short quotation, or the model's compliant rendering of one, is not that.
Answer, in one line

The engine performs unless Google's own classifier says otherwise; the compiler has no independent way to check, and the one case that looked like a problem on paper (the Scene Card's own famous quotations) turns out not to be recitation at all. Full reasoning and consequences in ADR 0013.
